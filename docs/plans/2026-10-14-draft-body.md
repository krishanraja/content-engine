Going by the reviews, people keep telling Meta's new agent to go and do things, and it keeps doing them. That is why Muse went to the top of Apple's App Store in September, above ChatGPT, and it has very little to do with clever answers.

Meta launched Muse on 8 September. By 23 September it had been downloaded more than 2.5 million times, according to Sensor Tower figures reported by CNN. On Apple's US store it rates 4.9 out of 5. Launch-week stars flatter every app, so I don't lean on them. I read the reviews instead.

One reviewer says Muse took on a clothing company that had billed $6,000 for an order of around $1,500, tracked the orders, wrote the escalation letter, then found and closed subscriptions the household had forgotten about. Another uses it for job applications "from start to finish" and found cheaper health insurance with it. A self-described ChatGPT loyalist deleted ChatGPT from their phone and MacBook after two weeks. Each of these is one person, and nobody audited them. The pattern is still unusual. People describe jobs that got finished, where a year ago they described answers that sounded good.

The doubters are just as specific. The sharpest review on Apple's store is titled "Permanent Privacy Risk". Its writer connected their email, watched Muse fail to clean up the inbox, and then started worrying that an agent from Meta could read every one-time password email in it. Bank of America's analysts put it more politely, writing that "privacy and trust remain key considerations for broader adoption".

So the public verdict is split, and in an interesting way. The fans and the sceptics agree that it works. They disagree about the price.

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY UNDER THE BONNET

Meta calls Muse a personal AI agent, meaning an AI that does tasks for you rather than just chatting. Each one runs on its own computer in Meta's cloud, which Meta calls Muse Secure VM. It can open a browser, fill out forms and negotiate on your behalf. It keeps working after you close the app, and it checks with you before it sends an email or makes a purchase. You talk to it in its own app or straight from WhatsApp. The brain doing the thinking is Muse Spark, Meta's own model.

Now the honest part. Very little of that is unique. CNN's reviewer found that ChatGPT and Gemini "can handle similar tasks and sometimes provided more detailed responses", and Muse suggested two date-night spots that had closed years ago. On Lenny's How I AI, Claire watched it fail to buy a pair of New Balance 9060s in the right colour. Browser shopping is still flaky for every agent, Muse included.

So the brain isn't the moat. I'd point at two other things.

The first is craft. The How I AI review noticed that Muse never showed a terminal or asked for permission at an awkward moment. Every step it takes is written into an activity feed you can read. That is twenty years of building apps for people who never read a manual, and it shows. Meta also says Muse is "free for most of what people need", which is a big part of why the downloads came so fast.

The second matters more, and I think it is the one thing ChatGPT can't copy. Meta's own launch example has it turning a recipe reel you saved on Instagram into a grocery list, then remembering your friends' dietary needs before it sends the dinner invites. On 29 September Meta added Muse for Small Business, which links to Meta ad accounts and professional Instagram and Facebook profiles, alongside tools like Slack and Canva. Its agent can act on what it finds in those apps. An outside agent would have to ask for that access piece by piece. My read is that this edge comes from proximity, and intelligence has little to do with it.

## THE BARGAIN PEOPLE ARE WEIGHING

Every account you connect makes Muse more useful, and it makes the bet bigger. The two rise together, and that is the trade.

Meta says Muse doesn't share your conversations or the data on its computer with Meta's ad systems. It also says people "can opt out of their interactions being used to train Meta's AI models". Meta's help page adds that this setting "is on when you first use Muse". I would bet most people never go looking for it.

Then there was the Mac flaw. On 21 September the security researcher Patrick Wardle showed that malware already running on a Mac could redirect Muse's dictation and abuse whatever access the user had given the app. The next day Wardle said Meta had hot-fixed it. The attack needed the ability to run code on the Mac already, so a stranger on the internet couldn't use it alone. Wardle's wider point still lands. Apps like this, he told The Register, "basically could do anything on your computer" once you set them up to be useful.

Meta's answer is a stronger lock. It has promised Muse Confidential VM "later this year", with everything encrypted by a key only you hold, "so not even Meta can access it". Jules Polonetsky, a privacy specialist who tested Muse with accounts kept for testing, called handing over all your accounts "a leap of faith". He wants that protection for all users, "not just the paid tier".

## WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

If you use it, give Muse the jobs where a mistake is small and easy to undo, like chasing a refund or sorting a calendar. Keep its approvals on for anything that sends money or messages, or deletes things. Turn off AI training in the settings, and connect only what the job in front of you needs. That is my read, and the bet stays yours. Meta's own app store listing says Muse "may be inaccurate or take unexpected actions".

## WHAT IT MEANS IF YOU'RE BUILDING A BUSINESS

Meta says 200 million small businesses are on Facebook. If you sell to them, an agent that can read their ad accounts and Instagram numbers just moved into their phones, free. That changes who they ask for advice.

If you're building an agent yourself, the CNN and How I AI tests should worry you more than any benchmark. In those tests a smarter brain didn't come out on top. My read is that Muse wins on context it already holds and a price Meta calls "free for most of what people need". So ask what your product knows about its users that nobody else can see. If the honest answer is nothing, a bigger company can copy you with one release.

## HOW THE OLD WAY IS CHANGING

For twenty years software waited for you. You searched, it answered, you did the work. Muse keeps working after you close the app and comes back when something changes. I think the contest is shifting from who has the best answer to who already holds the most of your life, and on your social life that is Meta.

## MY PREDICTION

Meta has promised Confidential VM "later this year". My call is narrower.

By 31 March 2027, Meta will offer Muse Confidential VM to free users in the US, not just paying ones.

How sure we are: 35%.

That number is low on purpose. A lock that even Meta can't open also stops Meta learning from what is inside, and free products tend to pay their way in data. You can check it on Meta's own Muse pages that day.

The smartest agent was never going to win this round. The one already sitting inside your life was, and right now that one is Meta's.
