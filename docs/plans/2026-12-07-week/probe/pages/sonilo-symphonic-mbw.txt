$11M-backed AI startup Sonilo, founded by TikTok veterans, inks licensing deal with Symphonic to train its model on opted-in indie recordings

 November 18, 2026
By Mandy Dalugdug

Sonilo, an AI audio company founded by former TikTok staff, has signed a licensing deal with independent distributor Symphonic.

Continue to article...
The agreement allows Sonilo to train its AI music model on recordings from Symphonic’s independent artists and labels – but only those that choose to opt in.
The companies, which announced the deal on Wednesday (November 18), say it lets independent rights holders get paid for helping shape how AI music is made.
Earlier in October, Sonilo announced a USD $11 million funding round led by B Capital, with participation from Redpoint.
Sonilo said at the time that it was finalizing master recording and publishing deals with “major rights organizations,” with agreements expected to close “in the coming weeks.” It repeated the claim when announcing the Musical AI partnership. No such agreement has yet been announced.

Sonilo says it will provide “attribution reporting to support transparency and compensation for the rights holders behind the music.”
Financial terms were not disclosed, and the companies did not say how many Symphonic artists or labels have opted in.
“The future of video-to-music is soundtracks made for every story, and that future has to be licensed from the ground up,” said Keli Li, Co-Founder and COO of Sonilo. “Licensing isn’t a feature we’re adding later. It’s how we built Sonilo.
“The future of video-to-music is soundtracks made for every story, and that future has to be licensed from the ground up.”
Keli Li, Sonilo
“Independent artists make some of the most distinctive music in the world, and they deserve a seat in the AI music value chain.”
“AI is only a real opportunity for artists when it’s built on consent, transparency and fair payment,” said Jorge Brea, CEO of Symphonic. “This agreement gives our independent artists and labels a direct, paid way to take part in AI music, on terms that respect their rights.”

The Symphonic deal follows Sonilo’s partnership with attribution company Musical AI, announced on October 27.
Under that arrangement, Musical AI’s technology will examine each piece of audio that Sonilo generates and measure the contribution of the licensed recordings and compositions behind it.
Sonilo says the resulting data will let its licensing partners account for usage and support payments to the songwriters, artists, and rights holders involved.
“AI is only a real opportunity for artists when it’s built on consent, transparency and fair payment.”
Jorge Brea, Symphonic
The San Francisco-based company’s Co-Founder and CEO, Shawn Song, was previously a multimodal AI tech lead at TikTok, according to Sonilo.
Li, the company says, “built and led global music at TikTok.”
Sonilo’s models generate music and sound effects from video footage, text, or both.
The company names Shutterstock as an early licensing partner.
Sonilo says it trains its models only on licensed catalogs, and that rights holders and artists receive licensing fees and a share of revenue.
The company pitches its platform at short-form and short-drama creators, developer platforms, and enterprise production teams.
Its distribution partners include fal, TapNow, and ComfyUI.

Symphonic was founded in 2006 by Brea and is headquartered in Tampa, Florida.
The Sonilo agreement adds to the distributor’s existing AI licensing partnerships.
In July, Symphonic partnered with Sureel AI, the attribution company acquired by Warner Music Group in June, to let its artists opt in to licensed AI datasets.
“AI represents real opportunity for artists, but only if we use it correctly,” Brea said at the time. “By giving our artists the chance to opt-in to Sureel’s fully licensed AI datasets, we are pushing back against unpaid training and opening new revenue opportunities for those who wish to participate.”
In February, Symphonic signed a deal with sync platform SourceAudio that lets its artists and labels opt in to SourceAudio’s AI music dataset licensing marketplace.
Symphonic’s own ties to Musical AI, now Sonilo’s attribution partner, go back to 2024, when the distributor struck a deal that let opted-in clients join a licensed dataset used for AI training.
At the time, Musical AI Co-Founder and COO Matt Adell called Symphonic the company’s “first major rights holder partner.”
Symphonic’s website names the Sureel and Musical AI partnerships, alongside an opt-in AI Dataset Licensing program for its clients.
The distributor also offers MusicShield, a tool from ArtyShield designed to make recordings harder for generative AI models to learn from without permission.
In a September op-ed for MBW, Brea argued that the rising volume of AI-generated music on streaming services was a smaller risk than another effect of the technology.
AI has lowered the cost of starting a distribution business, the Symphonic CEO wrote, letting new entrants look established before they have built systems to verify rights, process royalties, or detect fraud.
“AI will not break music, but abandoning standards might,” wrote Brea.Music Business Worldwide
