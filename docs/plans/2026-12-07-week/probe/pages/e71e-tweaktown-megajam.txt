Epic Games' 2026 AI MegaJam offers $65,000 in prizes, but the AI requirements are confusing
The 2026 Epic AI MegaJam lets teams of five create a playable Unreal Engine or UEFN game from November 12-15 for cash and continuation grants.

What you need to know
The 2026 Epic AI MegaJam runs November 12-15, challenging teams of up to five to build a playable Unreal Engine or UEFN game. AI tools are optional. Prizes total about $65,000, with continuation grants of up to $50,000; the theme will be announced November 11, and registration is open.

Epic Games is running another game jam, and this one leans hard into the current AI hype. The 2026 Epic AI MegaJam starts November 12 and gives participants just four days to build a playable game in either Unreal Engine or Unreal Editor for Fortnite (UEFN). Teams can have up to five members plus unlimited AI agents.

The official pitch is pure "vibe coding." Epic is motivating people to utilize whatever AI tools they prefer for asset generation, coding agents, or anything else that speeds up the process.

The theme will be revealed during a livestream on November 11.

Epic Games' 2026 AI MegaJam offers around $65,000 in cash prizes, and Epic has clarified that using AI tools is optional.VIEW GALLERY - 3 IMAGES
Cash prizes total around $65,000 across the main and modifier categories. Best Overall Unreal Engine, Best Overall UEFN, and Best Student Project each pay $7,500 for first, $5000 for second, and $2,500 for third. Additional awards cover best team, solo developers, visuals, mobile games, and even the best dev log. Standout projects can also pick Development Continuation Grants of up to $50,000 so the work doesn't die the moment the Jam ends.
Epic is also pushing its own Model Context Protocol tools way ahead of the Jam, with dedicated setup guides and livestreams for both Unreal Engine and UEFN so teams can get coding agents talking directly to the editor.
3
The Epic Games Launcher library represents Epic’s gaming platform; its 2026 AI MegaJam asks teams to build games in Unreal Engine or UEFN, with AI use optional.These moves make sense when you consider the company's recent vibe-coded Epic Games Store launcher and AI-first Disney shooter, suggesting Epic wants developers to treat AI as an important part of the workflow. Funnily enough, Epic initially worded the rules to make AI use sound mandatory, then quickly clarified that it is completely optional. You can utilize as much or as little AI as you want.
