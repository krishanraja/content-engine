Key highlights 
 • Sonilo and Symphonic announced a licensing deal on November 18, 2026 that lets Sonilo train its video-to-music AI on recordings Symphonic distributes. 
 • Only songs whose artists or labels opt in go into training, through Symphonic's optional AI Dataset Licensing program. 
 • Sonilo says it will give rights holders attribution reporting, licensing fees and a share of revenue. 
 • Neither company disclosed the fee, the revenue split or how many Symphonic artists and labels have opted in. 

Sonilo, the AI startup that writes soundtracks to match your video, can now train its model on music from Symphonic’s independent artists and labels. There’s one condition: a song only goes in if its owner opts in.

The two companies announced the licensing deal on November 18, 2026, Music Business Worldwide reported. Neither side shared the fee, the revenue split or how many Symphonic artists have opted in so far.

Former TikTok staff founded Sonilo, and it raised $11 million in October in a round led by B Capital. Symphonic is the Tampa distributor Jorge Brea started in 2006, and it’s one of the picks in my AI music distribution roundup.

So what does opting in actually mean for a song you release through Symphonic?

How Symphonic’s opt-in AI training works in the Sonilo deal

Training is how an AI music model learns. It listens to a lot of recordings and picks up how music behaves, so it can write something new later. Opt-in means Sonilo only gets your recording for that if you said yes first, so nobody’s catalogue goes in by default.

On Symphonic’s side, that yes runs through its optional AI Dataset Licensing program, where Symphonic says enrollment is “entirely opt-in.”

Sonilo says it will send back “attribution reporting to support transparency and compensation for the rights holders behind the music.” That’s a usage report: which licensed songs shaped the music Sonilo generated. Sonilo signed Musical AI in October to measure exactly that on its soundtracks.
 Design: The AI Musicpreneur, from Music Business Worldwide's November 18, 2026 report and Symphonic's AI page 

Sonilo also says rights holders receive licensing fees and a share of revenue. That’s the payment step, and it’s the one step with no numbers attached yet.

 “ AI is only a real opportunity for artists when it's built on consent, transparency and fair payment. 
— Jorge Brea, CEO of Symphonic, November 18, 2026 

What Sonilo’s video-to-music AI makes with the songs it trains on

Sonilo’s models write music and sound effects from video footage, a text prompt, or both. You hand it a clip, and it scores the clip.

Sonilo pitches it at short-form and short-drama creators, developer platforms and production teams, and developers can already reach it through fal, TapNow and ComfyUI. Its consumer app is due in the fourth quarter of 2026.

So an opted-in Symphonic song becomes training material: music Sonilo learns from before it scores somebody else’s clip.

Keli Li, Sonilo’s co-founder and COO, said licensing “isn’t a feature we’re adding later. It’s how we built Sonilo.” Its first named licensing partner was Shutterstock, whose music catalog trained Sonilo v1.0 in May 2026.

What the Sonilo and Symphonic deal leaves out

3 things a Symphonic artist would want to know before opting in aren’t public yet:

How much Sonilo pays, per recording or per catalogue.

How the revenue share splits between Sonilo, Symphonic and the artist.

How many Symphonic artists and labels have opted in.

There’s also an open promise. When Sonilo announced its funding, it said master recording and publishing deals with “major rights organizations” would close “in the coming weeks.” It repeated that in October, and no such deal has been announced as of November 19. So far, the named music sources are Shutterstock and now Symphonic’s opted-in roster.

For Symphonic, Sonilo joins a list of opt-in AI partners: Musical AI in August 2024, SourceAudio in February 2026 and Warner-owned Sureel AI in July. Brea has also argued that AI’s bigger risk sits with distributors that launch without fraud controls.

Symphonic has the opposite option too. Its MusicShield tool alters your audio so AI models learn less from it.

So a Symphonic artist can now license a song for AI training or shield it from training. Until Sonilo or Symphonic publishes a rate, the first option comes without a price tag. 
Frequently asked questions 

Which AI training partners did Symphonic sign before Sonilo? 
Musical AI in August 2024, SourceAudio in February 2026 and Sureel AI in July 2026. All 3 are opt-in, so a Symphonic artist's music is only licensed for AI training if they enroll. The Sonilo deal from November 18, 2026 joins them. 

Who announced the Sonilo and Symphonic licensing deal? 
Keli Li, Sonilo's co-founder and COO, and Jorge Brea, who founded Symphonic in 2006 and runs it as CEO. Both gave statements when the deal was announced on November 18, 2026. 

Does the Sonilo and Symphonic deal include major-label music? 
No. It covers recordings from Symphonic's independent artists and labels who opt in. Sonilo has said since October 2026 that it is finalizing deals with major rights organizations, but none had been announced as of November 19, 2026.
