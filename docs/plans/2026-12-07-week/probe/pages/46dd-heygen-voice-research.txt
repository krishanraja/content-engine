HeyGen Voice: Scene-Aware Voice Cloning for Talking Videos
An open beach. A moving car. A busy factory floor. HeyGen Voice brings your voice into the picture, shaping the delivery and sound to fit the scene.
By HeyGen ResearchOct 14, 20269 min read
Read the report

A convincing talking video needs a voice that belongs in the picture. The warmth of a close microphone, the openness of a street, the echo of a large room: these details help a person feel present.
HeyGen Voice turns a recording of your voice, a script, and a target image into speech for the person and place on screen. An everyday recording becomes the starting point for a studio narration, an outdoor conversation, or a presentation in a large room—with delivery and acoustics shaped for each scene.

Model design
Separate the voice from the recording.

A voice recording captures more than a person’s voice. It also captures a microphone, a room, background sound, and one particular delivery. HeyGen Voice treats these as distinct sources of information: the reference establishes who is speaking, while the image and descriptions of the scene guide how the speech should sound.

From an image to a performance
A language model turns the target image and requested delivery style into two descriptions. A speaker caption describes tone, pace, emphasis, and expressiveness. A background caption describes the space and its sound: for example, a close microphone in a quiet studio or a voice surrounded by traffic. These captions give the speech model explicit guidance alongside the visual input.

A multimodal autoregressive transformer combines the script, voice reference, image, and scene descriptions to generate speech. A flow-matching decoder and waveform decoder turn its output into 44.1 kHz audio.

One voice and one room, throughout
For longer scripts, HeyGen Voice reuses an audio anchor to carry the same voice, delivery, and acoustics across segments. Each segment also receives the end of the preceding speech as context, helping the narration flow naturally.

Training
Learning what to keep—and what to change.
Training progresses from reading in a chosen voice to adapting that voice across scenes, then refining clarity and expression.

Learn speech and voice identity.
Supervised pretraining on recordings and scripts establishes multilingual speech generation and voice cloning.

Vary the recording conditions.
Simulated changes to noise, reverberation, microphones, and timing teach the model to preserve the speaker even when the reference sounds different from the target.

Learn from real changes of scene.
Pairs of the same speaker in different videos teach changes in delivery and setting that audio augmentation alone cannot capture.

Reward clear, recognizable speech.
Reinforcement learning uses speech-recognition and speaker-similarity scores to reward clear speech and a recognizable voice.

Refine expressive delivery.
Preference training refines phrasing, emphasis, and rhythm through an optional expressiveness adapter, using automated feedback followed by human preferences.

Ethics and Safety
HeyGen Voice is designed for authorized voice creation, with consent and control at its foundation. HeyGen’s platform requires verified consent from the person whose voice is cloned and allows them to request removal of their voice. Uploaded and generated content is subject to automated moderation and human review. The serving system uses AI audio watermarking to support detection of generated speech.
