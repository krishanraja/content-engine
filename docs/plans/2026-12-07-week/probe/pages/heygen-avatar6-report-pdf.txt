1 Introduction
Audio-driven avatar generation synthesises video of a specific person delivering new speech. It has become
a standard component of video production for training material, multilingual communication, outreach and
creator content, and the field has advanced quickly. Early methods re-rendered only the mouth region of
an existing video to match new audio [4], or animated a single portrait through intermediate 3D or latent
motion representations [5, 6]. Diffusion models [11], built on latent image and video backbones [12, 13], then
made it possible to generate the face directly from audio, with far richer expression and head motion [7, 8,
9, 10, 14], and were soon extended to the half body [15] and to longer, higher-resolution output [16]. Built
on large video diffusion transformers [17, 18, 19], many of them trained with flow matching [20], recent work
extends generation to the full body and the surrounding scene [21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 28], from one speaker to
several [26, 27], and to long or unbounded durations [29, 30, 31]. In parallel, general video generation models
such as Sora [32], Kling [46], Veo 3 [33], Seedance [34, 35, 36] and Wan [19] now produce convincing talking
people from an image and a prompt, increasingly with synchronised speech generated jointly with the video.
HeyGen has developed audio-driven avatar models over several generations, from Avatar III and Avatar IV
to Avatar V [1], which introduced the video-reference formulation, together with HeyGen Voice for speech
in the speaker’s own voice [2] and composable control of avatar behaviour [3]. These models are deployed in
HeyGen’s products for businesses and individual creators.
As visual quality has matured, the central requirement has shifted from realism to identity fidelity. Most of
the methods above condition on a single image of the person: they reproduce how the person looks in one
frame, but must invent how the person speaks, gestures and moves. An avatar today usually stands in for a
specific person, and its output is useful only if people who know that person would accept it as theirs. This
requires reproducing not only appearance but also manner of speaking and habits of motion, in the person’s
own setting and over videos of arbitrary length. Avatar 6 is built around this objective.
Avatar 6 is an audio-driven avatar video generation model that produces video of a specific person that not
only looks like them but also speaks and moves like them, at 1080p and 2K and with no limit on duration.
It extends the video-reference formulation of Avatar V into a unified framework: the person is specified by a
short identity recording or by a single photograph, and generation is conditioned on speech and an optional
written motion prompt. The system comprises the generation model, which includes a super-resolution
stage and outputs 1080p and 2K natively, a motion planner for speech- and prompt-driven performance, and
HeyGen Voice; all components are developed in-house. Compared with Avatar IV and Avatar V, Avatar 6
improves likeness, motion, resolution and long-duration stability; it is consistently preferred in large-scale
online preference tests and comes close to real footage in a Turing test with external raters (Section 2).

The same framework extends beyond a single talking head to multi-speaker dialogue, fine-grained motion
control from written prompts, and word-level editing of real recordings. Avatar 6 is offered in two configu-
rations: Avatar 6 Quality and Avatar 6 Turbo, a lower-latency variant served by our in-house accelerated
inference stack.
Its key capabilities are as follows.
• Identity fidelity from a video reference. From a short identity recording, Avatar 6 learns a person’s
appearance, including skin texture, hair and clothing, together with their manner of speaking and the
motion of their head, face, hands and body, rather than imposing a single learned presenter style. In a
Turing test, Avatar 6 clips shown alone are judged real in 65.1% of answers, against 78.9% for genuine
recordings, and next to the real recording it is 84.4% indistinguishable from real footage, against 44.4%
for Avatar V (Section 2.5).
• Generalisation across scenes and styles. Avatar 6 is not restricted to a presenter in front of a plain
backdrop. It generalises to studios, oﬀices, stages and concerts, and from photorealistic footage to stylised
characters, preserving the scene, lighting and framing of the reference.
• High resolution and unbounded duration. Avatar 6 natively generates 1080p and 2K video. Identity,
framing and sharpness remain stable over videos of any length: a 30-minute sequence is generated end to
end in a single pass, without identity drift or late-stage loss of sharpness.
• Diverse applications. The same model supports (1) multi-speaker dialogue within one frame; (2) fine-
grained motion control, placing actions such as a wave, a nod or a pointing gesture at a specified
moment; and (3) talking video editing, which replaces words or adds gestures within a real recording
while leaving the surrounding footage intact (Section 3).
Avatar 6 is available in the HeyGen web app, the mobile app and the API from mid-November 2026.
From the video-reference formulation introduced by Avatar V [1] to the unified framework of Avatar 6, the
HeyGen avatar series rests on a single premise: a person’s own footage is the most faithful description of
their appearance, manner of speaking and motion.
Looking ahead, we will continue to scale the reference itself. Avatar 6 learns a person from a short recording;
we aim to extend its capacity and memory to tens of minutes and ultimately hours of a person’s footage, so
that identity fidelity keeps improving with the amount of footage a person provides.
Because an avatar reproduces a real person, it is gated by that person’s consent. Before an avatar created
from a person’s footage can generate any video, the person must record a short on-camera consent statement
that includes a one-time code generated for that session, so footage recorded in advance or taken from
elsewhere cannot satisfy the check; HeyGen then verifies that the person in the consent recording matches
the person in the training footage, and the avatar cannot render until this check passes. The verified consent
recording also becomes the identity reference against which later looks of the avatar are checked. All people
shown in this report are HeyGen employees or contracted actors who consented to the use of their likeness
in this report. The AI avatars in Section 3.1 are synthetic characters, and the real-person avatars there are
HeyGen public stock avatars.
2 Evaluation
2.1 Overview
We evaluate Avatar 6 against three references: state-of-the-art general video generation models; avatar-centric
video generation models, namely our previous models and commercial avatar products; and real footage of
the same people. Section 3 then presents applications.

Metrics. Against our previous models, raters on HeyGen’s in-house pairwise evaluation platform choose the

from their own scripts and voices or their own stock avatars, a side-by-side comparison category rating
(CCR). A Turing test with external raters compares Avatar 6 with real footage.
2.3 Comparison with General Video Generation Models
General video generation models now produce convincing talking people from an image and an audio track.
We compare Avatar 6 Quality with two of the strongest, Seedance 2.5 and Wan 3.0, in studies where HeyGen
employees judge videos of themselves by which looks most like them (Figure 1). Because the judges are
the people shown in the videos, these studies isolate identity fidelity, and Avatar 6 is preferred in 86.5% of
comparisons against Seedance 2.5 and 87.9% against Wan 3.0 (Figure 1). The external ratings in Section 2.4
measure something different: raters who have never met the person judge overall quality, lip sync and motion
in a general one-shot setting (Table 2 and Figure 4). In this setting the margin over Seedance 2.5 and Wan
3.0 is smaller: Avatar 6 wins 50.1% to 74.1% of the answers with a preference.

2.5 Comparison with Real Footage: Turing Test
The strictest test of likeness is whether Avatar 6 can pass for the person next to their own footage. We
use real recordings of HeyGen employees; each recording is split in two, one part serving as the Avatar 6
reference and the other held out as the real comparison (Figure 5, Table 3). In the single-clip test, raters
label each clip, real or generated, as real or AI, with Avatar 6 driven either by the person’s own voice or
by a HeyGen Voice clone of it [2]. In the side-by-side test, the real clip and the Avatar 6 clip say the same
sentence over the same audio, and raters pick the real one. The single-clip test covers 77 people and the
side-by-side test 76, with 20 external raters and about four raters per clip or pair. To our knowledge, this is
the largest Turing test of a talking-avatar model in a public technical report.

3 Applications
At its core, Avatar 6 is a talking avatar video generator supporting diverse identities, native output up to 2K
and unlimited duration (Section 3.1). This foundation supports a range of advanced applications, including
motion prompt control (Section 3.2), multi-speaker dialogue and podcasts (Section 3.3), and talking video
editing (Section 3.4), for which we also give a quantitative comparison with other methods.
3.1 Unlimited Talking Avatar Video Generation
Avatar 6 generates talking videos from a single photo, optionally with a short recording as an identity
reference, across diverse identities and visual styles, at up to 2K and with no fixed duration limit.
Any identity. Avatar 6 places no restriction on who appears in the reference: real people in studios, oﬀices,
on stage or at concerts, as well as stylised and cartoon characters. In every case it keeps not only the
appearance but the subject’s own manner of speaking, with the expressions, gestures and postures that
make them recognisable.
Native 2K. Avatar 6 supports a range of resolutions and aspect ratios, from portrait to landscape, and
natively generates 1080p and 2K: its super-resolution stage, developed in-house jointly with the generator,
is part of the model and restores identity-specific detail such as skin texture and hair.
Unlimited duration. Avatar 6 imposes no fixed limit on video length. A 30-minute talking video is
generated in a single pass, with identity, framing and this personal repertoire of motion maintained over the
full duration.

3.2 Motion Prompt Control
Avatar 6 accepts an optional written motion prompt that can request gestures on specific phrases, counted
gestures such as holding up three fingers, facial expressions, and head or body actions. An in-house motion
planner aligns each requested action with the words it refers to and blends it into the person’s own motion
style. On requests with a motion prompt, Avatar 6 is preferred over Avatar V in 78.1% of votes in online
pairwise preference tests.
Figure 7 shows Avatar 6 following written motion prompts, from a thumbs up or a wave to pointing, cupping
an ear or counting on the fingers. Each action lands on its words, while identity, setting, lip sync and the
person’s own motion style between actions are preserved.
3.3 Multi-Speaker Dialogue and Podcasts
Avatar 6 generates two speakers within a single frame, supporting interview, dialogue and podcast formats.
Figure 8 shows frames from a two-minute podcast between a host and a guest, alternating between wide
shots with both people in frame and closer views of each speaker.
3.4 Talking Video Editing
Avatar 6 edits existing recordings at the level of words and motion. A word or phrase can be replaced, a
gesture can be added on a chosen word, or both at once. The new speech is synthesised in the person’s own
voice, and only the span around the edit is regenerated, so the rest of the recording is preserved as filmed.
General video generation models instead regenerate the whole clip. We compare Avatar 6 with MiniMax
H3-max, Wan 3.0, Seedance 2.5 and HappyHorse Edit on word, motion and combined edits of real recordings.
Avatar 6 leads on every metric (Table 5). It is the only method that keeps the original recording: frames
outside the edit match the original at 48.3 dB, the level of video encoding noise, while every other method

edits, the requested gesture appears on the chosen word while the footage around it is preserved (Figure 9).
