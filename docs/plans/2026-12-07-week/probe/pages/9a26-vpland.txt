It released 21 AI-generated short-form dramas, said production costs fell 50% to 80% and production cycles shortened by as much as 80% versus traditional methods on certain AIGC animation and children's titles, and counts about 20 active long-form drama projects that combine live action and AI. 
 The Ferry Man paid for itself fast. The AI-generated content (AIGC) internet feature film series earned more than RMB14 million ($2.1 million) in revenue-sharing income. Each chapter recouped its full production cost in its first week. 
 AI output is scaling across formats. The 21 AIGC short-form dramas were an increase from the second quarter, and the company launched eight AI-generated internet feature films. 
 Micro dramas are mostly AI. CEO Yu Gong said AI-generated micro dramas made up nearly 90% of the category's content volume and nearly 80% of its views in the quarter's final month. 
 Adjusted profit returns in a down quarter. Revenue fell to RMB6.01 billion ($896 million), while content costs dropped 13% year over year and adjusted operating profit returned at RMB25 million ($3.7 million). 
 The Ferry Man reuses licensed actor likenesses, sets and props across chapters 
 The series, produced by iQIYI and GHY Culture & Media, streams on iQIYI and iQIYI International . 
 Producer Zhichao Li said in iQIYI's release on the series that the first two films went from project initiation to launch in less than four months.  iQIYI described where AI entered the pipeline: 
 Generated art department. AIGC created visual assets for concept design, sets, props and other art elements, which were then turned directly into video based on the director's requirements. 
 Simpler physical shoots. iQIYI said this simplified parts of the traditional process, including physical shoots and scene transitions. 
 Faster work on a green-screen-heavy scene. For a key scene in "The Ferry Man: The Dream of the Celestial Maiden," iQIYI said AI generation sped up a process that would traditionally involve months of visual effects and green-screen production, making complex scenes more feasible to produce. 
 A reusable digital cast. The three lead actors licensed their likenesses and recorded their own voiceovers. The digital characters, scenes and props were built as standardized, reusable assets so later chapters keep visual consistency at lower cost.

 About 20 long-form dramas mix live action and AI, with more AI films planned 
 Gong said on the Q3 earnings call that AI-generated content is the main source of supply for micro dramas and micro animations, and that iQIYI is extending it into internet feature films and longer dramas. Neither the earnings coverage nor the call highlights detail how live-action and AI work is divided on the roughly 20 hybrid projects. 
 We previously walked through one hybrid AI and live-action workflow built on Nano Banana Pro, Kling, Wan and Resolve. 
 More AI films ahead. Chief Content Officer Xiaohui Wang said iQIYI expects its supply of AI-generated internet films to increase substantially in the fourth quarter. 
 Savings framed as lasting. Wang said AI-driven cost reductions could be "structural and sustainable," though management gave no numerical forecast for their effect on earnings or margins. 
 Short-form slate. iQIYI expects about 190 revenue-sharing short-form dramas for the full year, with AI-generated titles making up roughly 30%. It defines short-form dramas as series with episodes typically running 15 to 30 minutes. 
 Citing figures from data provider Enlightent , iQIYI said its short-form dramas took the No. 1 domestic market share on a quarterly basis for the first time. It released 58 revenue-sharing short-form dramas in the quarter, and revenue from them rose both year over year and quarter over quarter. 
 AI production arrives alongside falling revenue and a shift to revenue-sharing 
 In iQIYI's third-quarter results , year over year, membership revenue fell 6% to RMB3.97 billion, content distribution revenue dropped 38% and online advertising rose 3% to RMB1.28 billion. 
 GAAP still in the red. iQIYI posted a GAAP operating loss of RMB41.9 million ($6.3 million), narrower than in the year-ago quarter. 
 AI in ads too. Revenue from AI-generated performance ads rose nearly 30% from the previous quarter. 
 Creator supply is growing. Uploads to IQID, the iQIYI Creator Center, rose more than 360% year over year, and launches of long-form revenue-sharing titles grew more than 200% from the prior quarter. 
 Gong said revenue-sharing shifts some spending from upfront investment to payments tied to performance. Speaking through a translator, he said, "In the future, the number of original titles will decrease," adding that he hoped premium originals would contribute a larger percentage.
