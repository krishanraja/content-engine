Headline: Festive market cancels bookings after AI backlash
Byline: Jonathan Morris and Britta Zeltmann, South West
Image caption: The AI promise (left) did not match the reality (right)

The organisers of a Christmas market have cancelled all bookings and promised to refund stallholders following a backlash to AI-generated promotional materials.

David Rowland, who produced the images for Kernow Christmas Market in Pool, Cornwall, said claims they did not match reality were a "lesson learned".

Images showed rows of stalls beneath Christmas decorations, fairy lights and festive displays for the event last weekend. But photographs taken by visitors showed a mostly empty hall with only a handful of traders, little Christmas decoration and buckets on the floor collecting water from a leaking roof.

Rowlands Attractions said it was "gutted" things "didn't go as planned".

It said: "The criticism we have faced and the words that have been seen on social media and across the news make us look at the world slightly differently.

"Kindness and compassion goes out of the window so easily to knock something that people don't care to understand the full story of."

The company added: "As a result of this, we are going to cancel and refund all current and future bookings and Kernow Christmas Market will take no further bookings for 2026.

"Any current bookings will be fully refunded and if any traders wish to continue to trade they must book direct with Pool Market."

Prior to the cancellation announcement, one stallholder, Ceilidh Hurrell, said she felt "let down" after paying £180 to trade at the event.

Hurrell, who runs Nails, Hair and More in Falmouth, paid for a pitch at the Christmas market, which formed part of the wider Winter Wonderland attraction with more stalls, Santa's grotto, an ice rink and fun fair.

She said she had expected a decorated venue to "entice people in for Christmas".

Instead, she said traders arrived to find no advertisement for the stallholders and no decorations.

"It was just an empty space. There was nothing to suggest that there's a market going on," Hurrell said.

Sammie Retallick, who attended with her partner on Saturday expecting a festive shopping experience, said they found "only about five stalls", as well as no decorations, Christmas music or lights.

Retallick said she felt sorry for traders who had invested money and time in the event.

"It's heartbreaking for them," she said.

Another trader claimed there were no signs directing visitors to the market area.

They added: "People turning up to the Winter Wonderland wouldn't have been aware we were even there.

"They've told us we're going to be part of this massive Winter Wonderland, 50-plus stalls, visitors of 2,500 people plus a day, then we get shoved in a dark corner of a warehouse."

Prior to the cancellation announcement, Rowland, who organises Winter Wonderland, said he took on the promotion of the market to help the Pool Market location.

"I thought it would be nice to help them," he said.

He said the AI images were "so easy to do" and gave a "rough idea" of what people could expect.

"You are never going to make it look exactly like the picture," he said.

"It's a lesson learned."

Pool Market has been approached for comment.

Kernow Christmas Market said on Facebook the event was organised and operated by Pool Market, running alongside Cornwall Winter Wonderland.

It said: "We are a small, family run business that started many years ago here in Cornwall.

"This year, as an add on from Cornwalls Winter Wonderland we thought we would try and do a good deed and bring a little festive magic into Pool Market and for those who live locally in Cornwall.

"The agreement was always with Pool Market to do their bit and we do ours. We advertise it, book the stalls and they do the rest- such as signage, decorating and greeting the traders and helping them with queries.

"We are gutted, just as much as you all that things didn't go as planned."

It said Cornwall Winter Wonderland will remain open until 3 January and Rowlands' focus will remain "always remain on providing the very best experience for those who visit".

It added: "Entry is free, kindness is free, and we will always be grateful for those who continue to recognise the efforts, cost and dedication that goes on behind the scenes."
