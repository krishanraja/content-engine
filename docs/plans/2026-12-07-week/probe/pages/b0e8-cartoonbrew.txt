Following a hugely unpopular update to Etsy’s terms of use, artists are shutting down their shops in droves and posting illustrated goodbye notes to tell customers where to find them next.

The reason is right there in the online market’s updated terms. Starting December 16, the agreement explicitly gives Etsy and its third-party partners and vendors permission to use content uploaded by sellers to train and improve AI systems.

Ironically, just weeks ago, Etsy announced tighter rules on misleading AI product photos and bot-generated listings. The marketplace still allows sellers to list work generated from their own prompts, as long as they disclose the use of AI to buyers.

Etsy says it uses sellers’ content to spot fraud and help shoppers find products, and that it doesn’t train models designed to copy artists’ work. That said, the vague terms leave open just how its partners and vendors might use that same content.

For an independent animator, an Etsy shop can bring in money between gigs by selling character stickers, prints from a short, or zines of development art. It’s also a popular platform for merch sales that can help finance original projects.

Closing a shop just weeks before the holidays means giving up potential sales, years of reviews, and a storefront customers already know how to find. The artists posting their goodbyes have decided it’s worth walking away from that revenue source to keep their work from being used to train AI.

Etsy will surely continue to run its site and fill the search results with more prints, stickers, and custom pet portraits than anyone could ever scroll through. But when the artists who made the marketplace worth visiting are telling customers where to find them next, why keep sifting through the slop?
