More Than 80% of Americans Want an AI Music ‘Kill Switch,’ UMG Research Finds
 Ashley King November 19, 2026Photo Credit: Rich Smith
A new survey from Deezer finds that 41% of listeners want to filter out AI music entirely, but research from UMG suggests that number is much higher.
According to a survey published by Deezer this week, 41% of listeners would prefer to filter out 100% of AI-generated music from their music streaming platform, rather than just having it transparently labeled. However, Universal Music Group (UMG) conducted its own research in this area and found the number to be much higher.
“Consumer research indicates […] that the vast majority of consumers demand transparency. They want the content identified. They want the content labeled. An increasing percentage are looking for what we call a kill switch,” said Michael Nash, Chief Digital Officer and EVP at Universal Music Group.
“We did surveys earlier in the year that pointed to, in the U.S. market, 74% of consumers—globally, 68% of consumers—wanting to have the option to flip a switch, and basically screen out 100% of AI-generated content from their listening experience. We just did a new round of search, and in the U.S., that number is now north of 80%.”
Deezer also showed a shift in attitudes toward AI in music, particularly among respondents in the United States, who it says were “significantly more negative” toward AI in 2026 than in 2025. Moreover, those who believe AI will play a significant role in music creation over the next decade dropped from 62% to 51%.
Now, 69% of U.S. respondents believe that the use of AI by musicians and composers might lead to a loss of creativity, up from 65% in 2025. The number of U.S. respondents who say they have listened to fully AI-generated music also fell from 52% to 45%, marking the only market out of the eight surveyed to record a decline.
“It is clear from this year’s survey that an overwhelming majority still want transparency when it comes to AI music and that there is a significant gap between what listeners think is in their playlists and what is actually there, which highlights the need for continued efforts to achieve greater awareness for music streaming users,” said Alexis Lanternier, CEO of Deezer.
“Concerns about AI having a negative impact on creativity and artistry are clearly growing, especially in the United States, where there is a striking difference in attitudes compared to last year. A majority of people in all surveyed countries also consider fully AI-generated music a threat to the livelihood of artists.”
Interestingly, Deezer says that 74% of music streaming users in the survey believed their playlists contained no fully AI-generated music. However, the company says that nearly 57% of the 53,000-plus people who joined its platform in October already had at least one fully AI-generated track in their playlists. This number is based on Deezer’s internal data on new subscribers joining from other streaming platforms, not specifically from those surveyed.
