Epic Uses AI to Port Over 20,000 Fortnite Blueprints to Verse

Epic's AI experiment converted over 20,000 Fortnite Blueprints into roughly 3 million lines of Verse.
Unreal Engine 6 drops Blueprints and Actors entirely so gameplay must be rebuilt in Verse on Scene Graph.
Epic is backporting UE6 AI tools into the UE 5.9 Preview to ease the eventual conversion burden.
Epic Games said on November 20, 2026 that an AI-assisted experiment had produced a working Fortnite Battle Royale prototype with about 3 million lines of Verse after porting more than 20,000 Blueprints. Its technical update also changes the path for developers moving existing projects to Unreal Engine 6.
The prototype supports a revised migration plan. UE6, including Early Access, will not include Blueprints or the Actor Gameplay Framework; gameplay will instead be built in the Verse programming language on the new Scene Graph gameplay framework. For studios with UE5 projects, the upgrade path now depends on conversion instead of carrying those existing technologies into the next engine.
That reverses Epic’s June 22, 2026 roadmap, which said Actors and Blueprints would coexist in UE6 Early Access and 6.0 while Scene Graph matured. That plan targeted Early Access for the end of 2027 and a full release 12–18 months later.
Epic acknowledged that news of the framework decision had already emerged from Unreal Fest Tokyo, held November 3–4, 2026. The new post’s fresh contribution is the prototype evidence and a fuller explanation of the migration approach.
Fortnite Conversion Still Needs Validation
Epic said the experiment started two weeks before the update and sought to convert every Battle Royale Blueprint to Verse. The conversion project had been expected to take years. The team is still iterating to validate the prototype’s performance, memory usage and architecture. The large-scale effort is intended to make conversions easier for other developers.
Epic expects AI to make UE5-to-UE6 conversion practical and cost-efficient, with potentially modest costs by the time UE 6.0 is released, and to keep UE6 on its existing schedule or deliver it sooner. The rationale is that AI task costs are falling while the complexity of converting a game remains broadly constant.
Epoch AI researchers estimated in The plunging price of thought, published September 22, 2026, that the cost of a fixed level of AI performance had fallen about 47% per quarter, or 13-fold annually, across five benchmarks with data beginning in 2023. The estimates are approximate: benchmark performance is not synonymous with useful work, the dataset spans barely three years, and real users do not constantly switch to whichever model is cheapest for each task. Epic’s forecast extends that benchmark trend to game conversion.
UE 5.9 Brings UE6 AI Tools to UE5
Developers can keep projects on UE5, and Epic said targeted UE5 features, fixes and support will continue while UE6 work proceeds. The company has also begun moving AI tooling built for UE6 back to UE5, starting with the UE 5.9 MegaJam Preview.
The UE 5.9 Preview expands Python access to editor scripting and adds Model Context Protocol settings and toolsets that connect models and external agents to projects. The experimental Unreal Canvas supplies a shared workspace for people and agents, with prompt-driven text, image, video and mesh generation. The workspace combines asset creation with current editor context, references and feedback that developers can control.
The preview includes an experimental WebAssembly target with WebGPU rendering for browser delivery. Preview 2 also lets Blueprint-only projects package for the web with the engine’s standard game executable, though the web toolkit remains required. Epic said AI agents built much of UE 5.9, including most of its web-platform work.
The wider UE6 plan still combines UE5 and Unreal Editor for Fortnite into one engine, with Scene Graph built from scratch on Verse. Epic’s design goals for the engine are a new data model, a Verse-first architecture, modularity and AI operability. The June roadmap also proposed making content, code and economies portable across games, ecosystems and engines through open standards and specifications.
For developers, the practical transition combines continued UE5 support and near-term AI tooling in 5.9 with a UE6 framework that requires conversion to Verse and Scene Graph. The Fortnite prototype establishes the scale of Epic’s conversion attempt; the continuing performance, memory and architecture validation will determine how well the generated codebase meets production demands.
