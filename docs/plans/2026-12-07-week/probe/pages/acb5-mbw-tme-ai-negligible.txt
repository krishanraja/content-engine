Tencent Music says AI content streams are ‘negligible’ – as it takes legal action over AI infringement

November 17, 2026

By Murray Stassen

Photo credit: Piotr Swat / Shutterstock.com

Tencent Music Entertainment says streams of AI-generated content remain “negligible” – and are declining as a share of listening.

Continue to article...

TME also says it is taking legal action over AI-related infringement, and that its stance is aligned with that of “the leading global rights holders”.

The company’s management made the comments on its Q3 2026 earnings call, following the publication of its quarterly results on Tuesday (November 17).

The remarks follow warnings from TME‘s leadership in May that the “proliferation of unauthorized AI-generated content” was creating “headwinds” for its music subscription growth, as MBW reported at the time.

In his prepared remarks on the Q3 call, Executive Chairman Cussion Pang said: “While technology advancement makes content supply more abundant, users are becoming more selective about what they choose to engage with.

“As a result, AI content stream volume remains negligible with a declining share of listening, while high-quality differentiated content continues to gain traction.

“This trend further endorses our belief in the value of music that builds emotional connections and stands the test of time.”

“AI content stream volume remains negligible with a declining share of listening, while high-quality differentiated content continues to gain traction.”

Cussion Pang, TME

Later in the call, a Bank of America analyst asked about TME’s outlook for 2027.

Speaking through an interpreter, Pang said: “Looking ahead into 2027, first is about copyright protection.”

He said the proliferation of “low-quality and unauthorized AI-generated content” still weighs on subscription monetization.

Pang said TME’s stance was aligned with that of “the leading global rights holders”, and that it was tackling infringement through “legal action and proactive content management, with encouraging early results”.

He added: “Streams from AI-generated songs remain very low and with a declining trend.”

In his original Chinese remarks, Pang said TME’s own proactive control measures had kept the share of plays of AI-generated songs on its platforms at a relatively low level.

He continued: “Despite some short-term pressure on membership and advertising, our IP-related business continues to gain momentum with market share gains.”

TME did not name the rights holders it was referring to, or identify any specific legal cases.

In its 2025 ESG report, published in April, TME said it collaborates with music labels “to deploy technology that identifies and addresses unauthorized AI-generated content”, as MBW reported in May.

The report said TME took down more than 250,000 policy-violating songs and reviewed over 600,000 cases involving “high-risk copyright content” across its platforms in 2025.

TME also said it identifies and tags AI-generated content on its platforms using audio models.

TME said it only incorporates content into AI model training “after obtaining licensing from copyright holders”.

On the company’s Q1 2026 earnings call in May, Pang warned of “industry chaos”, adding: “We welcome tech innovation, but we’ll do everything in our power to suppress ‘song-washing’ and other infringing behaviors.”

CEO Ross Liang accused competitors on the same call of using AI “to quickly fill their music libraries” in a bid to attract users.

TME faces competition in China from ByteDance‘s Soda Music and NetEase Cloud Music.

On the Q3 call, Liang said TME’s music business “continued to deliver steady progress this quarter despite the persistent competitive overhang”.

In March, Pang told analysts that “in 2026, our subscription revenue will experience some short-term pressure due to the intensive competition”.

TME‘s membership services revenue rose 9.2% YoY to RMB 4.99 billion (USD $744 million) in Q3, according to its earnings report.

CFO Shirley Hu said on the call that audio platform Ximalaya, whose acquisition TME completed in May, contributed to that growth.

The company stopped disclosing subscriber numbers after publishing its full-year 2025 results in March.

TME also develops AI music tools of its own.

In March, Liang said TME’s AI music production platform had more than 10 million users and over 150,000 professional creators, as MBW reported.

Liang said at the time that, on AI-generated music, “in terms of royalty and revenue sharing, we do not see material changes”.

Outside China, Deezer said in July that it was receiving nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks per day, more than 50% of daily uploads on peak days, as MBW reported.

Deezer says such tracks account for just 1% to 3% of streams on its own platform.

Believe founder and CEO Denis Ladegaillerie told MBW earlier this year that AI-generated content accounts for “less than 0.5%” of total streams across the industry.
