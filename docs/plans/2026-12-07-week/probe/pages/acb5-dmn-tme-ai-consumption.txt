Tencent Music Says AI Music Consumption is ‘Negligible’ and ‘Declining’ Across Its Platforms

 Ashley King

November 17, 2026

Photo Credit: Tencent Music Entertainment

Tencent Music revealed negligible and declining AI music consumption across its platforms during its recent quarterly earnings call.

Despite the enormous upload volumes associated with so-called “AI slop,” Tencent Music says very few people are actually seeking out and listening to such content. The Chinese company, which oversees properties like QQ Music, Kugou Music, Kuwo Music, WeSing, and Ximalaya, recently revealed that data point during its quarterly earnings call on November 17.

“While technology advancement makes content supply more abundant, users are becoming more selective about what they choose to engage with,” said Tencent Music Entertainment (TME) Executive Chairman Cussion Pang. “As a result, AI content stream volumes remain negligible, with a declining share of listening, while high-quality, differentiated content continues to gain traction.”

“This trend further endorses our belief in the value of music that builds emotional connections and stands the test of time,” Pang continued. “When content continues to resonate with users, it can grow into premium IP and create new opportunities for engagement and consumption.”

TME attributed this low share to proactive content management, filtering out low-quality AI slop, alongside legal action against unauthorized, copyright-infringing AI-generated music.

Pang followed up on those remarks by detailing how TME is focusing on strengthening its differentiated human-artist catalog—specifically citing expansion across K-pop, Mandopop, and drama soundtracks, rather than relying on an abundant AI-generated supply.

Revenue grew by 6% to 8.9 billion CNY ($1.34 billion) in Q3, while net profit remained roughly flat at 2.2 billion CNY ($330 million). Membership, merchandise, live performances, and the newly consolidated Ximalaya audio business offset its weakness in traditional advertising. Advertising continues to face macroeconomic and competitive pressure, with the company reporting adjusted EBITDA up 3% to 3 billion CNY ($450 million).
Music-related services revenue rose 10% from the previous year to 7.7 billion CNY ($1.15 million), aided by the addition of Ximalaya and growth in artist merch and offline performances. Membership services revenue increased 9% to 5 billion CNY ($750 million), with Ximalaya contributing to that gain.

Revenue from marketing and consumption services rose by 12% to 2.7 billion CNY ($405 million), led by merchandise and performance-related services. The company delivered physical albums for artists, including Cai Xukun and Zhou Shen during the quarter, as well as working with artists like Silence Wang on merch and performances.
