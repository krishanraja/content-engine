 ElevenLabs to Acquire Japanese Voice AI Company Kotoba in Reported $100 Million Deal 
 By Radar TeamNovember 17, 202616 mins read 
AI voice technology company ElevenLabs has agreed to acquire Japanese voice AI specialist Kotoba Technologies in a deal reportedly worth approximately $100 million.
The acquisition, announced on November 16, 2026, represents a significant expansion of ElevenLabs’ international operations and its ambitions to develop more natural, multilingual AI voice technology.
Although ElevenLabs has not officially disclosed the financial terms, reports from Nikkei Asia and Business Insider Japan place the transaction at around $100 million.
The deal is expected to close later in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to regulatory approval and customary closing conditions.
For a company that has rapidly expanded beyond AI voice generation into music creation and entertainment, the acquisition could have wider implications for the future of AI-generated audio.
What is Kotoba Technologies?
Kotoba Technologies is an AI research company specialising in speech recognition, voice generation and real-time translation.
Founded in 2023 by AI researchers Noriyuki Kojima and Jungo Kasai, the company is headquartered in San Francisco, with its research and development operations based in Tokyo.
Kotoba focuses on developing AI speech models capable of understanding and reproducing the complexities of the Japanese language.
Its flagship voice model, Koto, supports several applications:
Text-to-speech technology that converts written Japanese into natural-sounding speech
Speech-to-text systems that transcribe spoken conversations
Speech-to-speech technology for simultaneous interpretation and translation
Conversational AI systems designed to handle Japanese-language interactions
The company’s technology is already being used by major enterprise customers.
Salesforce, for example, uses Kotoba’s Koto model to support Japanese-language conversations through its Agentforce Voice platform.
This existing commercial adoption makes Kotoba an attractive acquisition for ElevenLabs as it expands its AI-powered voice products.
Why is ElevenLabs acquiring Kotoba?
The acquisition forms part of ElevenLabs’ strategy to make conversational AI accessible across different languages and cultures.
Japan represents an important market for voice AI, but developing accurate Japanese-language models presents challenges beyond simply translating English speech.
Japanese communication frequently relies on context, levels of politeness and subtle differences in speech patterns.
An effective AI voice assistant therefore needs to understand not only the words being spoken but also the cultural expectations behind them.
Kotoba has developed technology specifically designed to address these challenges.
By combining Kotoba’s Japanese-language expertise with ElevenLabs’ existing AI models and infrastructure, the companies aim to improve how businesses and consumers interact with AI.
ElevenLabs CEO and co-founder Mati Staniszewski said the company’s ambition is to develop technology that reflects the language and social norms of different cultures.
The acquisition is also expected to strengthen ElevenLabs’ presence in Japan, where it established a local subsidiary in April 2025.
ElevenLabs continues its rapid expansion
The reported $100 million acquisition comes during a period of substantial growth for ElevenLabs.
Founded in 2022, the company initially gained attention for its realistic AI-generated voices and text-to-speech technology.
Since then, it has expanded into conversational AI agents, automated dubbing, audio production and AI music generation.
In September 2026, ElevenLabs announced that its valuation had reached $22 billion following a $300 million employee share tender offer.
That represented a doubling of the $11 billion valuation announced during its $500 million Series D funding round earlier in the year.
The company’s expansion has increasingly focused on enterprise customers using AI voices for customer service, digital assistants and automated conversations.
The Kotoba acquisition provides access to specialised research expertise and an established Japanese-language AI model.
It also demonstrates how major AI companies are increasingly acquiring smaller developers with expertise in particular languages, technologies or regional markets.
What does the acquisition mean for ElevenMusic?
Although Kotoba primarily focuses on conversational AI rather than music generation, the acquisition is particularly interesting given ElevenLabs’ growing involvement in the music industry.
ElevenLabs entered the AI music generation market in August 2025, introducing its music technology alongside licensing arrangements with Merlin and Kobalt.
Its ElevenMusic platform has since developed into an AI-powered music creation service that allows users to generate, edit, remix and discover music.
The company has also been developing tools that offer greater control over vocals, instrumentation and musical arrangements.
In September 2026, ElevenLabs announced a multi-year agreement with Universal Music Group.
The partnership includes plans to develop a separate AI-powered platform allowing fans to create remixes, mashups and alternative interpretations of music from participating artists.
The agreement reflects the growing interest among major music companies in licensed AI music experiences.
Could Kotoba improve AI-generated singing and vocals?
Kotoba’s expertise in Japanese-language speech technology could eventually contribute to improvements across ElevenLabs’ audio products.
For example, multilingual voice models may help AI systems better understand pronunciation, timing and the nuances of different languages.
These capabilities could potentially benefit music creation tools, particularly those involving synthetic vocals and multilingual content.
However, ElevenLabs has not announced any specific plans to integrate Kotoba’s technology into ElevenMusic.
For now, the acquisition is primarily focused on improving Japanese-language conversational AI.
Any potential application to AI-generated music remains speculative.
Why Japan is an important market for AI audio
Japan has a substantial entertainment industry spanning recorded music, anime, gaming, film and digital media.
These industries create opportunities for AI-powered audio technology, particularly in localisation and multilingual content production.
For example, voice AI could help media companies develop Japanese-language versions of content originally produced in other languages.
Real-time translation and synthetic speech technology could also support international gaming platforms, customer-facing applications and digital entertainment services.
The potential applications extend beyond entertainment.
Companies operating in Japan could use more natural voice AI systems for customer support, sales, accessibility and automated services.
ElevenLabs has indicated that it plans to expand its local sales operations and continue investing in Japanese infrastructure following the acquisition.
Kotoba’s 15 employees are also expected to join ElevenLabs as part of the transaction.
AI voice technology and the future of the music industry
AI-generated audio is becoming an increasingly important part of the wider music technology industry.
While AI music generators have attracted significant attention, voice technology also presents opportunities for artists, labels and music businesses.
Potential applications include:
1. Multilingual artist promotion
Voice AI could make it easier for musicians to create spoken promotional content in multiple languages.
For artists attempting to reach international audiences, this could provide additional ways to communicate with fans.
2. Music and video localisation
AI dubbing and translation tools could help creators adapt interviews, promotional videos and educational content for different markets.
3. Interactive fan experiences
Conversational AI could support new forms of interactive entertainment, allowing fans to engage with authorised digital experiences connected to artists and music.
4. AI-assisted music production
More advanced speech technology may eventually contribute to improvements in synthetic vocals, pronunciation and multilingual music creation.
However, the development of AI voice technology also raises questions around consent, licensing and the commercial use of human voices.
Artists and rights holders will need confidence that their recordings, identities and performances are protected as these technologies develop.
When will ElevenLabs complete the Kotoba acquisition?
ElevenLabs expects the acquisition to close before the end of 2026.
The transaction remains subject to regulatory approval and other closing conditions.
Once completed, Kotoba will join ElevenLabs’ operations, with its researchers continuing to contribute to the development of Japanese-language voice technology.
The combined teams aim to improve AI-powered conversations and support the adoption of voice interfaces across Japan and other markets.
What happens next for ElevenLabs?
The acquisition of Kotoba highlights the increasing importance of specialist AI research as major technology companies compete to develop more capable voice systems.
For ElevenLabs, the transaction offers an opportunity to strengthen its position in Japan while improving the multilingual capabilities of its existing products.
For the music industry, the deal is another reminder that the companies developing generative audio are expanding well beyond basic text-to-speech applications.
With ElevenMusic, its Universal Music Group partnership and continued investment in advanced voice technology, ElevenLabs is positioning itself across several areas of the rapidly evolving AI audio market.
Whether Kotoba’s technology eventually becomes part of ElevenMusic remains to be seen.
However, the reported $100 million transaction demonstrates the commercial value being placed on specialised speech AI technology.
As artificial intelligence becomes more closely integrated into music creation, distribution and promotion, developments like this are likely to remain important for artists and music businesses.
