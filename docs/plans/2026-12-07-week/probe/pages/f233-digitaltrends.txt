 Spotify is giving every artist a bouncer to keep AI slop off their page 
 Artist Profile Protection is open to every artist, after a months-long test. The default setting still lets a release go live if you ignore it. 
 By
 Dwayne Cubbins 
 Published November 18, 2026 
 Save 
 Spotify 
 Spotify has opened Artist Profile Protection to all artists on the service, eight months after the initial test with a few thousand artists . It allows artists to review, and in some cases approve, music that’s being delivered under their name in Spotify for Artists.
 “Artists should never have to go and find someone else’s music under their name,” Sam Duboff, Spotify’s global head of marketing and policy, told TechCrunch . “Their artist profile should be in their control.”
 How the review works
 Admins and editors will turn the tool on in Spotify for Artists . The default setting is Review releases, which will send you an email when a delivery lists you as an artist. You can remove yourself prior to the release going live, or up to 28 days after it has gone live. If you do nothing, it will go live as scheduled and will count towards your stats and recommendations. If you remove yourself, it won’t appear on your profile, but it may still show up under your name on other services.
 Spotify 
 The new tool still relies on artists watching their inbox. In default mode, a release you’ve ignored will still go live under your name. So artists may want to pay much closer attention to their inboxes going forward.
 Recommended Videos
 A stricter Block releases mode will hold a delivery until you approve it (which can take up to 24 hours). Spotify notes that this option is still only available to a limited number of artists, typically those with common names. Trusted distributors can include an artist key so that legitimate releases can bypass the manual step entirely. Some content providers are not eligible for removal or approval.
 Why the tool is arriving now
 Spotify banned unauthorized AI voice impersonation in September 2025, announced it would be supporting DDEX AI credits, and reported that over 75m spam tracks had been removed in the previous year. Separately, the BBC reported that an AI song had appeared on the verified profile of Blaze Foley (who passed away in 1989). The Guardian also reported on The Velvet Sundown , which had passed a million Spotify streams before it was revealed to be an AI project . Deezer has said that AI tracks now account for 44% of its daily uploads. 
 Meanwhile, YouTube Music has started to make AI tracks easier to spot , and Australia’s charts have banned AI tracks . Platforms have been labeling, filtering, and banning AI tracks for more than a year. What artists still couldn’t do was decide what showed up under their own name, and that’s exactly what Spotify aims to address with this release.

 Xiaomi’s clip earbuds are so pretty, my social circle confused them for new piercings 
 Even customs confused the Xiaomi Clip earbuds for jewelry 
 I've tried a bunch of open-ear earbuds over the last couple of years. While I don't personally gravitate towards them, they serve a few specific and useful features. The freedom they offer has its perks, but they aren't always comfortable--at least for me. Some have bulky hooks that wrap around your ears, while others start becoming uncomfortable after a few hours. 
 So after Xiaomi sent over the new Clip earbuds, along with the Xiaomi 18 Pros, I was really looking forward to how different these feel, and not just how they sound. After all, clip-on earbuds have become pretty popular, and several manufacturers have already experimented with more fashionable designs. And getting the shiny Sand Gold variant, I wasn't expecting people to start asking whether I got my ears pierced.
