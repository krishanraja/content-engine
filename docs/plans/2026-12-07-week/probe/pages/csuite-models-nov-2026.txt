Violet days carry the number of entries in the ledger below, from October 31 to November 26, 2026. Amber dots are dates already on a vendor’s calendar, past ones included. Seven October releases our last edition missed are in the ledger too, marked by their October dates. This post is updated every Friday and summarised once the month closes.
On November 2, two days before the date it had promised, Moonshot put the full weights of Kimi K3.1 Pro on Hugging Face: 2.8 trillion parameters in about 1.56 TB of files. Three days later OpenAI launched GPT-6 Cyber, a model most readers cannot call without an application. Those two releases sum up the first week of November. Plenty arrived, and the most useful question for each one is not how good it is but who is allowed to use it, where, and on what terms.
The second week moved the other way. On November 9 Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 at a lower price than the version before it, and on November 10 Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon, which had been limited to security teams, to every paying developer. Those were then the two highest-scoring models on Artificial Analysis’s index, and both take a credit card instead of an application.
The third week filled in the tier below. Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12 at $1.50 and $7.50 per million tokens, with a free tier, and Alibaba followed on November 16 with Qwen4 Max at $1.50 and $4.50. Thinking Machines also posted the Inkling 2 Small weights it had promised a week earlier.
The days since crowded the top of the board. OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra on November 17 at $8 and $40, the same price as Fable 5.2, and Google added Gemini 4 Neon a day later at $3 and $15. Microsoft announced seven models at its Ignite conference the same week, and the image model on our waiting list since October was one of them.
The fourth week belonged to downloads. Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini on November 20, an Apache 2.0 model of about 20 GB that runs on a Mac with 32 GB of memory. Tencent and Baidu both posted weights this roundup had been waiting for, and Google made Genie 4, which generates a world you can steer as it plays, generally available to paying developers.
This is edition five of the monthly roundup, and the second live one. It opened on November 6, covering everything since October’s final sweep, and it is updated every Friday and summarised once the month closes. The method has not changed: every date, price and spec below was checked against a primary source on the day of its pass and linked in place, vendor benchmarks are labeled as such, and the non-events are named at the end. As of the November 26 pass, 94 entries have landed since October 31, and seven October releases we missed open the ledger.
The TL;DR so far: three things to act on
If you pay for a top-tier text model, there are four new ones to test, and one of their prices doubles on December 1. Claude Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra both cost $8 in and $40 out per million tokens, each down from $10 and $50. Gemini 4 Neon costs $3 and $15. Gemini 4 Carbon costs $2 and $10 until November 30, then $4 and $20, which will make it dearer than Neon. One tier down, Gemini 4 Hydrogen costs $1.50 and $7.50 and has a free tier to try it on. If you make images or short video clips, price them again, because the cheap end moved. Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash costs $0.07 a second at 720p, MiniMax’s H3 Lite costs $0.02 a second through Replicate, GPT Image 2.6 returns SVG files and animated GIFs at GPT Image 2.5’s prices, Seedream 5.5 now has an API at $0.02 to $0.035 an image, and Microsoft prices MAI-Image-2.7 Flash at about a cent. Ideogram 4.6, new on November 19, renders at up to 4096×4096. If you plan to download any of this month’s open models, read the license before the model card. Three releases in the MiniMax H3 family (P-H3-Image, H3 Image and inclusionAI’s Duo) exclude the US, the EU, the UK and Korea, and three inclusionAI repositories still state no license at all. Text: four of this month’s models score 60 or more, and all take a credit card
Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9. The release notes price it at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, against $10 and $50 for Fable 5.1, with a 1M context and up to 128K tokens of output. It scores 65 at its highest effort setting on Artificial Analysis’s Intelligence Index, still the top of the board on November 19. Anthropic’s launch post says it writes “up to 30% faster” than 5.1, and it also says most coding and knowledge work should start with the cheaper Claude Opus 5.5. Claude Mythos 5.2 is the same underlying model for security and biomedical research, offered “only to organizations verified through Anthropic’s verification programs.” OpenRouter and Runware list Fable 5.2 at the same price.
Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon became generally available on November 10. Its first release on October 28 went to “a subset of cyber defenders,” and the new post opens it to “all paying developers and enterprises.” The pricing page lists $2 in and $10 out per million tokens through November 30, then $4 and $20, with no free tier and a 1M context. It scores 60 on the same index, second to Fable 5.2 on the day it opened. Google still recommends Gemini 4 Argon for most work and describes Carbon as the model for tasks that “test the limits.” A version tuned for security work remains inside Google’s Fairwind Program.
Two days later Google filled in the tier most people will use. Gemini 4 Hydrogen (November 12) is, per the release notes, built “for everyday coding and agentic workflows.” It costs $1.50 in and $7.50 out per million tokens, takes text, images, video, audio and PDFs in a 1M context, and unlike Carbon it is on the free tier. It adds a fourth thinking level, xhigh, above the default high. At that level it scores 54 on the index, six points under Carbon for less than 40% of what Carbon will cost from December 1. Google’s migration guide treats it as the successor to Gemini 3.8 Flash. That model’s introductory price ends on December 31, when it rises to the same $1.50 and $7.50.
OpenAI answered on November 17 with GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol. The changelog offers Astra “for demanding reasoning and coding tasks” and Sol “for everyday coding and agent workflows.” The model page prices Astra at $8 in and $40 out per million tokens, against $10 and $50 for GPT-6 Astra, and Sol stays at GPT-6.1 Sol’s $2 and $10. Both have a 1,050,000-token context and up to 128,000 tokens of output, and requests above 272,000 input tokens are billed at a higher rate. On the index Astra scores 62 at its highest effort setting, against 65 for Fable 5.2 at the same price, and Sol scores 57. Two details matter if you are migrating: neither model supports OpenAI’s cheaper Flex processing, and a new option for background jobs takes 30% or 40% off the token rates in exchange for a two-hour or eight-hour wait. OpenRouter lists both models at OpenAI’s prices, and Runware has added both.
Google’s third Gemini 4 release in nine days came on November 18. Gemini 4 Neon, per the migration guide, “sits between” Hydrogen and Carbon and is meant for complex work that runs often. It costs $3 in and $15 out per million tokens on the paid tier only, with a 1M context and the same four thinking levels as Hydrogen. At xhigh it scores 61 on the index, a point above Carbon’s 60 at Carbon’s high setting. That makes December’s arithmetic simple: once Carbon rises to $4 and $20, Neon is the cheaper of the two and scores about the same. Google’s guide still points to Carbon for “your hardest architecture, debugging, science, and security problems.” Four of this month’s models now score 60 or more on this index, and every one of them is open to anyone with a credit card.
Meta’s Muse Ember (November 9) sits at the other end. Meta’s post calls it “our smallest and most efficient model,” built for the work agents repeat all day. It takes text, images, audio, video and PDFs in a 1M context, and Meta’s price list charges $0.10 in and $0.50 out per million tokens for requests up to 200K input tokens. A longer request is billed at $0.30 and $1.50 for all of its tokens, not only the ones past 200K. It scores 36 on the index and its weights are closed. An API is a set of rules that lets one piece of software ask another for information or services. Think of a wall socket: you don't need to understand the power plant, you just plug in. Similarly, a weather app plugs into a weather service's API to fetch forecasts.

Kimi K3.1 Pro was the first week’s biggest release. It scores 46.5 on the index, and Runware began serving it on November 4 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with a 1M context. For scale, the same index puts GPT-6.1 Terra at 51 for half that price, so this is not a new mid-tier pick. What is new is that the weights are public, which the open-weights section covers.
OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber arrived on November 5. The model page describes a model for investigating vulnerabilities and reproducing them “in an authorized test environment,” and the pricing page lists $20 in and $100 out per million tokens, with a 1,050,000-token context. An organization needs approval through OpenAI’s Daybreak program before it can call it. It was the third model in nine days released only to approved groups, after Gemini 4 Carbon and GPT-6 Rosalind; Carbon has since opened up, and Rosalind has not. On the same changelog, OpenAI set February 1, 2027 for the removal of gpt-5.5-cyber-preview.
Tencent’s Hy4 reached OpenRouter on November 4 at $0.417/$1.25 with a 1M context and image input. The listing carries a 50% discount, so the full price would be about $0.83/$2.50, and it scores 34.6 on the index. On November 12 the discount was still in place and Tencent’s Hugging Face repository for it was still private. Z.ai’s GLM-5.3-FlashX-Legal (November 2) is a legal-domain variant at $0.37/$1.25, confirmed on Z.ai’s pricing page, and Ricursive’s Ripple-65B Preview, covered below, is the first week’s other new name at $0.20/$0.60 hosted.
Alibaba’s Qwen4 Max went on general sale on November 16, five days after a preview limited to Alibaba’s Token Plan subscribers. Model Studio’s price list charges $1.50 in and $4.50 out per million tokens for requests up to 1M input tokens, a quarter less than the $2 and $6 of Qwen3.8 Max. It reads text, images and video, and reasoning is on by default. It scores 43 on the index, which puts it under Kimi K3.1 Pro (46.5) at less than half of Kimi’s output price. Artificial Analysis also measured it as slow: 32 minutes per task against a median of 13. OpenRouter lists it 5% lower, at $1.425/$4.275. Tencent added a smaller sibling to Hy4 the same day: Hy4 flash reached OpenRouter at $0.035/$0.14 with a 1M context, also at a 50% discount, so expect about $0.07/$0.28 later. We found no Tencent post for it at the time. Its weights followed on November 17, covered in the open-weights section, while Hy4 itself was still discounted on November 19 with its repository still private.
Microsoft used its Ignite conference on November 17 to announce seven models at once. Two are for writing code. Local-runnable: Ripple needs 39 GB, and H3 Image got local support on launch day
stable-diffusion.cpp added P-H3-Image in master-1049 (November 4), alongside a second, higher-resolution pass for H3. The smallest diffusion file is 13.36 GB, and with the 3.6 GB text encoder and a 3.2 GB VAE the total lands near 20 GB before anything is generated. master-1051 and master-1052 added packed 4-bit models and faster 4-bit kernels on NVIDIA cards. Since master-1044 the Linux CPU and Vulkan builds target Ubuntu 26.04 only, so older distributions need an older build.
The project kept that pace in the second week. Builds from November 7 and 8 added ControlNet Union for SDXL, RelayVSR video upscaling on NVIDIA cards, HyperFlow H3 Flash and SeedVR2 upscaling for images and video. master-1088 added MiniMax H3 Image on November 10, the day MiniMax released it, along with a lighter decoder for H3 video. H3 Image is not a small model: the smallest repackaged file we found, in Comfy-Org’s repository, is 12.55 GB before the text encoder, and the license above still applies at home.
The third week was quieter. Builds master-1090 to master-1106 (November 12 to 16) let H3 Image reuse P-H3-Image’s smaller text encoder, added prompts that mix several images for vision models, sped up MOSS-Image on NVIDIA cards, and moved the project to ggml 1.0. Unsloth posted H3 Image in GGUF on November 12, a second source of repackaged files under the same H3 Image license. The project published no new build between November 16 and November 19.
Ollama v0.40.16 (November 9) added Ripple Preview, the 65B Apache 2.0 model from the first week: 39 GB at 4-bit, 46 GB in the Apple silicon build, and 130 GB or more at full precision. The same release cut Qwen3.8 Flash Next’s memory use on Apple silicon by about 27 GiB and added CUDA 13.5 for Windows on ARM. There has been no stable release since; v0.40.17 was still a release candidate on November 19, and Inkling 2 Small appeared in Ollama’s library as a cloud-only tag.
Ollama v0.40.15 added two families in the first week. Xiaomi’s MiMo V2.6 is a 9B model for code and agent work under MIT, 6.6 GB at 4-bit; its weights date from September, so the news is that it now runs with one command. AutoTrust’s JEV decision models come at 9B (11 GB), 27B with vision (21 GB) and 35B (26 GB) under Apache 2.0, on Apple silicon only in this release. Perplexity’s pplx-decider-flash, a 4B decision model from October, also arrived on Ollama as MLX tags from 4.6 to 9.1 GB, so Apple silicon only too.
llama.cpp’s v0.9.0 (November 4) added Chatterbox speech, NVIDIA’s Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe and the Mistral Large 4 architecture, and later builds added Ripple and Fish Audio’s Drama 3. There has been no numbered release since, but the rolling builds added Kimi K3.1 Pro’s text model on November 6 and its vision side on November 10, audio input for Gemma 4, and a speech endpoint in the built-in server. Builds from November 15 to 17 added ZONOS3-Mini and Fish Audio’s S2 for speech, Higgs v3 and VibeVoice for transcription, MiniMax-M3.1, and the chat format Inkling 2 Small needs. Builds from November 17 to 19 added Kokoro speech, voice cloning from a reference clip for Qwen3-TTS, VoxCPM2, and Zyphra’s Zaya1 8B. Ollama v0.40.17 added inkling-2-mini at about 20 GB, on Apple silicon only for now, with PC support promised in a later release. Unsloth posted GGUF files on launch day. llama.cpp shipped v1.1.0 on November 26 with the Inkling 2 family, Raon-1 mini, Hy4 flash, Zaya1 8B and Domyn Edge 4B, plus the speech models its rolling builds had collected. stable-diffusion.cpp resumed on November 22 after six quiet days: builds master-1111 to master-1133 added edit LoRAs for MOSS-Image, a small language for describing generation pipelines, and server options that load models on demand, unload idle ones and accept an API key.
