Etsy tells sellers not to use AI, but may train its own models on their content. Is there a way to opt out?
Can’t train AI on AI.

 Key takeaways
 01
 Etsy’s updated terms let it and some partners use seller content to train and improve AI systems.
 02
 Sellers cannot opt out in account settings, they must email Etsy’s data protection officer.
 03
 Some sellers have closed their Etsy shops after the policy change, though the total number is unknown.
 04
 Etsy also bans AI-generated listing images and descriptions as it tries to reduce fake listings.
Key Takeaways by nexos.ai, reviewed by Cybernews staff.
Etsy tells sellers not to use AI to generate listings. However, it may use their content to train its own AI, prompting some businesses to abandon the platform. 
“We closed our Etsy shop,” wrote War and Peas, a shop that sells prints, books, and apparel. 
While customers can still purchase items from its website, built by webcomic creators Jonathan Kunz and Elizabeth Pich, the items were previously available on Etsy. 
“You might have heard of Etsy's new terms and artists fleeing the platform. We're out, too,” wrote War and Pease on X.

Etsy to train AI with seller data
“Photos, descriptions, reviews. Everything sellers upload can now be used to train AI. By Etsy, by its 'partners and vendors,' a phrase roomy enough to fit pretty much anyone,” shared the post.
According to Etsy’s Terms of Use, the company, as well as its third-party partners and vendors, has the right to use sellers’ content to develop, train, test and improve artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies.” 
Since the updated Terms of Use will take effect on December 16th, users still have time to review the changes and decide whether to continue using the platform.

Etsy didn’t provide much information on how users can opt out of having their content used for AI training. 
After speaking with Etsy’s customer service representatives, Megan Lewis, who owns the educational platform Digital Hammurabi, learned that users can’t disable this through account settings.
Instead, they need to contact the company’s data protection officer via email and ask that their data not be used to train AI. 
“Hopefully, they’ll listen to us and actually do it,” shared Lewis.
Some users who have already contacted the company about the matter have said they had been advised to close their accounts if they disagreed with Etsy’s terms.
Many also agreed that closing their accounts was the best option. It is not yet known how many sellers have left Etsy because of these changes.

Etsy condemns seller’s use of AI
In October, Etsy announced that it’s changing how AI can be used on Etsy. The updated guidelines stipulated that users can no longer add AI-generated images, descriptions, and guidelines. 
The company said that it was “investing” in tools to help identify fake images and remove listings with fraudulent information. 
Etsy’s decision to combat fake listings with AI-generated information made them look like “the good guys,” noted War and Peas. 
However, the company’s upcoming changes regarding AI training suggest that it may be seeking to use human-created listings as fresh training data for its AI models.
“Can't train AI on AI, they know what they are doing, and it's disgusting,” wrote one user on X.
