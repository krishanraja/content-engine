80% of people want AI music labeled, says Deezer/Ipsos survey – as 37% of Americans now see AI as a ‘valuable tool’ for musicians, down from 54% in 2025

 November 17, 2026
By Mandy Dalugdug

Photo Credit: Blue Planet Studio/Shutterstock

Some 80% of people across eight countries agree that fully AI-generated music should be clearly labeled for listeners, and 80% say the same about AI-assisted music.

Continue to article...
That’s according to the second annual AI music study commissioned by Deezer and conducted by Ipsos, published on Tuesday (November 17).

The study also records a shift in attitudes toward AI in music among respondents in the United States.
Deezer says US respondents were “significantly more negative” in 2026 than in 2025 on all questions about general attitudes to AI in music.
Those who believe AI will play a significant role in music creation over the next 10 years dropped from 62% to 51%.
The share who agree AI is a valuable tool to help musicians, artists, and composers create music fell from 54% in 2025 to 37% in 2026, the largest drop of any of the general-attitude questions in the US.
Meanwhile, 69% of US respondents now believe the use of AI by musicians, artists, and composers might lead to a loss of creativity, up from 65%.
Among US respondents who had heard of AI, 21% named trust as one of their feelings toward the technology, down from 32% a year earlier.
The share of US respondents who say they have listened to fully AI-generated music also fell, from 52% to 45%, making the US the only one of the eight markets to record a decline.
In its summary of the US findings, Ipsos said that “perceptions have shifted from enthusiasm to caution versus last year.”
Ipsos surveyed 9,000 adults online in the US, Canada, Brazil, the UK, France, the Netherlands, Germany, and Japan between October 9 and October 14, with 1,000 respondents in each market apart from the US, where it polled 2,000 people. Of those, 6,672 were music streaming users.
The first edition of the study, published in November 2025, found that 97% of respondents couldn’t tell fully AI-generated music apart from human-made music in a blind test.
That edition found that 80% wanted fully AI-generated music clearly labeled. The question on labeling AI-assisted music is new this year.
Last year’s sample was limited to adults aged 18 to 65, while this year’s covers all adults aged 18 and over.

“It is clear from this year’s survey that an overwhelming majority still want transparency when it comes to AI music, and that there is a significant gap between what listeners think is in their playlists and what is actually there, which highlights the need for continued efforts to achieve greater awareness for music streaming users,” said Alexis Lanternier, CEO of Deezer.
“Concerns about AI having a negative impact on creativity and artistry are clearly growing, especially in the United States, where there is a striking difference in attitudes compared to last year. A majority of people in all surveyed countries also consider fully AI-generated music a threat to the livelihood of artists.
“Concerns about AI having a negative impact on creativity and artistry are clearly growing, especially in the United States, where there is a striking difference in attitudes compared to last year.”
Alexis Lanternier, Deezer
“In 2025, Deezer became the first streaming service to implement measures to increase transparency for fans and minimize AI-based fraud and revenue dilution. By gathering intelligence through this annual survey, and continuously listening to feedback from our users and the music ecosystem, we are better positioned to improve the fan experience, and protect artists and songwriters.
“The stakes are high, but we will continue to do our part in building a thriving music ecosystem, and minimize any negative effects of generative AI.”
According to Deezer, 74% of music streaming users in the survey believed their playlists contained no fully AI-generated music.
However, Deezer says that nearly 57% of the more than 53,000 people who joined its platform in October already had at least one fully AI-generated track in their playlists.
The 57% is drawn from Deezer’s internal data on new subscribers joining from other streaming platforms, not from the Ipsos survey sample.
Deezer added that it has tuned its AI-detection tool “to achieve maximum accuracy,” but that false positives may occur.
France registered the highest demand for labeling of the eight markets, with 88% of French respondents saying fully AI-generated music should always be clearly labeled.
Support for labeling AI-assisted music was even higher, at 90%.
French respondents were also the most likely to agree that the use of AI by musicians might lead to a loss of creativity, at 76%, up six points from 2025.
Ipsos described Brazil as “the most enthusiastic market toward AI.”
In Brazil, 61% of respondents agreed that AI is a valuable tool for music creation, against 35% across the full sample.
Brazilian respondents were also the most likely to say they had listened to fully AI-generated music at least once, at 63%, up from 46% in 2025.
At the other end of the scale, 46% of respondents in Japan said they had never listened to fully AI-generated music, the highest share of any market, though down six points year-over-year.
Across all respondents, the share was 32%, down seven points.

Across the full sample, 67% of respondents believe AI could lead to a loss of creativity in music production, up three points, according to Deezer.
On charts, 60% said fully AI-generated songs should not be included in the main charts alongside human-made songs, up eight points year-over-year.
Just 7% said fully AI-generated music should be treated the same as other tracks on the charts, down four points.
Among music streaming users, 72% want to know when a streaming service is recommending fully AI-generated music to them.
Asked about their favorite artist, 54% of respondents said it would be unacceptable for that artist to create a full track with little human involvement.
By contrast, 45% said it would be acceptable for the artist to use AI to produce, mix, or master tracks.
In all eight countries, the share of respondents who believe fully AI-generated music will become popular in the future fell by a statistically significant margin, according to Ipsos‘s market-by-market data.
The largest drop came in the US, from 60% to 41%, followed by Germany (46% to 33%) and the UK (49% to 37%).
The findings come as the RIAA and IFPI lead a push for streaming services to label AI-made tracks.
Their proposal, reported in July, sets out two tags: “AI-generated” for tracks made entirely by AI or with AI-produced lead vocals or main instrumental takes, and “AI-assisted” for tracks that are mostly human-made but use AI in places.
The proposal came a month after MBW‘s ‘Label The Slop’ op-ed, which argued that streaming services should mark AI content on the track itself, as they already do with explicit content.
Deezer says it is receiving nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks every day, accounting for more than 50% of its total daily deliveries on peak days.
The company first disclosed the 90,000 figure in July, up from the 50,000 a day it reported alongside the first edition of the survey.
Deezer says up to 85% of the streams generated by fully AI-generated tracks in 2025 were fraudulent, and that it excludes manipulated streams from royalty payments.
In October, the company announced an on/off toggle that will let users exclude fully AI-generated music from their search results, due before the end of 2026.
The 2025 edition of the survey found that 45% of music streaming users would like to filter out fully AI-generated music.
This year, 41% said the same, although Ipsos notes that results for the question are not comparable with 2025 because new answer options were added.Music Business Worldwide
