Generative AI on This Crown’s Cover

November 20, 2026
by
Ilona
Dear Readers,
When the cover of
This Crown Will Not Rule Me
was released, you expressed your concerns about possible AI-generated elements. We notified our publisher, asked for the proof of human-created art, and settled down to wait.
We have a no-AI clause in our contract. Tor also has a no-AI clause in their art contracts. Everyone wanted to be thorough.
Statement from Tor:
Tor Publishing Group has learned that generative AI was used in the creation of the cover art for
This Crown Will Not Rule Me
, without the knowledge of either Tor or of the authors.  As a result, we are taking down the recently revealed cover design and will not be using this imagery on any completed books.
Tor takes full responsibility for the breakdown in our review process. Beyond our existing policies and contractual provisions forbidding this kind of AI use, we will be bolstering our internal review processes to mitigate the possibility of this happening again. We know this situation has caused undue stress to our authors, Ilona Andrews, and we sincerely apologize to them for this error. To be clear, this is on us, not the authors.
We would also like to apologize to our readers. We pride ourselves on our longstanding standard of excellence, but clearly we fell short in this instance.
We look forward to sharing a new cover for
This Crown Will Not Rule Me
as soon as we can.
Thank you for caring enough to pay attention and taking the time to let us know. You were right.
We specifically would like to thank
Markova
and
Zakuga Art
, for their expertise and knowledge and their Instagram comments. Their feedback alerted us to this issue. We deeply appreciate their kindness and care.
That was the official statement. Here is a less official one.
We have a lot of feelings about this whole mess, just as some of you probably do.
Please don’t flame the artist on social media. We don’t know what was going on that person’s life or what led to this unfortunate incident. This situation is miserable enough already.
The generative AI has gotten very sophisticated. We commission a lot of art, and we are painfully aware that we lack expertise in this area. We ask for sketches, and we pay attention to whether or not the artist can do revisions. In this case, the cover was commissioned by our publisher, and a sketch was provided to the art department. It looked like a typical cover sketch. We didn’t know enough to see cause for concern.
We are so sorry.
We intend to do everything we can to make sure this doesn’t happen again. We may have to request project files for review. We will be doing that for all of our commissioned work going forward, and we are discussing this matter with our publisher. As their statement indicates, they are equally committed to getting this right.
We wanted to thank you again for letting us know. This would not have been caught without your help.
Statement for the BDH and the regular readers of this blog:
This has been a nightmare.
AI is everywhere. This is very new and very difficult to navigate. Creating anything in this new space is exhausting.
Earlier this year we signed a contract with a vertical drama company. We were under a very tight deadline, and this deal involved a company that we have worked with before. They made browser games in their previous incarnation. We specified no AI and asked that only human actors and writers would be used. The company was very upfront and agreed to our conditions.
We signed the contract, put out a press release, and realized that the level of generative AI involved in the company’s other productions did not align with our preferences. This is on us. We didn’t do our research. We have terminated that contract.
The company has been incredibly gracious and understanding. We are deeply grateful to them for how this was handled.
We didn’t want to post anything until the process had been concluded. We are mentioning it now to illustrate how new and confusing the creative landscape has become.
Going forward, we will have to vet everyone. Even the people we usually work with. This is so insulting to the artists, but we don’t know what else to do. We can no longer vet the artist. We have to vet every individual painting.
We can’t really talk too much about how all of this feels, because a lot of people read this blog, and we don’t want to inflame the situation.
You probably have questions. We have, too. We will let you know the answers as soon as we get them.

The concerns surrounding generative AI in art are principally ethical ones.
They include the use of artists’ work to train AI models without their consent or compensation, the subsequent displacement of working artists, matters of copyright and creative ownership, as well as wider ones of severe impact on the environment, resources and communities.
Ilona and Gordon are professional creatives themselves and want to ensure that the artists they commission are fairly compensated for their original work.
As they have publicly stated on previous occasions:
“Artists and writers work very hard, and most of us don’t earn enough. To see our opportunities shrink because of a language model that was trained on our stolen work is infuriating.
”
If I may, because my legal background demands it, the issue in this case is also a purely contractual one. Both the authors and their publisher explicitly prohibit generative AI in commissioned artwork.
