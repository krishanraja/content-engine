Prime Video introduces AI-powered Progressive Actions for Champions League
New Prime Insight tracks forward-moving passes and ball carries, debuting during Arsenal vs Borussia Dortmund on 24 November
Published
4 days ago on
November 23, 2026
By
indiantelevision.com Team
Follow us on Google News
Follow us on WhatsApp
Share
Tweet
MUMBAI: Football is often decided by the pass before the pass, and Prime Video wants to make sure those moves do not go unnoticed. The streaming platform has introduced Progressive Actions, an AI-powered feature for its UEFA Champions League coverage that tracks the passes and ball carries pushing teams towards the opposition goal, bringing the work behind an attacking move into the broadcast spotlight.
Developed by Prime Video’s producers, engineers, artificial intelligence specialists and on-air analysts, Progressive Actions is the first Prime Insight created specifically for the platform’s Champions League coverage. It focuses on players whose contributions may not always appear in traditional match statistics but can be crucial in moving a team forward and creating scoring opportunities.
The feature uses proprietary AI-powered models to identify progressive actions by measuring how far the ball travels during different phases of the pitch. The distance threshold changes according to the ball’s position, becoming shorter as play moves closer to the opposition goal. This accounts for the tighter defensive spaces and reduced room for advancement near the penalty area.
While similar data is used behind the scenes by some of Europe’s leading football clubs, presenting it clearly during a live broadcast has traditionally been difficult. Prime Video’s system aims to bring that analysis directly to viewers as the action unfolds.
Whenever a player completes a Progressive Action, an on-pitch graphic will identify the player, mark where the move began and ended, and display the distance the ball advanced. This allows viewers to follow the contribution in real time rather than relying solely on goals, assists or other conventional statistics.
The feature will also aggregate the data throughout a match, helping viewers identify which players have contributed most to moving the ball up the pitch. A midfielder who repeatedly breaks opposition lines with forward passes or a defender who initiates an attack through an incisive carry could therefore receive greater visibility, even if neither appears on the scoresheet.
Andrew Hornett, director of live sports production for Europe at Prime Video, said the feature was developed through Prime Vision, the platform’s initiative to bring lesser-known match metrics and player contributions into its broadcasts.
He said Progressive Actions was designed to highlight the players doing important work during the build-up, including those moving the ball forward two or three passes before an assist. The feature will measure and visualise these contributions during live coverage, while also compiling match-wide data to show which players have had the greatest influence on the game.
Progressive Actions will make its broadcast debut in the UK and Ireland on the evening of 24 November, when Arsenal take on Borussia Dortmund at the Emirates Stadium on Matchday 5 of the UEFA Champions League.
The feature will also be available to viewers in Germany and Italy. Fans watching any live Champions League fixture on Prime Video can access Prime Vision through their remote control from the match detail page or through the in-game menu.
Progressive Actions joins an expanding collection of analytical features within Prime Vision, which offers an alternate feed for Champions League coverage. The existing features include Tactical Map, Momentum, Physical Insights, Advanced Analytics, Passing Options and Player ID.
Together, these tools are intended to give viewers a closer look at the tactical and physical elements shaping a match, from player positioning and movement to passing choices and changes in momentum.
With Progressive Actions, Prime Video is adding another layer to its football coverage by shifting attention from the final shot to the build-up that makes it possible. For viewers, the next decisive contribution may not be the goal or the assist, but the pass that started it all.
