I've been looking at what's really inside the AI everyone's selling us. I'm Krish.

I've been having a little play around with Muse, Meta's new personal assistant. Meta owns Instagram and WhatsApp too. Reviewers keep comparing Muse with ChatGPT and Gemini. I did too, at first. But is that even the right fight?

The more I used it, the more that comparison felt like apples and oranges. This is a big shift in how AI comes into our personal lives. So I wanted to break down what Muse actually is, and what it changes for you.

Meta's pitch is simple. Muse is an agent that takes on more of the work for you. That way, you can focus on what matters to you. And people seem to like it. On Apple's US app store, it rates 4.9 out of 5.

To see why the ChatGPT comparison is off, you need one idea. There are now three kinds of AI. I'll take them one at a time.

The first kind is the chatbot. You ask, it answers. That's ChatGPT or Claude in your web browser. Gemini too. For most people, this is still what AI means.

The second kind is the work agent. An agent is an AI that does jobs for you, and keeps going without you. You hook it up to your email, your documents and your calendar at work. You give it a job. Then you leave it running. OpenAI has one called dots. Google has Gemini Spark, which keeps working in the background. Claude Cowork, from Anthropic, takes a job and keeps at it in the cloud. All three are built for people at a desk.

The third kind is the new kid. I call them ready-made assistants. They come ready inside the phone and apps you already have, and they run your errands. Meta says Muse can open a web browser, fill out forms, and even negotiate for you. It runs on its own computer in the cloud. Apple describes the new Siri getting things done across your apps. Think drafting an email from scratch, or editing a set of photos and sharing them. These are built for everyone else. That includes people who've never opened a chatbot in their life.

So the fair comparison for Muse isn't ChatGPT. It's Siri.

Why did the camps split like this?

Follow the money, and it all makes sense. Each company builds the kind of AI that makes it money.

Start with Meta. Last quarter it made $60.8 billion. Of that, $59.36 billion came from advertising. Ads pay best when everyone's there. And Meta owns the apps most of us open without thinking. So Meta builds an assistant for everyone, and makes it free for most of what people need.

Apple, as far as I can tell, just wants to sell you the next iPhone. A better Siri is a reason to upgrade.

OpenAI and Anthropic are different. There, you're the customer. The prices make that clear. The cheapest ChatGPT plan with dots is called Pro 100. It costs $100 a month. Claude Pro is $20 a month, if you pay monthly. People who pay that every month mostly do it for work. So that's who the work agents are built for.

Google sits in both camps. It sells ads, like Meta. But Gemini Spark needs a paid Google AI plan, and the cheapest one is $19.99 a month.

So I gave all five my own scores. Each one got a mark out of 10 on six things. Here they are. Doing the job. Price. Whether it's already in your life. Whether it asks before it acts. Who can read your stuff. And whose side it's on. The numbers are my opinion. The facts under them come from each company's own pages.

There's a picture of the full grid in the article. Here it is in words.

Muse and Siri tie, on 43 out of 60 each. But they get there in completely opposite ways.

Muse scores a 9 for doing the job. It gets a perfect 10 on price, and another 10 on reach. On asking first, it gets an 8. Meta says Muse checks with you before sensitive things, like sending an email or buying something. And when it pays, it uses a one-time card, so your real card details stay hidden. Then it drops to a 3 on privacy. And a 3 on whose side it's on.

Siri is almost the mirror image. It gets a 3 for doing the job. It gets 8 on price and 8 on reach. On asking first, a 7. Then it jumps to a 9 on privacy, and an 8 on whose side it's on.

The work agents score lower overall. That's mostly on price and on reach, because they were never built for that market. Claude Cowork comes third, on 39. It's the most careful of the lot, with a 9 on asking first. Out of the box, Anthropic says Claude asks before taking an action. ChatGPT's dots and Gemini Spark both land on 36. Dots gets a 2 on price, which is what $100 a month buys you.

Worth saying, though. The brains are roughly level. CNN's reviewer found ChatGPT and Gemini could handle similar tasks to Muse, and sometimes gave more detailed answers. Muse also suggested two date-night spots that had closed years ago. Romantic. So my scores are about what each one is allowed to do. How clever they are is a separate question.

Now, I know what you're thinking. Siri? Really? Siri is the assistant everyone loves to mock. Apple even admitted its smarter Siri wasn't ready, and that it would take longer than it thought. Then came a lawsuit over "false advertising".

So what's different now?

The brain, for a start. Back in January, Apple and Google both said Apple's next AI would be based on Google's Gemini. And the new Siri knows you. It can dig a hotel booking number out of an old email. A tester at the tech site SlashGear said that when it works, "it works exceptionally well". And it works more often than it doesn't.

Then there's reach. Tim Cook says Apple has 2.5 billion iPhones, Macs and other devices in use. Muse lives in its own app and in WhatsApp. Siri comes built into the phone. My guess is most people will try it by accident.

To be fair to Muse, the new Siri is still a test version. It has daily limits, and it isn't on iPhones in the EU yet. But it's no longer the punchline it was.

And here's where Muse falls behind. Muse runs on its own computer in the cloud. Meta says that's where your data and your logins are stored, for any service you connect. Those are Meta's servers. And Meta's own safety page says today's setup doesn't stop Meta getting to your data when it needs to. Meta says that's for running the service and keeping it secure. One more thing. Meta's setting to use your Muse chats to train its AI is switched on when you first use Muse. So for now, I give Muse a 3 on privacy.

Siri stands apart here. Apple says its AI runs on the phone itself. It also runs on servers using Private Cloud Compute (that's Apple's name for its private server setup). Apple says your data is not stored there, and isn't made available to Apple or anyone else. I give it a 9.

Here's the bit I think matters most. Muse can shop for you. My read is that every shop now has to decide whether to let it in.

Meta says it's adding shops like Walmart and Best Buy. Sephora too, among others. The aim is to make Muse a better shopper. Walmart had already signed up with OpenAI in October 2025, so customers could shop Walmart through ChatGPT. So Walmart, at least, is plugging into more than one assistant at once.

Amazon went the other way. Since 21 September, if you try to shop on Amazon through Muse, you get a popup. It says access by "an unauthorized AI agent" breaks Amazon's rules, which its customers have agreed to. My read is that Amazon wants you shopping in Amazon's own app.

Then think about your local restaurant. It can't set up with every AI company out there. My bet is it goes where its customers already message it, and that's WhatsApp. Meta has an AI helper for businesses there, called the Meta Business Agent. Meta says more than one million businesses already use it, on WhatsApp and Messenger. And since 1 August, Meta charges for it by how much it's used.

So my bet is that businesses split the same way the AI does. Big shops plug into every assistant they can. Small ones go where their customers already are. For most of us, that means Meta's apps.

Which one should you use, then?

If you just want answers, a chatbot is fine. If you live in documents and email at work, a work agent is built for you. And if you want errands done from your phone, the ready-made assistants are the new choice. Pick Muse if you want the most done for free. But turn the training setting off, and give it small jobs you can undo. Pick Siri if privacy is the deal-breaker. Whichever you pick, ask it now and then what the other options were.

So here's my prediction. By the end of 2027, I bet a big airline signs a deal with Meta and Apple. Under that deal, only Muse and Siri can sell its tickets. Ask ChatGPT for that flight, and it won't show up. That's when the assistant you pick starts deciding who you can buy from. I'll be wrong if every big airline keeps selling through every assistant, the way Walmart does today. Check back then.
