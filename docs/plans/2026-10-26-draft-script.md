Disney asked a dying AI firm to go bust first. That way it could hire the team and leave the lawsuits behind. So why are Disney and Google both paying for the same app, and what does it mean for you?

I've been reading the court papers on Character.AI, the app where millions of people chat with made-up characters. Two giants have now paid for pieces of it, for very different reasons. So I wanted to break it down for you.

Let's start with Google. In 2024, Google reportedly paid two point seven billion dollars for the right to use Character.AI's tech. And it hired the founders and their research team along with it. So Google wanted the brains. It was racing to build the smartest AI, and these people were very good at it. But most of the staff stayed behind, and the app carried on.

Now, Disney wanted something else, and to see why, go back to last December. Disney agreed to put a billion dollars into OpenAI. And over two hundred Disney characters would star in Sora, OpenAI's video app. So Disney was renting its AI. Mickey, Marvel and Star Wars, all living on someone else's app.

Then in March, OpenAI shut Sora down. The deal died, and no money changed hands. Disney said it respected the decision, which is a very polite way to say, well, that's awkward.

So Disney changed its plan. In September, it hired Character.AI's boss as its first ever head of technology. And this month, the court papers show the rest of the plan. Disney has offered jobs to sixty-six of the staff, at sixty million dollars a year for three years. And it has bid twenty million dollars for the code, the safety tools and more.

Here's the clever bit. Disney asked for the whole deal to go through the bankruptcy court, and the papers even say why. Character.AI was running low on cash, and it faced lawsuits and regulators. Going through the court means Disney takes the people and the code, clean, while the lawsuits stay behind with Character.AI.

So who is left holding the shell? The people who used the app, for a start. Their chats are not part of Disney's deal, and they get nothing from Google or Disney. And then there are the people suing Character.AI, whose claims stay with a firm that is being sold for parts.

See, the money followed the people. Google paid billions for the brains, and Disney is paying millions for the team. Everyone else is left with the empty box.

Now, why should you care if you're not Disney? Because most businesses now run on someone else's AI. It might be your writing tool, your chatbot, or maybe your whole customer service desk. And this story shows the warning signs, years before the end.

It also shows where the big media firms are heading. Netflix bought an AI film start-up this year, and Warner Music bought an AI firm too. And now Disney is taking a whole team. They have all stopped renting, and started owning.

So my prediction is that three more giants follow them. Skydance, the new giant made from Paramount and Warner Bros, is the first. Then Universal Music, and then NBCUniversal. By the first of March, 2027, I reckon each will announce buying an AI firm or its team. How sure am I? Seventy-five percent.

Here's what I'd do with this info if I were you. Pick the AI tool your business leans on most, and ask four things about the firm that makes it. First, are its founders still there? Google took Character.AI's founders two years before the end. Second, is your tool the main thing that firm sells, or a side bet? Sora was a side bet, and it vanished. Third, is the firm running short of cash? That was the first warning in the court papers. And fourth, is it collecting lawsuits? That's why Disney went through the court. If two of those answers worry you, line up a plan B now, while you still have time.
