A town in Ohio, Piqua, sacked its mayor over a Meta data centre. A big building full of computers that runs AI. Sacked, over a building. Pay for AI at work? One town's fight could hit your bill. Where does AI get built when towns say no? Let's find out.

Meta wanted a giant data centre, and the mayor said yes. A data centre like that uses as much electricity as a small city. Picture living in Piqua and opening your power bill to find you're chipping in for its power lines. Then Piqua voted. Ban big data centres? Yes, two to one. Sack the mayor? Yes, by a landslide.

But Meta's data centre was already being built. So Piqua now has a data centre going up in a town that just banned them. Meta says it'll work with local officials. Which is a polite way of saying, we'll get back to you.

When towns say no, the AI companies have to find that power somewhere else, and pay for it. And I reckon that cost lands in your subscription.

Next time I renew an AI tool, I'm asking where its new data centres are being built. Because if it's a town like Piqua, that fight ends up on my bill.
