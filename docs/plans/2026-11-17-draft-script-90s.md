Piqua, in Ohio, sacked its mayor for saying yes to a Meta data centre. That's a big building full of computers that runs AI. If you pay for AI at work, could this push up your bill? Where does AI get built when towns say no? Let's find out.

Meta wanted a giant data centre, and the mayor said yes. A data centre like that uses as much electricity as a small city. Picture living in Piqua and opening your power bill to find you're chipping in for its power lines. Then Piqua voted. Ban big data centres? Yes, two to one. Sack the mayor? Yes, by a landslide.

Meta's data centre was already being built, so it's still going up. Meta says it'll work with local officials. Which is a polite way of saying, we'll get back to you.

So the bigger question is who pays for the power. Towns like Piqua are now making the AI companies pay for it themselves. So I reckon they pass that cost on, into your subscription.
