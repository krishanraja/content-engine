Piqua, in Ohio, just sacked its mayor for saying yes to a Meta data centre. Over a shed full of computers. If you sign off the AI bill at work, that should worry you. So let's find out. Where does AI get built when towns say no?

Meta wanted a giant data centre on the edge of town, and the mayor said yes. Then the neighbours did the maths. A shed like that uses as much electricity as a small city. And they worried they'd be paying for it on their own bills. On election day, Piqua got a ballot paper with two questions. Ban big data centres? Yes, two to one. Sack the mayor? Yes, and by even more.

And here's the twist. Meta's shed is still going up, in a town that has just banned it. I'd love to be at that council meeting.

So I reckon where AI gets built is the wrong question. The real one is how many towns say yes, because the next, smarter AI still needs a giant shed. Fewer yeses, and I reckon the new features in your tools arrive later, and cost more. Next time we renew an AI tool, I'm asking our supplier where its new computing is being built.
