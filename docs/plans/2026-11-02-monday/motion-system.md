# Monday 2 November, follow.the.money: the motion system, "The Ladder"

Piece `9a0c97af-f0d6-4d12-a7ce-773c622d4b67`. One system for the whole piece, decided before the storyboard
(learning `visuals-add-not-repeat`). Style: script `fry-explainer`, production `fry-talking-head`, experiment
`data-story` (one number per frame, from his note on the chart: "The chart makes a boring research report instantly
understandable").

## The object

One career ladder, drawn flat in cream line on the ink desk, the brand's grain behind it. Each rung is a stage of a
working life, bottom to top. The bottom rung is the junior rung. Every number in the piece is drawn on, beside or
instead of a rung, so the viewer always knows which part of a career a number is about.

- **Numbers.** Anton, cream, one number per frame. The number that matters most in a frame takes follow.the.money's
  butter as its underline only (the channel colour marks identity, never a warning). Mint marks only the answer: the
  two questions and the actions.
- **Proof.** Every number carries its producer in IBM Plex Mono at the foot of the frame (the floor: numbers credited
  to their producer): ANTHROPIC · OCT 2026, IMF · BESSEN 2015, NPR PLANET MONEY · 2015, HOSSEINI & LICHTINGER · 2025.
  Real logos (Anthropic, Microsoft Excel, IMF, NPR) appear once, at the size of a rung, never redrawn.
- **Pages.** The Anthropic report is shown as itself: its cover, then the one line he paraphrases, highlighted, with a
  slow push.

## The moves (each one means something)

1. **The drop.** "27% down to 9%": the bottom rung's number counts down from 27 to 9 in one second, and the rung thins
   to a hairline as it does. This is the piece's big moment (the one sound, if he has approved a set by then).
2. **The agents.** "far more hours than its people": a bar of agent hours grows beside a bar of staff hours to six
   times its height (Anthropic: "6 to 1" by August).
3. **The past, twice.** The ladder steps aside for two short scenes on the same desk. Tellers: a branch of 20 teller
   marks loses 7 (20 to 13), then the branch copies itself until there are 43% more. Spreadsheets: 400,000 clerk marks
   fall away as 600,000 accountant marks stack up above them. Each scene ends by turning back into a ladder that is
   taller than before ("the job that was left got bigger").
4. **The missing rung.** "This time": the ladder returns, taller, with no bottom rung. The camera rests on the gap.
   This is the frame the cover and thumbnail echo.
5. **The quiet cut.** "people who never get hired": figures that would have stepped onto the bottom rung never appear;
   their outlines are dashed and fade before they are drawn. No redundancy letter is drawn.
6. **2030.** "your 2030 seniors are juniors somewhere today": the top rung lights mint with 2030 on it, and a line runs
   from it down the ladder to the gap where the junior rung was.
7. **His story.** The 5-minute cut only: a spreadsheet of 500,000 rows scrolls by itself while he talks; on "Excel
   macros" one recorded action repeats down the rows; on "three weeks of work became a few hours" a three-week calendar
   collapses to a few hours. The Excel logo sits on the sheet's title bar once.
8. **The close.** The two questions arrive as two mint cards on the empty rung, one on each question. Then the actions,
   one card each, then the single house end card. One opening (the hook, on his face), one close.

## What it never does

- No figures or icons acting out a sentence; the ladder only ever shows a number or the gap.
- No on-screen words that repeat what he is saying: a card adds the number, the producer, the date or the changed word.
- No eyebrow labels, asterisks, "our read" tags or AI notes.
- Never his name. His face carries the hook, the "Here's what worries me" turn, his story and the questions.
