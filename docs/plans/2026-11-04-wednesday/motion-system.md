# Wednesday 4 November, Claude Duet: the motion system, "The Queue"

One system for the whole piece, decided before the storyboard (learning `visuals-add-not-repeat`).

## The idea it carries

The piece's one point: with Duet your job moves from asking to approving. So every drawn picture is the same object, a
queue on the ink desk. Work arrives in it already prepared, slides along a rail and stops at a lilac line (the
under.the.hood colour, used only as the series mark). Beside each item waits an empty YES pill. When the yes is given,
the pill slaps on in mint (the answer colour). Nothing is ever sent past the line without that pill.

## Its parts

- **The rail and the line.** A thin cream rail across the desk, a lilac stop line near the right edge. Items slide in on
  the brand's slide (12 frames, hero easing) and halt at the line.
- **Items.** Cards in ink-soft with a cream title in Archivo: a draft reply, a meeting prep, a note, a payment, a
  signature, a delete. Real app logos (Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, Microsoft 365) sit on the cards they come from, at
  equal size, never redrawn.
- **The YES pill.** Empty outline until the words say yes, then mint fill, slapped on in 5 frames. A NO pill beside it
  stays outline.
- **The phone.** Duet's own screen, from the 29 October screen recording, sits in a plain phone frame on the desk and
  pushes in slowly to the line that proves the words. The recording is silent and plays under his voice, time-lapsed
  where the beat is shorter than the moment.
- **Redaction bars.** Anything private on his screen (his name, the K avatar, the first task's two words, his ventures,
  people, places, providers, amounts) sits under ink bars cut like paper, with grain, so they read as part of the look.
  Never a blur that leaves letters guessable.
- **Ask once, tool for good.** The one mechanism picture: a chat bubble folds into a tool tile, and the tile is used
  again three times while the bubble never returns.
- **Numbers.** One number per frame in Anton on the desk (36 HOURS), counting up on its word.

## What it never does

No icons acting out an idea, no clip-art phones, no arrows that restart, no loop on no cue, no eyebrow labels, no source
footers on cards (sources live in the direction and the article). One opening (the hook on his face with the wordmark
and the under.the.hood tag in the corners from the first frame) and one close (the makeyourmindup.ai card).
