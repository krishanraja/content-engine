# Wednesday 4 November, Claude Duet: plates for Codex to make

The passports precedent (`precedent/passports-P-f1.jpg`): one physical object per piece, made of real materials (paper,
guilloche, print), that carries the piece's idea and animates. For Wednesday the object is **The Queue**. The mocks in
`queue/` are drawn by Claude in HTML to fix the idea, the layout and the timing; Codex makes the finished plates.

| Plate | Its point | Materials | Size | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dispatch rail | Work arrives on its own and moves towards you | brushed steel rail with ticks, steel clips, soft contact shadow on the ink desk | 3840x400, tiling | the rail ticks past; clips swing as orders hang on them |
| Work order (x4: Send a message, Make a payment, Sign a document, Delete anything) | Each is a real action Duet prepares and stops before (Anthropic's own four) | heavy cream paper (`assets/plates/two-passports/paper.jpg`), a guilloche band (`guilloche.svg`), Archivo title, Plex Mono order number, an outlined YES and NO; the app's real logo where there is one (Gmail on Send) | 660x840 each, transparent | slides in on its verb, sways on its clip, stops at the line |
| The lilac line | Nothing crosses without your yes | a lit lilac bar with a soft glow, "WAITS FOR YOUR YES" in Plex Mono | 20x1120 | glows a little brighter as an order reaches it |
| The YES stamp | Your job becomes yes or no | mint rubber-stamp impression, uneven ink, a slight overprint and splash | 360x180, transparent | slaps on in five frames, scale 1.6 to 1.0, rotation -8 to -4 degrees, an ink bloom that fades in half a second |
| The phone in his hand | His own Duet, shown as it is | the approved hand-held plates (`assets/plates/hand-held-screen`, approved 28 Oct): phone bezel, glass, quad | 941x1672 at 2x | the recording plays inside; the hand drifts and tilts; ink bars ride with the screen |
| The two boxes (company, personal plan) | Work email moved to a personal plan leaves the company's control | a steel company cash box and a cardboard personal box, a paper email card, a padlock | 1600x900 | the card slides across on "connect"; the lid snaps shut and the lock drops on "doesn't control" |

No figure or icon acts out an idea; every plate is an object the viewer can name. Generated material is never shown as
evidence and carries no ILLUSTRATION tag on screen (Krish, 28 Oct).
