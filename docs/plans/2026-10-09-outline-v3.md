Spine: If how hard your AI thinks matters more than its name, who will end up deciding how hard it thinks, and what does that mean for you?
Answer: The AI itself: thinking really does change the answer, the labs sell names while every extra thought costs them money on a flat-fee plan, so the decision is moving to software that picks for you, which helps the many who never touch a setting and costs anyone who can't see what it picked.

1. THE NAME GAME | proves: the labs keep the spotlight on new names, because a launch buys attention, users and money, so the setting that decides your answer gets chosen out of sight
2. THE SWITCH THAT MATTERS | proves: on the same model, more thinking gives better answers on hard questions, with limits (each extra thought helps less, and easy questions can be overthought)
3. WHAT THINKING COSTS THEM | proves: every extra thought is a cost on your flat-fee plan and a sale on the developer meter, which is why the fast setting is the one you start on
4. WHO DECIDES NEXT | proves: the choice is moving to the AI itself, even though OpenAI handed it back to paying users in September (the prediction)
5. THE TRADE-OFFS | proves: an AI that decides helps people who never change settings and wastes less, but hides what you got and lets the company's costs steer your answer
6. WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT | proves: until you can see the gear, pick it yourself for anything you will act on
