I got early access to Claude Duet, and the first thing that struck me is a really weird thing to say about an app. It knows what it knows about me.

So what does it actually change if you already use Claude at work? Let me break it down.

Duet is Anthropic's new Claude app, and it messages you first. Every few hours it checks your email, your calendar, Slack and whatever else you've connected. Each morning it sends you a short brief. And while you're off doing other things, it drafts replies, meeting prep and documents for you.

Then it stops. Before it sends a message, pays for something, signs a document or deletes anything, it shows you exactly what it's about to do and waits for your yes.

The setup is really seamless. Most apps ask you ten questions to customise your experience. Duet skips all that. Anthropic says it carries over your memory, your skills and your connected apps from your Claude account. I've lived my life in this Claude account for a while and I've got everything connected, so it pretty much walked in already knowing me.

There is one catch. It's tied to one email account. If you're anyone over the age of 15, you've probably got a few email accounts, and they're all a bit of a jumble. The one I connected is full of bills and not really my work stuff. And if your work email runs on Microsoft, Duet can't connect to it yet. So I'd basically need two Duets.

So what did it actually do? It pulled a bunch of tasks for me. The first one was a job search, which was strange. So I said, get rid of that one and go and find ten tasks for my to-do list. And it came back with things I know I need to do, but I'd probably only do once they're on fire. Lots of life admin. Bills to clear, money admin, that kind of thing.

It's slow, though. In classic Claude style it loves a good long think, like me. It's clearly built for you to leave and come back, because nothing happens in an instant with this app.

Here's the bit I think matters most. I clicked on the calendar scheduler. And it went away and built me what Claude calls an artifact. Basically a little dashboard, custom to me, that I can send to people. I asked for a one-off favour and I got a tool back. I didn't even know I was making a dashboard, but lo and behold, I've got a dashboard.

If you're a calendar-scheduling business or a note-taking business, I'd be worried. Because that's the hint about where software is going. You ask once, and it builds you a system you can use again and again.

So here's what that changes if you already live in Claude. Today, you open Claude and paste in the email thread, the invite and your notes. Then you ask. With Duet, the work turns up first, and your job becomes saying yes or no.

One small business owner on Reddit put it best: "I'm not doing anything that I don't think a normal version of Claude could do. I just hadn't thought to use it this way." They'd fallen behind on their work, and once Duet started bringing it to them, they caught up in a day and a half.

But here's the catch. A habit only sticks if something keeps pulling you back. Claire Vo's episode on Lenny's Newsletter is literally called "Claude Duet is beautiful. I still forgot to use it." And that's my read too. It's picking up things that really do need doing, but they'd only get done if someone nudged me. So it's only useful if it keeps doing the nudging.

Two more things. All that checking in the background counts toward your Claude plan's usage limit, so you can hit it sooner. And right now Duet is only for Pro and Max plans in the US, through a waitlist. If your company pays for your Claude, you don't get it yet.

So here's my question for you this week. Of the jobs in your week, which one only gets done when someone nudges you? That's the one to hand over first.

And if you run IT, a quick word. People will want to connect their work email to a personal Claude plan before your company gets Duet. That puts company email in an account your company doesn't control. So decide now whether that's allowed, before they ask.

Here's what I'd do with this info if I were you. Pick that one job you only do when it's on fire. Make sure the information it needs sits in apps Duet can reach, like your email, calendar or Drive. When you get Duet, let it draft for a week and check every draft before you say yes. Then look in your Claude settings to see how much of your limit it used.
