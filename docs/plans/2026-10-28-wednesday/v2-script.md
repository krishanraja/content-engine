I got early access to Claude Duet, and the first thing that struck me is a really weird thing to say about an app. It knows what it knows about me.

So if you already use Claude at work, what does Duet actually change for you? Let me break it down.

Duet is Anthropic's new Claude app, and it messages you first. Every few hours it checks your email, your calendar, Slack and whatever else you've connected. Each morning it sends you a short brief. And while you're off doing other things, it drafts replies, meeting prep and documents for you.

Then it stops. Before it sends a message, pays for something, signs a document or deletes anything, it shows you exactly what it's about to do and waits for your yes. And a few things are off-limits even if you say yes, like trading shares or moving money between your accounts.

The setup is really seamless. Most apps ask you ten questions to customise your experience. Duet skips all that. Anthropic says it carries over your memory, your skills and your connected apps from your Claude account. I've lived my life in this Claude account for a while and I've got everything connected, so it pretty much walked in already knowing me. Obviously only certain businesses can do that, because you have to already be holding someone's life.

There is one catch. It's tied to one email account. If you're anyone over the age of 15, you've probably got a few email accounts, and they're all a bit of a jumble. The one I connected, I set up about three years ago, and it's full of bills and not really my work stuff. And if your work email runs on Microsoft, Duet can't connect to it yet. So it only reads from one silo. I'd basically need two Duets, and I'm interested to see if Anthropic ends up joining those up at some point.

So what did it actually do? It pulled a bunch of tasks for me. The first one was a job search, which was strange. I think it was trying to impress me with a "here's what I know about you" moment. So I said, get rid of that one and go and find ten tasks for my to-do list. And it came back with things I know I need to do, but I'd probably only do once they're on fire. Lots of life admin. Bills to clear, money admin, that kind of thing. Which makes sense, because it's on my personal account.

Now, speed. In classic Claude style it's very introspective. It loves a good long think, like me. People sometimes think I've stopped talking because I think mid-sentence, and now I know how annoying that is. Claire Vo from How I AI said the wait made her less willing to sit and work with it, and I get that. It's clearly built for you to leave and come back, because nothing happens in an instant with this app.

And there's a glitch or two. I pressed Start on a task and nothing seemed to happen. It had gone off into a separate task, but it didn't open it in front of me. That's the kind of thing they'll fix pretty quickly. It's an early preview.

Here's the bit I think matters most. I clicked on the calendar scheduler. And it went away and built me what Claude calls an artifact. Basically a little dashboard, custom to me, that I can send to people. I asked for a one-off favour and I got a tool back. I didn't even know I was making a dashboard, but lo and behold, I've got a dashboard. The morning brief worked the same way: one page with all my meetings from all my calendars on it.

If you're a calendar-scheduling business or a note-taking business, I'd be worried. Because that's the hint about where software is going. Those apps that ask you ten questions are going to turn into this. You ask once, and it builds you a system you can get used to, find your way around and use again and again.

Dan Shipper at Every saw the same thing. He says it keeps a running list of what it's working on and what it needs from him, and keeps his docs, slides and dashboards up to date without leaving the app. Every called it the first personal agent they'd call a pro tool.

And honestly, I think artifact is a stupid, pompous word. But I get why they chose it. So here's what that changes if you already live in Claude. Today, you open Claude and paste in the email thread, the invite and your notes. Then you ask. With Duet, the work turns up first, and your job becomes saying yes or no.

Two more things before you rush off. All that checking in the background uses up your Claude plan's usage limit. Hit the limit, and your normal Claude stops too, until it resets. And right now Duet is only for Pro and Max plans in the US, through a waitlist. If your company pays for your Claude, you don't get it yet. Anthropic says it plans to bring it to those plans later.
