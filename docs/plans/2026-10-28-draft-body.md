Claude Duet is the first Claude that messages you before you message it.

Anthropic opened it on 21 October as a research preview, off a waitlist, for Pro and Max users in the US. Its own page says it "comes to you with what needs your attention, completes tasks you ask of it, keeps working even when you step away, and checks with you before it acts on your behalf." The reviews so far tell you whether it is any good. Most of them skip the question that matters if you already run half your working week through Claude.

So, one question. What does Claude Duet actually change for someone who already works in Claude?

## WHAT DUET DOES ON ITS OWN

Duet runs on its own computer in the cloud, with its own browser, so your laptop can be shut. Every few hours it checks the apps you have connected: Gmail, Slack, your calendar, HubSpot, or any of the "over 6,000 connectors and plugins" Anthropic counts. Each morning it sends you a short brief on what matters. In between, it reads, drafts and prepares work: replies, documents, decks, follow-ups.

Then it stops. Before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document or deletes anything, it shows you exactly what it is about to do and waits for your yes. A few things stay off the table even with your yes, such as trading securities or moving money between accounts.

For a Claude user, the interesting part is what comes along for the ride. Anthropic says Duet "carries over your memory, skills, and connected apps from your Claude account", and it can search your past chats so you don't have to repeat yourself. It already knows who you are, which saves an awkward first date.

## WHAT PEOPLE THINK OF DUET

In its first week, the mood is curious and mostly warm about the idea and the design, and sceptical about cost, speed and access. Most people talking about it haven't got in yet. On Reddit the waitlist is the single biggest topic, closely followed by the US-only rule and one plaintive "You know, us Teams users need love, too :)".

The people who have used it agree it is polished. Dan Shipper at Every calls it "the first one we'd call a pro tool, even with the plumbing still unfinished". Claire Vo of How I AI ran it on an inbox, Slack, a sales pipeline and a sales deck, liked the design a lot, and still titled the episode "Claude Duet is beautiful. I still forgot to use it". Others found it slow: one Reddit user says voice requests end in "hang on" followed by a two-minute wait.

The sharpest worry is your plan limits. Duet's background checks come out of the same allowance as the rest of your Claude work, and Anthropic says so. One Reddit user reports it "used 50% of my usage this morning without me using Claude or it doing anything". That is one report and nobody has checked it, but anyone who already runs out of Claude on heavy days will feel it.

## FROM ASKING TO APPROVING

Here is what neither the launch page nor the reviews say outright. For someone who already lives in Claude, Duet adds few new abilities. What it changes is who starts the work.

Today you open Claude, paste in the email thread, the calendar invite and the notes from your sales system, and ask. With Duet, the work turns up first: a brief in the morning, a running list of "what it's working on and what it needs from me", as Shipper puts it, and drafts waiting for a yes or a no. Your job moves from asking good questions to giving good answers (which, if you manage people, will sound familiar).

A small-business owner on Reddit, u/freakyslug, put it best: "I'm not doing anything that I don't think a normal version of Claude could do. I just hadn't thought to use it this way." They also say they caught up on a backlog in 36 hours.

The catch is that a habit only forms around something that keeps coming back. Vo's test is the warning: after the first few tasks, nothing "would pull me back in". My read: Duet earns its place only when you give it a job that comes round every week.

## WHAT COMES WITH YOU AND WHAT STAYS BEHIND

Power users have built a lot around Claude, and not all of it makes the trip.

What comes: your memory, the skills and connectors on your account, and the apps you connect. The sales pipeline in Vo's test is the good example. Duet already had Slack, email and the CRM, so there was no need "to assemble and paste all the inputs myself".

What stays behind: files on your computer, and some of your Claude projects. Duet can't reach either. Some skills that lived on Vo's own computer didn't come across. Shipper found that its scheduled runs can't reach custom connectors, and that it can see the Claude sessions on Shipper's computers but can't direct them. Brian Govanlu asked it to work with the Claude Code sessions on a laptop; it couldn't, so it filed a feature request with Anthropic instead, which is at least very polite. Microsoft 365 isn't connected yet, and Slack has limits.

And if your company pays for Claude Team or Enterprise, you don't get it yet. Anthropic says it plans to bring Duet to those plans later.

## WHERE TO HOOK IT IN FIRST

So start with one weekly job whose inputs already live in the apps Duet can reach, and that you now do by copying and pasting into Claude. Three good places to start:

- Prep for a client meeting: the calendar invite, the last email thread and the latest proposal, pulled into one brief with what you need to settle before the call.
- A weekly pipeline review: your CRM, inbox and Slack, turned into a short note on which deals moved and which went quiet.
- Your follow-ups: the threads waiting on you, drafted and lined up for your yes.

Keep it read-only for the first week. Let it prepare and report, and do the sending yourself. Run it beside the way you do it now, and compare what it caught with what it missed. Then look at your usage at the end of the week, because the same allowance pays for everything else you do in Claude. On a Team or Enterprise account, this is the job to have ready for when it arrives.

## OUR PREDICTION

So my prediction is that by 1 March 2027, Duet will be able to start and steer your Claude Code and Cowork work, as well as see it. Today it can see your Claude sessions but can't direct them, and early users are already asking for exactly that. When it can, Duet becomes the place a Claude power user starts the working day. I'd put it at 55%.

## 3 things you can do tomorrow

1. Write down the one Claude job you do every week by copying and pasting from your email, calendar, CRM or Slack. That is your first Duet job.
2. Check where that job's inputs live. If any of it sits in files on your computer or behind a custom connector, move it into a connected app, or pick a different first job.
3. Note how much of your Claude allowance you use in a normal week now, so you can see what Duet costs you once you're in.
