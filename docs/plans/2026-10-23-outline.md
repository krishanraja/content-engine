Spine: Why does every country want its own AI, and what does that mean for the AI your business uses?
Answer: Because where an AI was built shapes what it tells you and where it runs decides who sees your data, and the same two checks tell you what your business is relying on.

Reframed on 24 October after Krish: "The whole point of this is to understand what sovereign AI means and why countries are fighting to build their own AI as opposed to use another countries. We must not make this feel like we're stirring geopolitical hatred against China, it's just an example." Every country's example is treated the same way; the test is what one model did on one question, never a verdict on a country.

1. EVERY COUNTRY WANTS ITS OWN AI | proves: what sovereign AI means, and that America and China lead (59 against 35 notable models; 2.7% apart at the top) while Britain, France, the Gulf, India and Korea build or fund their own
2. THE AI YOU USE ALREADY CROSSES BORDERS | proves: firms pick AI from other countries for speed and price (Airbnb and Qwen, Cursor and Kimi, Lindy and DeepSeek, in their own words; HSBC and France's Mistral)
3. WHERE IT WAS BUILT SHAPES WHAT IT SAYS | proves: the newspaper's test of Chinese chatbots (35%; DeepSeek 63%; Claude answered all) and Grok checking Elon Musk's posts: every maker's choices travel with its AI
4. WHERE IT RUNS DECIDES WHO SEES YOUR DATA | proves: DeepSeek's own app stores what you type in China; HSBC runs Mistral on its own systems; running a model yourself keeps your data home but keeps what it was trained to say (the US government's test)
5. I TRIED IT ON WORK QUESTIONS | proves: on a cotton-supplier question DeepSeek wrote an answer and deleted it in 3 of 3 runs (and again on camera on 24 October), while on the Taiwan briefing all three behaved alike
6. MY CALL: KNOW WHICH PASSPORTS YOU HOLD | proves: any country's AI can be the right tool when you know both passports, and (my view, labelled) tariffs could reach AI the way they reached supply chains, so keep a second option

Then the closing section, "3 things you can do tomorrow": ask your main software suppliers which AI their product runs
on and where it runs; put your own work questions to your team's AI and to one from another country, and see where they
differ; keep a second AI ready so you're never stuck with one supplier.
