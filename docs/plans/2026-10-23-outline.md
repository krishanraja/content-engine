Spine: Does it matter which country your AI comes from?
Answer: Yes: where an AI was born shapes what it tells you and where it lives decides who sees your data, so check both before your business relies on one.

1. YOU MAY ALREADY BE USING ONE | proves: Airbnb, Cursor and Lindy chose Chinese AI for speed and price, in their own words
2. TWO COUNTRIES LEAD, AND EVERYONE ELSE WANTS ITS OWN | proves: America and China lead (59 against 35 notable models; 2.7% apart at the top), and Britain, France, the Gulf, India and Korea are building sovereign AI
3. WHERE IT WAS BORN SHAPES WHAT IT SAYS | proves: the Monitor's test (35% refused, gave Beijing's line or got it wrong; DeepSeek 63%; Claude answered all), with Grok checking Elon Musk's posts as the American case
4. WHERE IT LIVES DECIDES WHO SEES YOUR DATA | proves: DeepSeek's app stores what you type in China; HSBC runs Mistral on its own systems; running a Chinese model yourself keeps your data home but keeps what it was trained to say (NIST)
5. I TRIED IT ON WORK QUESTIONS | proves: on Xinjiang cotton DeepSeek wrote an answer and deleted it in 3 of 3 runs (and again on camera on 24 October), while the Taiwan briefing was a draw
6. MY CALL: KNOW WHICH PASSPORTS YOU HOLD | proves: a Chinese AI is fine for a clever job done cheaply if you know both passports, and (my view, labelled) tariffs could reach AI the way they reached supply chains

Then the closing section, "3 things you can do tomorrow": ask your main software suppliers which AI their product runs
on and where it runs; put your own work questions to your team's AI and to one from another country, and see where they
differ; keep a second AI ready so you're never stuck with one supplier.
