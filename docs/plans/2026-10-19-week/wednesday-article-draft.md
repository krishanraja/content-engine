# Can Meta’s Muse or Grok Bot run my life? I tested both

I put Grok Bot and Meta's Muse head to head, two of the new done-for-you AI assistants. Flights, emails, the day plan, the things I keep meaning to cancel. The sort of jobs that make an assistant sound very attractive at half past ten on a Tuesday.

If you're a leader trying one on your own life, I get it. Your holiday search sits beside a work email. Your personal calendar holds half your job.

I ran five head-to-head tests. One assistant wrote a lovely reply to a scammer. So are they worth the hype? Let's dive in.

Every round gets two scores: mine, and the community's, meaning other people who have tested the same tools.

Both count equally in what I'd try next. Mine show one run on my accounts. The research adds other people and longer runs. Five anecdotes still aren't a clinical trial.

## The inbox

I asked each which emails needed a reply. Several asked to order six products and check payment security. None named a product.

Grok flagged them as likely scams and kept them out of my reply queue. Muse checked my refund policy and wrote a polished reply. Then it put that reply first on its day plan for me. Customer service, for people trying to rob me.

They were scams. Well crafted emails. The reply matched the shop's published policy. Making it my next job was the problem. Muse made the wrong job look beautifully finished.

The research is kinder to Muse. [AI for Mortals ran a fake coffee business](https://www.aiformortals.co/blog/grok-bot-vs-meta-muse-vs-chatgpt-dots) with scam traps, and neither Muse nor Grok fell for them. Reassuring for Muse. Quite flattering for my scammers. Its overall company grade was still a D.

Each got a separate copy of the business. That earns Muse another look. It doesn't make the reply in my draft folder a good idea. The trap still matters.

**My test: Grok. Community: level.**

## The flight

London to New York, five nights in November, cabin bag only.

Grok found a £335 return. Muse showed £328 through an agent, or £335 from the airline. The catch was twenty-nine hours in Lisbon. That's quite a lot of Portugal for a trip to New York.

Both steered me away. Grok offered a shorter stop. Muse suggested a direct flight and listed what it couldn't check. It promised four nights in New York. Its flight times left three hotel nights. Off by a night, on a job that is basically counting.

[The Montreal comparison](https://www.aiformortals.co/blog/grok-bot-vs-meta-muse-vs-chatgpt-dots) preferred Grok for speed and value. Muse worked around a blocked booking site through Expedia. Both completed the search; Grok reached the cheaper option first. That is one reviewer's journey, with different constraints from mine. It gives me a shortlist, rather than tomorrow's cheapest-ticket guarantee. I'd still count the nights myself. Apparently this now qualifies as specialist expertise.

**My test: Grok, on points. Community: Grok.**

## What to cancel

Both found a real recurring charge. Both warned me to check use or dependencies before cancelling. That's more thought than most of us give a gym membership.

I found no community test of this job for both tools.

The collection includes Muse subscription reports, without a matching Grok comparison or proof a cancellation completed. Giving research half the verdict means taking its gaps seriously too.

**My test: draw. Community: no result.**

## My day

Grok said my calendar was empty and built its suggested times around open follow-ups. Muse marked an invitation separately from confirmed plans. Both were useful.

The community adds a warning on Grok. [One operator running a small team of Grok bots](https://www.reddit.com/r/cursor/comments/1vxjipg/grok_bot_review/) reported forgotten instructions and duplicate messages, which also describes most group chats. A good afternoon doesn't prove it will run your week.

They reported expired approval cards and a shared work board losing progress. I haven't inspected those accounts. It is one report, with a more complicated setup than mine. My next test would leave it the same small job for a week and inspect what actually happened each day.

**My test: draw. Community: a caution on Grok.**

## The news

Grok's briefing was the more useful one. It also served up [an April article](https://www.emarketer.com/content/brands-about-spend-more-boosting-creator-content-than-creators-earn-making) as October news. Breaking news, six months late. Muse got the publication date right.

[Chris Alarcon's first week with Grok](https://christopheralarcon.com/blog/what-is-grok-bot) found the same strength: a regular news round-up earned a place in his routine. His lead-finding bot was a dud.

A recurring briefing has a clear output to inspect. Finding customers leaves more room to look busy. Alarcon also built a small analytics team around his channels. His account gives me a first job to try: recurring checks, with visible outputs I can inspect myself. It doesn't check my dates for me. Useful and accurate remain separate things to look for.

**My test: level, for different reasons. Grok for being useful, Muse for being right. Community: Grok for a repeatable briefing.**

## The work email

I gave both the same correction. Make it less formal. Don't promise a date.

Grok kept the right people copied in. It added a thank-you for "kind words", then flagged that for me to check. Generous, then honest. In that order. Muse detached its draft from the thread, then recreated it. It said it needed more access to delete the duplicate. So now I had two drafts. Progress. But it told me. I can fix a mistake I know about.

On another email, Grok asked me what fee to quote. It left the price to me. I'd quite like to keep that bit of my job.

## Where these get interesting

Muse found Messenger chats I'd left hanging and helped draft a reply. Grok found conversations on X worth joining and drafted one too.

If you are obsessed with X/Twitter, you might love Grok Bot. Likewise if your entire social circle is in WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook, Muse might be pretty helpful.

The community backs that up. [One user had Muse research windscreen wipers](https://www.reddit.com/r/singularity/comments/1wqu5wa/wait_metas_muse_agent_is_actually_amazing/), show the delivery cost and wait for a yes before buying. [Meta pitches it](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) turning saved Instagram recipes into a grocery list.

The wiper example has a clear moment to stop. The author reports that pause explicitly. Filling a form and showing delivery costs still don't prove a purchase completed. These are self-reports; Meta's examples are product claims. Errands look plausible. Sustained reliability stays open.

And Google's Gemini, in its chat app? It promised a cabin bag on that direct flight. The results it showed me didn't confirm it.

## The verdict

On my tests, Grok wins. It caught the scam Muse answered. That tips it for me.

Add the community's results and it gets closer. Muse is more careful than my inbox made it look. My afternoon with Grok went better than that operator's week.

So my call. For repeatable admin that creeps into work, start with Grok Bot. For personal errands, and a life already spread across Meta's apps, Muse deserves a look. Neither gets free rein.

I did a piece last week on this, and what price you pay for these conveniences: access to your data, as well as the bill. Deciding how much of your life to connect is still your job.

If you're a leader thinking these new shiny hypey tools are too good to be true, they're not. But they also are. Just make sure you or it says no at the right moment when things get hairy.

## 3 things you can do tomorrow

- **Hand one boring weekly job to one assistant.** Grok if the job touches work, Muse if it lives in WhatsApp, Instagram or Facebook. Just the one. You can promote it later.
- **Tell it to go as far as it can without spending money, deleting anything or affecting anything outside.** Then watch where it stops to check with you. That pause is the most useful thing it does.
- **Check the boring facts before you act on anything it hands you.** Dates, nights and who an email is really from. Those are exactly where they slipped in my tests.

*One initial run per job on my own accounts, 20 October 2026, with follow-ups recorded. Fares are what they found that day. Nothing was sent, booked, bought or cancelled.*
