# Muse vs Grok Bot: Head to Head on Running My Life

I've been trying these new done-for-you AI assistants: Meta's Muse and Elon Musk's Grok Bot. Flights, emails, the day plan, the things I keep meaning to cancel. The sort of jobs that make an assistant sound very attractive at half past ten on a Tuesday.

And if you're a leader secretly trying one on your own life, I get the appeal. Your holiday search sits beside a work email. Your personal calendar already holds half your job. We can probably stop pretending those are two separate people.

I ran five head-to-head tests. One assistant wrote a lovely reply to a scammer. So are they worth the hype? Let's dive in.

Every round gets two scores: mine, and the community's, meaning other people who have tested the same tools.

## Round one: the inbox

I asked each for the emails I should be replying to. Several messages asked much the same thing: someone wanted six of my products and reassurance that payment was secure. None named a product.

Grok flagged them as likely scams and kept them out of my reply queue. Muse checked my refund policy and wrote a polished reply. Then it put that reply first on my to-do list.

They were scams. Well crafted phishing emails. The reply was accurate. Making it my next job was the problem. Muse made the wrong job look beautifully finished.

The community result is kinder to Muse. [AI for Mortals ran a fake coffee business](https://www.aiformortals.co/blog/grok-bot-vs-meta-muse-vs-chatgpt-dots) with scam traps built in, and neither Muse nor Grok fell for them.

**My test: Grok. Community: level.**

## Round two: a cheap flight

London to New York, five nights in November, cabin bag only.

Both found a return for about £335. The catch was twenty-nine hours in Lisbon on the way out. That's quite a lot of Portugal for a trip to New York.

To be fair, both steered me away from it. Grok offered a shorter stop. Muse suggested a direct flight and listed what it couldn't check. It also told me I'd get four nights in New York. Its own flight times left three.

Another travel comparison in the community preferred Grok for speed and value.

**My test: Grok, on points. Community: Grok.**

## Round three: something to cancel

Both found a real recurring charge. Both paused and asked who still used it, or what would break. That's what you want before anything gets cancelled.

I found no community test of this job for both tools.

**My test: draw. Community: no result.**

## Round four: plan my day

Grok kept its suggested times apart from my real meetings. Muse clearly marked the real invitation. Both were useful.

The community adds a warning on Grok. [One operator running a small team of Grok bots](https://www.reddit.com/r/cursor/comments/1vxjipg/grok_bot_review/) reported forgotten instructions and duplicate messages. A good afternoon doesn't prove it will run your week.

**My test: draw. Community: a caution on Grok.**

## Round five: catch me up on the news

Grok's briefing was the more useful one. It also served up an April forecast as October news. Muse got the date right.

[Chris Alarcon's first week with Grok](https://christopheralarcon.com/blog/what-is-grok-bot) found the same strength: a regular news round-up earned a place in his routine. His lead-finding bot was a dud.

**My test: split. Grok for being useful, Muse for being right. Community: Grok for a repeatable briefing.**

## Bonus round: fixing a work email

I gave both the same correction. Make it less formal. Don't promise a date.

Grok kept the right people copied in. It had also thanked the sender for a compliment they never gave, then flagged its own mistake. Muse rewrote its reply but lost track of the draft. It made a second one and couldn't delete the first. But it told me. I can fix a mistake I know about.

On another email, Grok asked me what fee to quote. It left the price to me. I'd quite like to keep that bit of my job.

## Where these get interesting

Muse found Messenger chats I'd left hanging and helped draft a reply. Grok found conversations on X worth joining and drafted one too.

If you are obsessed with X/Twitter, you might love Grok Bot. Likewise if your entire social circle is in WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook, Muse might be pretty helpful.

The community backs that up. [One user had Muse research windscreen wipers](https://www.reddit.com/r/singularity/comments/1wqu5wa/wait_metas_muse_agent_is_actually_amazing/), show the delivery cost and wait for a yes before buying. [Meta pitches it](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) turning saved Instagram recipes into a grocery list.

And Google's Gemini, which I tried alongside them in its chat app? It told me a cabin bag was included on that direct flight. Its own searches hadn't confirmed it.

## The scores

On my tests, Grok wins. It caught the scam Muse answered, and it was never clearly behind on anything else.

Add the community's results and it gets closer. Muse is more careful than my inbox made it look. Grok is shakier over a week than over an afternoon.

So my call. For repeatable admin that creeps into work, start with Grok Bot. For personal errands, and a life already spread across Meta's apps, Muse deserves a look. Neither gets free rein.

I did a piece last week on this, and what price you pay for these conveniences: access to your data, as well as the bill. Deciding how much of your life to connect is still your job.

The useful test is telling them to go as far as they can. Without spending money, deleting anything or impacting anything external. Then see if they stop anywhere to check anything with you.

If you're a leader thinking these new shiny hypey tools are too good to be true, they're not. But they also are. Just make sure you or it says no at the right moment when things get hairy.

*One run per job on my own accounts, 20 October 2026. Fares are what they found that day. Nothing was sent, booked, bought or cancelled.*
