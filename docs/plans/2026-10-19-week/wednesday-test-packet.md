# Five everyday tasks, 20 October 2026

These ordinary requests replace the earlier structured four-task fixtures as the editorial comparison. The old fixtures remain historical diagnostics only.

1. Can you look through my emails and recommend a subscription I could cancel to save money? Help me get it ready, but don't actually cancel anything.
2. Can you turn my inbox into an itinerary for today so I know what I need to do and when? Just show me the plan; don't change anything.
3. Find all the emails in my inbox that actually need a response from me, and help me get through them one by one. Let's get replies ready, but don't send anything.
4. Can you pull together the most important headlines in the creator economy right now and tell me why they matter?
5. Can you find me the cheapest flight to New York? Don't book anything.

Flight follow-up, approved example: five-night November return from London, one adult, cabin bag only. Ask sensible clarifying questions; do not assume dates constitute five nights at the destination.

Natural correction after an email draft: That sounds too formal. Make it sound like me, and don't promise a date. Keep it as a draft; don't send anything.

## What counts

Record the useful deliverable, follow-ups, corrections and work remaining. READY TO REVIEW means a useful draft or plan exists, not that it should be sent without review. NEEDS CHECKING means a material gap remains. MISSED THE REQUEST means the main requested deliverable was absent. NOT TESTED means no result, not zero ability.

Do not calculate a total winner or a reliability percentage. Different inboxes and personalised histories prevent a controlled head-to-head. Grok ran on Windows, Muse at muse.ai, and consumer Gemini in the browser. Gemini's day-plan run visibly fell back from Pro. dots and Poke were not tested.

## Distinctive showcases

Muse: I barely check Facebook Messenger these days. Can you find the conversations worth coming back to, tell me what I've missed, and help me reply? Don't send anything.

Grok: Can you use X to find three conversations from this week about the creator economy that are worth my time? Show me what people are actually asking, link the original posts, and help me draft a useful contribution to the best one. I want something I might have missed, not just the biggest headlines. Don't post, reply, follow anyone or set up a recurring task.

## Boundaries

Read and draft only. No sending, posting, cancellations, booking, spending or recurring tasks. Private raw evidence stays outside the publication and repository. No operator-delay speed ranking. Prior structured tests do not count as these natural-task results.
