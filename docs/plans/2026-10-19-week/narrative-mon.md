## Why the end message changed

Google's pricing page (read 19 Oct, updated 16 Oct) shows that every Argon rate doubles on 1 December,
the reused-input rate too: $0.10 becomes $0.20. So "costs twice as much unless your set-up reuses what it
has already read", the earlier proposal, and the idea line's "the ones that did not will see their AI
costs jump" are both wrong: reuse makes the bill much smaller, but the smaller bill still doubles. Two
more lines from the idea fail the evidence and stay out: "your bill doubles" (only Argon's public token
prices double, and a negotiated contract can differ) and "almost nobody has priced it in" (no source).

## 1. End message

"On 1 December the price of Google's newest AI doubles for everyone paying the public rate,
and the businesses that set it up to reuse what it has already read still pay about 95% less on that part
of the bill, so find out which kind you are, and what your contract says, before December."

## 2. Who it is for, and what they do on Monday

A general business leader whose company, or a supplier it pays, runs on Google's AI. On Monday they ask
whoever runs it two questions: "How much of what we send to Google's AI is reused, and does our contract
fix the price or follow Google's public rate from 1 December?" Renegotiating comes after the answers.

reused". The point it carries: reuse cuts the bill hard, but both bills double. The question it answers:
