## PILLAR 1: more thinking gives better results, with numbers, and where it does not

The 2026 evidence strongly supports the core claim, with one important limit. Within a single model, the thinking setting moves results more than the most recent name change does. On Anthropic's own Opus 5.5 launch charts (22 Sep 2026), the same model goes from 38.5% to 66.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 between low and xhigh effort, and from 1224 to 1846 Elo on GDPval-AA between low and max. Artificial Analysis, testing independently on 15 Oct 2026, finds the same pattern on both sides. Opus 5.5 scores 42 at low and 58 at max, for about 11x the cost and 13x the tokens, against a 7-point gain from Opus 5 to Opus 5.5. GPT-6 Sol, which powers ChatGPT's slider, scores 29 with reasoning off and 48 at max, against a 4-point gain for the newer GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI's own 2025 developer page says higher effort 'maximize[s] quality' but helps unevenly: little on simple retrieval, several points on visual reasoning. The research roots are o1's 2024 claim that performance 'consistently improves' with more thinking time, and Snell et al. 2024, where test-time compute let a model beat one 14 times larger. The counter-evidence is real and current. The returns flatten or reverse at the top: Anthropic's FrontierCode chart is flat from medium to max at nearly 8x the cost, and Hex DataBench (7 Oct 2026) shows GPT-6 Sol scoring lower at Max than at XHigh. Papers document 'inverse scaling', where Claude Opus 4 fell from near-perfect to about 80% on a distractor task when it reasoned longer, and 'overthinking', with about 20x the tokens spent on 2+3. Most importantly for honest framing, a real generation jump can beat the old model at any setting: Anthropic says Opus 5.5 at default (medium) beats Opus 5 at max. Suggested wording for the piece: 'inside one model, the thinking dial moves the score more than the last upgrade did, and for hard questions, not easy ones'. All items were read live on 15 Oct 2026. Chart values from the Anthropic page are its embedded data labels, and every cross-comparison figure is our arithmetic, labelled as such. ## Pillar 4: will AI decide how hard AI thinks? State of play on 15 October 2026, the trajectory, the pros and cons, and three dated predictions

State of play, 15 Oct 2026. The three big apps have split on whether AI decides how hard AI thinks. Google has moved toward AI deciding: free Gemini defaults to Auto, which routes most prompts to Flash-Lite and harder ones to Flash or Pro (P4-01). OpenAI has moved away from it in the consumer app. It removed auto-thinking for Free and Go in Dec 2025, reportedly because the router cost more and hurt engagement (P4-03). It removed it for Plus and Pro on 14 Sep 2026 with no stated reason, keeping it only for safety and in Business workspaces, where it can quietly use up the reasoning allowance (P4-02). Anthropic's Claude app makes thinking always on for Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 and lets the user pick the effort level (P4-06). Underneath all three, the models already decide how much to think within whatever level is set: Anthropic's adaptive thinking, Gemini's dynamic thinking, OpenAI's 'reason adaptively', and GPT-6 trained to weigh your waiting time (P4-07, P4-08, P4-09). So the accurate framing is not 'AI will start deciding'. It is that AI already decides the inside, while companies and defaults decide the ceiling, and the fight is over who sets the ceiling. Pros of AI deciding: people stick with defaults (P4-04, plus Altman's <1% to 7% figure in v2), and always-max thinking wastes huge compute on easy questions (P4-10, 1,953% more tokens for 2+3). Speed also matters to users (P4-03, P4-05). Cons: you cannot see or audit it, since thinking time is quietly retuned and was cut by mistake (P4-05). Builders pay for invisible thinking with no hard budget, and Gemini can bill thinking on an empty answer (P4-07, P4-09). Models can under-think the hardest problems (P4-11). More thinking is not always better either, which cuts both ways (P4-12). Also verified but in v2 only: router 'trained on real signals' and 'autoswitcher broke'.
