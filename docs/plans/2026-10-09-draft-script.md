I've been watching what's coming with AI, before everyone notices. I'm Krish.

So I've been noticing something about the AI apps I use every day. Behind each app there are now lots of models, which are the AI engines underneath. And I think soon you won't see any of them. So I wanted to break it down for you. What happens when the app picks your AI for you?

Look at the menu today. Claude Code alone lists more than a dozen models you can pick by name. And the newest come with a dial for how hard they think.

Those models exist for the companies' reasons, not yours. Small ones are cheap to run. The computers that run the big ones are scarce, so they get saved for hard jobs. And every new name gets a launch day and a headline.

But that menu is already disappearing. In August 2025, OpenAI launched GPT-5 with a router built in. The router picks the engine for you, and the old ones people had chosen were replaced. People were furious, and OpenAI brought one back the next day. But I think one box wins in the end, because most of us don't want homework.

So what changes when the app does the picking? Start with money. If OpenAI or Anthropic picks the engine, they decide how much engine your twenty dollars buys that day. On a busy day, I'd bet that's the cheaper one. And I think they'll start selling the good one as an extra.

Then there's getting the job done. A router can send the same question to a different engine on a different day. When OpenAI's router broke for part of a day, Sam Altman said GPT-5 "seemed way dumber". And if an app quietly swaps the engine, nobody tells you. So when a task fails, you can't tell if it was you or the router.

If you build a business on AI, it's worse. Your costs and your quality can change without you touching anything. So stop looking at the price per word, and measure what a finished job actually costs you.

And the big one is power. I think we'll stop being loyal to the engine and just be loyal to the app. The engines become invisible parts, like the chip inside your phone.

So what do you do? When it really matters, find the setting and pick the model yourself. And if you build on AI, pick a model for each task, and rerun the same test every month.

My prediction. By the end of 2027, the big AI apps won't show you a list of models unless you ask. You'll get one box. And choosing will be for people who really go looking.
