## End message

Draft, joining both: when a big company wants AI it cannot lose, it now buys the team and the code and leaves the lawsuits, and the people who used the app, behind with the empty shell. Disney learned it the hard way: it tried renting its AI from OpenAI, and Sora vanished. So if your business runs on someone else's AI, plan for both: the supplier walking away, and the shell you could be left holding.

## The arc (checked facts, 26 Oct)

Krish, 2026-10-26, on question 3 (the order below, opening on "Disney asked a bankrupt AI company to go bust so it could buy its team without the lawsuits"): "Yes, that order works"

1. Google, August 2024: a reported $2.7 billion to license Character.AI's technology and hire its founders and pre-training research team; most staff stayed (Wired, 19 Oct 2026, Google's spokesperson).
2. Disney, December 2025: a $1 billion equity deal with OpenAI and a licence for 200+ characters in Sora (renting AI).
3. March 2026: OpenAI shuts Sora; the Disney deal ends with no money changed hands.
4. Disney, September 2026: hires Character.AI's chief executive as its first CTO; pays $5.5 million plus a $2 million refundable deposit (Wired).
5. Disney, October 2026: requires the deal to go through bankruptcy because of the startup's "diminishing cash reserve and significant litigation and regulatory risks"; seeks a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive licence to the code, trust and safety stack, training infrastructure and age-prediction technology, "free and clear", with liabilities left with Character.AI; users' data and the website are not in it (Wired, court filings). Offers jobs to 66 staff at $60 million a year for three years (Gizmodo, filings).
6. The users and the people suing: no money from either deal; the claims stay with the shell.
