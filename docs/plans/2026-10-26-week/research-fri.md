Source: Wilmers, Catipon, Massenkoff and McCrory, "How Employment is Changing at Anthropic", The Anthropic Institute,
published 16 October 2026,
https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/72628f1863c53432195db83279252dfd2cb53158/Employment%20at%20Anthropic.pdf (read 26
October 2026). Exact lines:

- "the share of junior-level employees in technical departments at Anthropic slipped from 21% to 14% since early 2025.
  The proportion of junior technical hires fell even faster, from 27% to 9%. Postings for juniors have fallen
  particularly sharply since December 2025."
- "By August interactive AI agent hours outnumbered employees' 6 to 1."
- "Within Anthropic, discussion of a decline in demand for junior technical employees emerged in core technical
  functions in January 2026." "By April, engineering and research teams had deemphasized hiring for lower band
  technical positions." "Around a third of ML and a fifth of SWE managers reported hiring less due to AI."
- "code commits per engineer have risen the fastest, coming in at 5x their velocity as 6 months ago."
- "Hiring managers increasingly emphasize applicants' autonomy and judgment relative to specific technical expertise."
- "In some roles, they are increasingly removed from the object-level work that originally built their skills."
- "employees at Anthropic expected that AI will be able to do 75% of their work by 2030, compared to 28% for other
  Americans." (As of August.)
- An executive assistant: "I think we've already moved past the point of Claude augmenting away the really entry-level
  admin type role".
- "Employment protections for incumbents will not benefit those not hired to begin with."
- "Prior work also finds that AI-native start-ups and AI-adopters are flatter and employ fewer entry-level employees."
  (cites Kim and Koning 2026, "AI-Native Firms"; Hosseini Maasoum and Lichtinger 2025, "Generative AI as
  Seniority-Biased Technological Change").
- Anthropic "has tripled in size in the last year"; employees with a year's tenure were "50% more likely to move across
  departments each month in 2026 than they were in 2025"; the company runs "an early warning system" for jobs at risk.

## The past (read 26 Oct 2026)

**ATMs and bank tellers.** James Bessen, "Toil and Technology", IMF Finance & Development, March 2015,
https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2015/03/bessen.htm. Exact lines:

- "the number of bank teller jobs did not decrease as the ATMs were rolled out"
- "Thanks to the ATM, the number of tellers required to operate a branch office in the average urban market fell from
  20 to 13 between 1988 and 2004. But banks responded by opening more branches to compete for greater market share.
  Bank branches in urban areas increased 43 percent."
- "The skills of the teller changed: cash handling became less important and human interaction more important."

**Spreadsheets and bookkeeping clerks.** NPR Planet Money, "Episode 606: Spreadsheets!", 25 Feb 2015,
https://www.npr.org/sections/money/2015/02/25/389027988/episode-606-spreadsheets (transcript
https://www.npr.org/transcripts/389027988). Exact lines:

- "since 1980, right around the time the electronic spreadsheet came out, 400,000 bookkeeping and accounting clerk jobs
  have gone away. But 600,000 accounting jobs have been added."
- "What happened is that accounting basically became cheaper. And sometimes, when something gets cheaper, people buy a
  lot more of that thing."

**Generative AI and juniors, outside Anthropic.** Hosseini Maasoum and Lichtinger, "Generative AI as Seniority-Biased
Technological Change: Evidence from U.S. Résumé and Job Posting Data" (SSRN 5425555, 2025; abstract on Stanford's
Digital Economy Lab page,
https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/event/seyed-m-hosseini-and-guy-lichtinger-generative-ai-as-seniority-biased-technological-change-evidence-from-u-s-resume-and-job-posting-data/).
Exact lines: "beginning in 2023Q1, junior employment in adopting firms declined sharply relative to non-adopters, while
senior employment continued to rise." "The junior decline is driven primarily by slower hiring rather than increased
separations." The paper itself (excerpt via search, to read in full before use): "junior employment in adopting firms
fell by about 9 percent relative to controls six quarters after".

What the past says, as our read: each earlier tool took the routine bottom of a job, the work got cheaper, demand grew,
and the rung changed shape (tellers moved to selling; clerks gave way to accountants). What may differ now, from the
Anthropic report: the work being taken is the work juniors learned on ("removed from the object-level work that
originally built their skills"), and the cut shows up as hires that never happen ("Employment protections for
incumbents will not benefit those not hired to begin with").
