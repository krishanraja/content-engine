## End message

Draft 1, for his yes (question 1, asked 26 Oct): every big work tool before AI took the dull bottom of a job, and the
job came back bigger. ATMs cut the tellers a branch needed from 20 to 13, but banks opened 43% more branches, and
tellers moved to selling. Spreadsheets took 400,000 bookkeeping clerk jobs and added 600,000 accountant jobs. AI is
doing it again, but this time it takes the work juniors learned on, and the cut shows up as people who are never
hired: at Anthropic, juniors fell from 27% of technical hires to 9%. So the question for any business is who trains
the seniors you will need in 2030.

Checked against the research: every number is on a filed line in `research-fri.md`. "The job came back bigger" is our
read of the two past cases; "who trains the seniors" is the open question the Anthropic report raises ("Employment
protections for incumbents will not benefit those not hired to begin with"). Checked against recent pieces: no recent
piece's takeaway was about the entry-level rung (learning `no-repeat-takeaway`).
