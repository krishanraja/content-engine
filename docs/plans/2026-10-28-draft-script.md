Claude now messages you first. So what does that actually change for you, if you already use Claude at work?

I've been reading what people are saying about Claude Duet, Anthropic's new assistant. It's a real shift in how we'll work with AI, so I wanted to break it down for you.

Claude Duet is a new Claude app, and it's the one that messages you first. Every few hours it checks your email, your calendar, Slack, your sales system and your accounting software. Each morning it sends you a short brief. And while you're busy, it drafts the replies, the prep for your meetings and the documents you'll need.

Then it stops. Before it sends a message, pays for anything, signs or deletes something, it shows you what it wants to do. And it waits for your yes. It only sees the apps you choose to connect, and you can cut any of them off.

Now, can you get it? Right now it's only in the US, on Claude's paid personal plans, off a waitlist. Company plans don't have it yet, and Anthropic hasn't said when they will. So for most of you, this is about getting ready to use it well from day one. And if you'd connect work email to a personal plan, check with your IT team first. Your company may have rules about where its email goes.

So what do people think? The mood is curious and mostly warm, because people like how it looks and they like that it asks first. But there are two moans. First, it's slow to finish jobs. Second, all that background checking uses up your Claude plan's usage cap, the limit on how much Claude you get. Hit it, and you wait for it to reset. So heavy users worry they'll hit it sooner.

Now here's the bit no one says out loud. If you already live in Claude, Duet doesn't give you many new powers. What it changes is who starts the work.

Right now, you open Claude and paste in the email thread, the meeting invite and your notes. Then you ask. With Duet, the work turns up first. You get a brief in the morning and a list of what it needs from you. And there are drafts waiting for a yes or a no.

So your role changes. You stop asking good questions, and you start checking its work. Then it's yes, no, or not today.

One small business owner put it nicely. They said normal Claude could already do everything they used Duet for. They just hadn't thought to use it that way. Once Duet started bringing them the work, they cleared a backlog in a day and a half.

But here's the catch. A habit only sticks if something keeps pulling you back. If what it sends you isn't worth opening, you drift away. Even a reviewer who loved how it looks forgot to use it, because nothing it sent pulled them back. That's a lot of polish for an app you stop opening.

And not everything comes with you. Duet carries over what Claude has learned about you, and the apps you've connected. But it can't open files on your laptop.

So where do you start? Pick one task you do every week, and ask Duet to do it for you every week. Everything it needs should already be in your email, your calendar, Slack, your sales system or your accounting software. And right now, you copy and paste it all into Claude.

Prep for a client meeting is a good one. That's the invite and the last email thread, turned into one brief before the call. Your weekly sales check is another. That's which deals moved and which went quiet. Or the unpaid invoices you need to chase, drafted and waiting for your yes.

Now, where does this go next? Right now Duet can't open anything on your laptop, like the files for a report you're working on. Early users are already asking for that. So my prediction is that by the first of March 2027, it will. Picture it. On the train home, Duet asks if it should start Thursday's board report from the files on your laptop. You say yes, and it's waiting when you sit down. And I reckon that's when Duet becomes the first place you go each morning.

Here's what I'd do with this info if I were you. Pick the one task you do every week by copy and paste. That's your first Duet job. Make sure everything it needs is in those connected apps. When you get Duet, let it draft for a week, and check every draft before you say yes. And check your usage cap at the end of that week.
