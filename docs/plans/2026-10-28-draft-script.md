Claude now messages you first. So what does that actually change for you, if you already use Claude at work?

I've been reading what people are saying about Claude Duet, Anthropic's new assistant. It's a real shift in how we'll work with AI, so I wanted to break it down for you.

Claude Duet is a new Claude app, and it's the one that messages you first. Every few hours it checks your email, your calendar, Slack and your sales system. Each morning it sends you a short brief. And while you're busy, it drafts the replies, the meeting notes and the documents you'll need.

Then it stops. Before it sends a message, pays for anything, signs or deletes something, it shows you what it wants to do. And it waits for your yes. It only sees the apps you choose to connect, and you can cut any of them off.

Now, can you get it? Right now it's only in the US, on Claude's paid personal plans, off a waitlist. Company plans don't have it yet, and Anthropic hasn't said when they will. So for most of you, this is about getting ready. And that's worth doing now.

So what do people think? The mood is curious and mostly warm, because people like how it looks and they like that it asks first. But there are two moans. First, it's slow to finish jobs. Second, it uses up the same usage limit as the rest of your Claude plan. So heavy users worry they'll hit that limit sooner.

Now here's the bit no one says out loud. If you already live in Claude, Duet doesn't give you many new powers. What it changes is who starts the work.

Right now, you open Claude and paste in the email thread, the meeting invite and your notes. Then you ask. With Duet, the work turns up first. You get a brief in the morning and a list of what it needs from you. And there are drafts waiting for a yes or a no.

So your role changes. You stop asking good questions, and you start giving good answers. Yes, no, or not today.

One small business owner put it nicely. They said normal Claude could already do everything they used Duet for. They just hadn't thought to use it that way. And with Duet, they cleared a backlog of work in a day and a half.

But here's the catch. A habit only sticks if something keeps pulling you back. If what it sends you isn't worth opening, you drift away. Even a reviewer who loved how it looks forgot to use it, because nothing it sent pulled them back. That's a lot of polish for an app you stop opening.

And not everything comes with you. Duet already knows what your Claude knows about you, and it uses the apps you've connected. But it can't open files on your computer. And some tools your company set up itself won't work with it yet.

So where do you start? Pick one task you do every week. Everything it needs should already be in your email, your calendar, Slack, or your sales or accounts system. And right now, you copy and paste it all into Claude.

Prep for a client meeting is a good one. That's the invite, the last email thread and the proposal in Google Docs, in one brief before the call. Your weekly sales check is another, so which deals moved and which went quiet. Or the unpaid invoices you need to chase, drafted and waiting for your yes.

Now, where does this go next? Right now Duet can see the bigger jobs Claude is doing on your computer, but it can't start them. Early users are already asking for that. So my prediction is that Duet will start those jobs for you by the first of March 2027. And I reckon that makes it the one place you go to get Claude working for you.

Here's what I'd do with this info if I were you. Pick the one task you do every week by copy and paste. That's your first Duet job. Make sure everything it needs is in your email, calendar, Slack or sales system. When you get Duet, let it draft for a week while you still press send yourself. And check your usage limit at the end of that week.
