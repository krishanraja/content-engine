## The evidence each section stands on

- **Section 1, the name game:** Claude Code's help page lists more than a dozen models
  you can pick by name (already checked). Each name does four jobs: a
  version number, a price tag, an advert (every new name gets a launch day)
  and a queue ticket (already in the draft). New: one line on how much
  coverage a launch gets. To be sourced on Sunday, or cut.
- **Section 2, what people actually do:**
  - Sam Altman, 10 August 2025: before GPT-5, under 1 in 100 free users
    (7 in 100 paying ones) used a reasoning model on a given day, though it
    was right there in the menu
    (https://simonwillison.net/2025/Aug/10/sam-altman). New: goes to the
    fact gate.
  - "How People Use ChatGPT", NBER working paper 34255, September 2025:
    practical guidance, looking things up and writing make up nearly 80 in
    100 conversations; coding is a small share
    (https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255). New: goes to the fact gate.
- **Section 3, when the pick does matter:**
  - GPT-5's router broke on day one and it "seemed way dumber" (already
    checked).
  - Once the app picked for people, reasoning use rose to 7 in 100 free
    users and 24 in 100 paying ones (Altman, as above). The pick moved
    behaviour more than the menu ever did.
  - The draft's contract, medical and big-decision cases (already in the
    draft).
- **Section 4, who the picking serves:**
  - SemiAnalysis: about five times the value on Claude's everyday plans
    against ChatGPT's (already checked).
  - The router decides how much engine your money buys (already in the
    draft).
- **Section 5, what to do:** Kept from the draft: the default for everyday asks; pick
  the biggest engine and turn the thinking up for a contract, a medical
  question or a big decision; builders pick per task and rerun the same
  test every month.
