Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Meta will spend nearly three quarters of a trillion dollars this year on AI data centres. The AI tools your team pays for run inside them. So who pays for it all?

These four are called hyperscalers. A hyperscaler is like a landlord that rents out computing power. It builds the data centre first, then earns rent from businesses like yours for years.

They used to pay for it out of spare profits. Now about one in three dollars is borrowed. And borrowed money costs interest every month.

That only works if the buildings open on time. But data centres use huge amounts of power, and many people blame them for rising bills. So in this month's US elections, town after town voted to limit new ones. Every month of delay is more interest, with no rent coming in. And those costs end up in the rent, which is your AI bill.

So my call is that AI won't get cheaper as fast as the AI firms promise. If I ran a business, I wouldn't budget for my AI bill coming down next year.
