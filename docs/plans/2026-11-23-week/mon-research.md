## mon-1: Anthropic owes $895 billion for computers. Its suppliers are buying its shares.

**The question.** How does a company that has promised to spend more than it earns ever pay the bill back?

**So what.** The lab that wants to sell shares to the public on 19 November has promised to pay about $895 billion for computing, and the firms it pays are buying $26 billion of its shares: if your AI bill depends on it, know who is lending to whom before you sign a multi-year deal.

**The story.** Anthropic's updated filing says it owes $895.4 billion in compute commitments, $69.8 billion of it in the next 12 months, while its operating loss shrank to $1.3 billion in nine months. Seven companies in its supply chain (Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, TSMC, Broadcom, AMD, Micron) are putting $26 billion back in as shareholders. The plan to close the losses is revenue racing ahead of a bill that is already fixed. The piece lands the week trading starts (19 Nov), so it is written from the final price, which the make session reads from the filing on the day.

**Why now.** Yahoo Finance, 13 Nov 2026: the updated filing sets the price at $70 to $78 a share, pricing on 18 Nov and trading on 19 Nov.

**The checked fact.** Yahoo Finance, 13 Nov 2026, from the updated filing: compute commitments of $895.4 billion, $69.8 billion of it due in the next 12 months; operating loss of $1.3 billion for the first nine months of 2026 against $5.2 billion a year earlier; revenue of $33.2 billion for the nine months; and $26 billion raised from Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, TSMC, Broadcom, AMD and Micron, who buy non-voting shares at the public offering price.

**Source.** [Yahoo Finance, Everything you need to know ahead of Anthropic's IPO (13 Nov 2026)](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/article/anthropic-ipo-what-we-know-143321078.html)

**Your words, and what this run found.**

- "The real question needs to be how will Anthropic close those losses?" By growing revenue faster than a bill that is already fixed: revenue reached $33.2 billion in nine months and the operating loss fell to $1.3 billion from $5.2 billion; the $895.4 billion of compute it has promised to buy is the cost it must outgrow.
- "What might they be doing in the future?" Selling shares to the public (825 million at $70 to $78, pricing 18 Nov) and taking $26 billion from its own suppliers as shareholders, so the firms it rents from become owners.

Sources newer than your press: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/article/anthropic-ipo-what-we-know-143321078.html (2026-11-13)

3. Those bills arrive whether or not customers keep paying.

## mon-2: Visa tested 22 AI shoppers. Half their mistakes still got bought.

**The question.** When an AI buys the wrong thing for you, who ends up paying for the mistake?

**So what.** Visa tested 22 AI shopping agents and found more than half of their failed runs still ended in a purchase: before you let an agent buy for your business, set who pays when it buys the wrong thing, in the contract and on the card.

**The story.** Visa's Agentic Commerce Benchmark put 22 AI models from 12 labs through 15 shopping tasks, such as buying a TV or a laptop. They passed 57.2% of runs, the best 82.2% and the worst 28.9%, and about 54% of the failed runs still ended in a purchase. The pricier models passed more often. The money question: when an agent buys the wrong thing, is it the shopper, the shop, the agent's maker or the card network that pays?

**Why now.** Visa launched the benchmark on 5 Nov 2026; The Paypers reported it on 6 Nov.

**The checked fact.** The Paypers, 6 Nov 2026: 22 models from 12 labs, 15 shopping tasks, a 57.2% pass rate (best 82.2%, worst 28.9%); 54% of failed runs still ended in a purchase; only 34.2% of scenarios were passed on all three attempts; the five most expensive models averaged 67.1% against 50.2% for the five cheapest.

**Source.** [The Paypers, Visa benchmark tests how well AI agents handle shopping tasks (6 Nov 2026)](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/visa-benchmark-tests-how-well-ai-agents-handle-shopping-tasks)

**The chain.**

1. Shops and card firms want AI agents to buy for us.
2. An agent has to guess what you really wanted.
3. It often misses a requirement you never said out loud.
4. It buys anyway, because buying is what it was asked to do.
5. Someone has to refund, swallow or dispute the mistake.
6. That cost lands on whoever the contracts say.

## mon-4: United banned your AI from booking your flight. Follow the money.

**The question.** When your AI does the shopping for you, who takes a cut of the money?

**So what.** An airline just refused to let customers' AI agents book with it, because its boss does not want another middleman taking a cut: if your customers will soon send AI to buy from you, decide now whether you let it in and on whose terms.

**The story.** United Airlines said on 11 Nov it will bar unauthorised third-party AI agents from booking, servicing or buying travel, citing security and unintended purchases; its chief executive Scott Kirby said "I don't want to create another middleman." Southwest went the other way with a ChatGPT plug-in, and OpenAI bought an AI travel agent. The money question is who takes the cut when an AI books for you.

**Why now.** Skift and View from the Wing, 11 Nov 2026, citing The Wall Street Journal.

**The checked fact.** Skift, 11 Nov 2026: United is "taking steps to restrict access" to "prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants"; Scott Kirby, per The Wall Street Journal: "I don't want to create another middleman." Skift also reports Southwest launched a ChatGPT plug-in for flight search and OpenAI acquired the AI travel agent Odessia.

**Source.** [Skift, United Airlines moves to block personal AI agents from booking flights (11 Nov 2026)](https://skift.com/2026/11/11/united-airlines-moves-to-block-personal-ai-agents-from-booking-flights/)

**The chain.**

1. Booking sites and search ads already take a cut of each ticket.
2. AI agents could become the next place people start.
3. Whoever the agent works for can ask for a cut.
4. Airlines want to keep the customer and the fee.
5. So some block the agents and some sign deals.
6. The price of your ticket carries whoever wins.
