## fri-1: The good employee is the one AI replaces.

**The question.** Which of the habits that made you good at your job are the ones AI will replace first?

**So what.** AI does the obedient, careful parts of a job cheapest, and the scarce skill is owning the result: decide who signs for each AI-made outcome in your team, and reward that in reviews, before someone else decides it in a layoff.

**The story.** You ticked the LBC clip you saved on 12 Nov. IBM's 2026 CHRO study (21 Sep 2026; 1,500 HR chiefs and about 8,800 staff) found 71% of HR chiefs rate checking, supervising and overruling AI as essential, and separately that 43% of staff say they get blamed when AI goes wrong. Challenger counts 121,401 US job cuts blamed on AI so far this year.

**Why now.** Challenger, Gray and Christmas October report, 5 Nov 2026; your tick of 12 Nov on the LBC card.

**The checked fact.** Challenger, Gray and Christmas, 5 Nov 2026: AI has been cited in 121,401 job cut announcements so far in 2026, about 20% of all cuts.

**Source.** [Challenger, Gray and Christmas, October 2026 job cuts report (5 Nov 2026)](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/october-layoffs-at-three-year-low-seasonal-hiring-plans-up-26/)

**The chain.**

1. Firms used to reward people who followed the process.
2. AI follows the process faster and cheaper.
3. So following is no longer worth a salary.
4. Someone still has to own the result when it goes wrong.
5. AI cannot be blamed or sacked.
6. Our call: the job goes to the person who signs for the outcome.

## fri-2: Is AI building itself? What is real and what is myth.

**The question.** Is AI really starting to build the next AI, or is that just a scary story?

**So what.** The labs now measure in writing how much of the next AI their AI is already making: expect the tools you use next year to change faster than this year's, and read any 'AI builds itself' claim against what the labs actually measure.

**The story.** On 10 Nov the AI Futures Project said all its forecasters now expect a superhuman AI researcher in 2027, moved most by METR's estimate that AI roughly doubles the speed of Anthropic's research. Anthropic's own filing warns it may soon cross its threshold for AI-driven acceleration, and says more than 80% of the code it merges is written by Claude. What is real and what is still a forecast, told simply.

**Why now.** AI Futures Project timelines update, 10 Nov 2026.

**The checked fact.** AI Futures Project, 10 Nov 2026: "Now, all of us forecast that Superhuman AI Researcher (SAR) will likely arrive at some point in 2027"; its biggest single reason is METR's estimate of about 2x overall acceleration of Anthropic's research, roughly 80% of it attributed to AI. Anthropic (Institute page, updated 18 Sep 2026): "more than 80% of the code we merge into Anthropic's codebase was authored by Claude" (as of May 2026). AI Futures Project, from Anthropic's chart: Claude led about 26% of model research tasks by August 2026.

**Source.** [AI Futures Project, Q3.5 timelines update (10 Nov 2026)](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q35-timelines-update-looks-like-the)

No lab says a model builds its successor alone yet; Anthropic's filing says none of its current models crosses its threshold, and the forecasts now point to 2027.

4. Faster models then write more of the code.
5. The loop speeds up, a little each turn.

## fri-3: Nvidia without Jensen Huang: the boom loses its face.

**The question.** What happens to a booming industry when the one person it seemed to depend on suddenly leaves?

**So what.** The company the whole AI boom buys its chips from lost its founder overnight, and its board named its first ever chair the same day: expect the biggest AI firms to look less like one person's vision and more like ordinary companies, and ask where your own suppliers depend on one person.

**The story.** On 12 Nov Nvidia said Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, days after its board opened an investigation into reports he was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort (Fortune, 9 Nov); the filing itself gives no reason. Its finance chief Colette Kress became interim chief executive, and Harvey C. Jones became the board's first ever chair. The pattern: the AI boom was built around a few founders, and the companies are now being forced to run without them.

**Why now.** Nvidia's filing, 12 Nov 2026.

**The checked fact.** Nvidia, 12 Nov 2026 (SEC filing): "Jensen Huang has resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer, and a member of the Board, effective immediately." Colette Kress "has agreed to serve as Interim Chief Executive Officer"; "The Board of Directors appointed Harvey C. Jones as its first-ever Chair of the Board."

**Source.** [Nvidia press release filed with the SEC (12 Nov 2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm)

**The chain.**

1. A founder builds a company around his own judgement.
2. The company grows faster than any structure around him.
3. The whole industry starts to read his words as the plan.
4. Then the founder is suddenly gone.
5. The board has to build what the founder was.
6. The company starts to look like an ordinary company.

## fri-4: AI found a lost Maya site. An archaeologist found it first.

**The question.** When a computer says it has discovered something new, how do you know nobody found it first?

**So what.** An 'AI discovered' claim is a lead until someone has checked what is already known: make a prior-art check the rule before acting on any AI finding in your own business.

**The story.** A Google researcher set 48 AI agents on NASA laser scans and in about 72 hours flagged a Maya site missing from Mexico's official register. Then he found it had already been described in a 2025 paper the agents missed. The pattern: AI is fast at finding and weak at checking what is already known.

**Why now.** Winzheng report, 12 Nov 2026; The Verge, 13 Nov 2026.

**The checked fact.** Winzheng, 12 Nov 2026: 48 agents reviewed about 1,200 square kilometres of NASA LiDAR data and reported to Mexico's INAH within about 72 hours; Pietro Miotti later said the site was already documented in a 2025 paper by Estrada-Belli et al.

**Source.** [Winzheng, 48 AI agents flag a Maya site in 72 hours, but miss a key paper (12 Nov 2026)](https://www.winzheng.com/en/article/ai-agents-maya-lidar-discovery-72-hours)

**The chain.**

1. AI can look at far more data than people.
2. So it flags lots of possible finds.
3. Checking what is already known is slow reading.
4. Agents skip or miss the reading.
5. Old finds come back as new ones.
6. Headlines run before the check.
