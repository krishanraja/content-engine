I've been looking at what's really inside the AI everyone's selling us. I'm Krish.

So I've been reading the reviews of a new app from Meta. It's called Muse. And something odd keeps coming up. People aren't saying it's clever. They're saying it gets things done. So what does a company that sells adverts want with a helper like that?

One person says a shop charged them $6,000 for clothes that cost about $1,500. Muse wrote to the shop to get the money back. Another says it filled in their job forms from start to finish. Millions of people have downloaded it.

Muse is an AI helper that does jobs for you. It visits websites, fills in forms and argues for a better price. And honestly? Most of that isn't new. A CNN reporter found that ChatGPT and Google's Gemini can do similar jobs. Muse also suggested two places for a date night that had closed years ago. Very romantic.

So I think it isn't a cleverer brain. What it has is closeness. Meta owns Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp. Muse can turn a recipe video you saved on Instagram into a shopping list. ChatGPT can't see your saved videos. Muse can.

And that's where I got stuck. Because there's a bigger question here.

Meta makes almost all its money from adverts. Last quarter it made about 60 billion dollars, and nearly every dollar came from ads. That's the business.

Now picture that company running a helper that picks things for you. Your dinner. Your plumber. Your next pair of trainers.

Meta says your chats with Muse stay away from its adverts. I believe them, for now. But that promise is about your data. It says nothing about the picks.

On Facebook, a shop pays so you'll see it. Inside Muse, a shop could pay so your helper chooses it. You scroll past an advert. But when your helper picks something, you probably just say yes. That's the whole point of having a helper.

Meta already has the shops. It says 200 million small businesses are on Facebook. Now millions of people are letting Muse choose for them. The only thing missing is a price.

So here's my prediction. By 30 June 2027, Meta will let businesses pay so that Muse recommends them to you. I'd put that at 70%. The only thing slowing it down is trust. Sell the picks too soon, and people leave.

Until then, I'd use it for small jobs. Getting money back from a shop? Great. And there's a setting that lets Meta use your chats to teach its AI. It starts switched on. I'd switch it off.
