I've been looking at what's really inside the AI everyone's selling us. I'm Krish.

I've been reading the reviews of Meta's new AI helper, Muse. And they're strange. They don't read like reviews of an app. They read like thank-you notes to an assistant. And Meta, an ad company, is giving that assistant away. Why?

One person says a shop charged them $6,000 for clothes that cost about $1,500. Muse wrote to the shop to get the money back. Another says it does their job forms from start to finish. Millions of people have downloaded it, and most of it is free.

That's what bugs me. Last quarter Meta made about 60 billion dollars, and nearly every dollar came from ads. So what's in it for them?

It's not because Muse is smarter. A CNN reporter found that ChatGPT and Google's Gemini can do similar jobs. Muse even suggested two date night spots that had closed years ago. Very romantic.

What Muse has is position. Meta owns Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp. So Muse can see the recipe video you saved on Instagram, and turn it into a shopping list. ChatGPT can't see that.

Now think about how ads have worked for twenty years. You scroll. A shop pays to get in front of you. Then you decide.

With a helper, you don't scroll. You just ask. Find me a plumber. Book dinner. And it picks one for you.

So I think the ad doesn't need your eyes any more. It needs your helper's choice. And whoever owns the helper owns the choice.

Meta already has both sides. On one side, it says 200 million small businesses are on Facebook. On the other, millions of people are now letting Muse choose for them.

To be fair, Meta says your chats with Muse stay away from its ads. I believe that, for now. But read it closely. That promise is about your data. It says nothing about who gets picked.

So here's my prediction. By 30 June 2027, Meta will let businesses pay so that Muse recommends them to you. I'd put it at 70%. The only thing slowing it down is trust. Sell the picks too early, and people walk.

Until then, I'd use it for small jobs, like getting money back from a shop. And there's a setting that lets Meta use your chats to teach its AI new things. It starts switched on. I'd switch it off.
