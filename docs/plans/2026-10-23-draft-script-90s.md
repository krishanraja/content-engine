You may already be using a Chinese AI. Does that matter?

Airbnb's boss said its customer service leans on Qwen, a Chinese AI. He called it fast and cheap, which is what most of us want from a holiday fling.

Every AI has two passports: where it was born, and where it lives.

Where it was born shapes its answers. An American newspaper found Chinese chatbots refused, gave the government's line or got it wrong a third of the time. An American AI, Claude, answered every one.

Now, where it lives decides who sees your data. Use DeepSeek, another Chinese AI, and it keeps yours in China.

So does it matter at work? I asked Claude, ChatGPT and DeepSeek about a supplier's cotton from Xinjiang, in China. Two flagged forced labour. But DeepSeek wrote an answer, then deleted it. Every time.

My call is it's fine, if you don't mind where your data goes. Just check what it tells you.

Here's what I'd do with this info if I were you. Ask suppliers which AI is inside and where it lives, and keep a second AI ready.
