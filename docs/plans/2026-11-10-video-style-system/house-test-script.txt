Cash machines should have ended the bank teller: the person behind the counter who hands you your cash.
In 1988, a city bank branch needed twenty tellers.
By 2004, it needed thirteen.
So you would expect those jobs to disappear.
They didn't. Each branch got cheaper to run, so banks opened more branches. Forty-three percent more in cities.
And the total number of teller jobs stayed about the same.
The tellers stopped counting cash. They spent more time talking to customers.
Two things moved. Fewer tellers in each branch. More branches.
