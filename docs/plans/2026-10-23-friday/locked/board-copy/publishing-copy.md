## LinkedIn

Britain, France, the Gulf, India and Korea are all spending serious money on an AI of their own. Airbnb's boss, meanwhile, says its customer service leans on Alibaba's Qwen because it's "fast and cheap". Which is also what most of us want from a holiday fling.

Both are reasonable. Countries want to rely on their own AI. Businesses want the clever job done for less. The question is what each choice carries with it.

My way of thinking about it: every AI has two passports. Where it was built shapes what it tells you. Where it runs decides who sees your data.

So I gave Claude, ChatGPT and DeepSeek the same work questions, three times each, changing only the country. A UK clothing firm with a cotton supplier in Turkmenistan, then the same supplier in Xinjiang. America has blocked cotton from both over forced labour.

All three flagged it for Turkmenistan. For Xinjiang, DeepSeek wrote a full answer covering the Modern Slavery Act, then deleted it and changed the subject. Every time. It knew the answer. It just wasn't allowed to let you keep it.

That's one afternoon and a handful of questions, so I wouldn't stretch it. A newspaper's test of 200 questions and the US government's own test point the same way.

Here's what I'd do with this info if I were you: ask your main software suppliers which AI their product runs on and where it runs. Put your own work questions to your team's AI and to one built in another country. And keep a second AI ready, so you're never stuck with one supplier.
