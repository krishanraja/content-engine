# Narrative lock: Mon 16 Nov follow.the.money - who is paying for the AI build

piece_id: 8287846b-6618-4a19-9fb8-23ad4eca2718
Publishes: 2026-11-16

## Surface story
Oracle is selling $10 billion of new shares and $10 billion of new bonds, on top of about $125 billion of debt, with a credit rating one step above junk. In June it said it did not expect to borrow any more in 2026. It now says customers paying up front are part of how it gets to positive cash flow, and it expects that only in the second half of 2028.

## Deeper story
The AI build used to be paid for out of profits. It is not any more. In 2024 less than 1 dollar in 10 came from borrowing or selling shares; this year, by one estimate, about 4 in 10 do. The rest of the bill is being split between lenders, shareholders, the chip makers who invest in their own customers, and the customers themselves: Oracle says $75 billion of its build is customers' money paid up front or chips its customers bought. So all this money is only real if the demand turns up on time, and for Oracle "on time" means 2028. Krish's brief: "where, in general, money comes from that tech companies get to build AI ... devastatingly simple and insightful even though it's pulling in a lot of complexity of insight."

## Arc
- Hook: "One of the world's biggest software companies is selling $10 billion of shares and $10 billion of bonds in a single day."
- Why: AI needs data centres, and a data centre needs the cash years before any customer pays for it.
- The giants used to pay from their own profits. In 2024 under 1 dollar in 10 was borrowed or raised; this year about 4 in 10.
- Follow one dollar: Nvidia puts money into OpenAI, OpenAI pays Oracle, Oracle buys Nvidia chips. Part of the money goes round in a circle.
- The click: the last payer in the line is the customer. If your supplier asks you to pay up front, you may be paying for its build.
- The date that decides it: Oracle expects positive cash flow only in the second half of 2028. If the demand comes late, whoever paid first waits longest.
- The use: what a leader does before paying an AI supplier up front.

## The use
Before you pay any AI supplier up front, ask it one question: what happens to my money if your build runs late? (Best guess, put to Krish 10 Nov; Friday's use asks where the computing is built, so this one asks about the money.)
