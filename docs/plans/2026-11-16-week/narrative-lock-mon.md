# Narrative lock: Mon 16 Nov follow.the.money - what a hyperscaler is and where its money comes from

piece_id: 8287846b-6618-4a19-9fb8-23ad4eca2718
Publishes: 2026-11-16

## Surface story
Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Meta will spend about $730 billion this year building for AI. In the last three months, between them, they spent more on building than their businesses brought in. Oracle, the most stretched builder, is selling $10 billion of new shares and $10 billion of new bonds, and says customers paying up front are part of how it gets to positive cash flow in the second half of 2028.

## Deeper story
Everyone in a board meeting now says "hyperscaler". Few could say what one is, how it makes money, or who pays for it. Krish: "What exactly is a hyperscaler? What are the economics of one in simple terms, and where do they actually get their money from?" The answer fits in a few minutes. A hyperscaler is a landlord for computers: it builds giant data centres first and rents the computing out for years. That used to be paid for out of profits. Now, by one estimate, about 4 in every 10 dollars come from borrowing, new shares, partners, chip makers and customers paying up front. So the whole build is a bet that the renters turn up on time. The viewer should finish feeling "super intelligent and like they can enter their next board meeting with serious knowledge within minutes" (Krish, 10 Nov).

## Arc
- Hook: "Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Meta will spend about $730 billion this year building for AI. In the last three months, they spent more on it than their businesses brought in."
- What a hyperscaler is: a landlord for computers. Amazon, Microsoft and Google rent theirs out; Meta builds for itself; Oracle and CoreWeave are the newer, smaller landlords.
- How it makes money: build first, collect rent for years. The building costs come now; the rent comes later.
- Where the money used to come from: their other businesses (ads, shopping, software). In 2024 under 1 dollar in 10 was borrowed or raised.
- Where it comes from now, line by line: their own profits, then bonds, then new shares, then partners' buildings, then the chip maker's money, then customers paying up front. About 4 in 10 dollars from outside, by one estimate. Only Microsoft still had cash left over last quarter.
- The click: Oracle, at the edge. One step above junk, borrowing again, $75 billion of its build paid for by customers up front, and positive cash only in 2028. The rent has to arrive on time.
- The use: three numbers to take into your next board meeting.
