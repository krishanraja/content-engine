# Narrative lock: Mon 16 Nov follow.the.money - what a hyperscaler is and where its money comes from

piece_id: 8287846b-6618-4a19-9fb8-23ad4eca2718
Publishes: 2026-11-16

## Surface story
Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Meta will spend about $730 billion this year building for AI. In the last three months, between them, they spent more on building than their businesses brought in. Oracle, the most stretched builder, is selling $10 billion of new shares and $10 billion of new bonds, and says customers paying up front are part of how it gets to positive cash flow in the second half of 2028. In the same weeks, the midterms turned data centres into a vote: in 41 local ballot measures, voters backed limits or turned down new data centres in all but one, and within days OpenAI promised to pay the full cost of powering its own.

## Deeper story
Everyone in a board meeting now says "hyperscaler". Few could say what one is, how it makes money, or who pays for it. Krish: "What exactly is a hyperscaler? What are the economics of one in simple terms, and where do they actually get their money from?" The answer fits in a few minutes. A hyperscaler is a landlord for computers: it builds giant data centres first and rents the computing out for years. That used to be paid for out of profits. Now, by one estimate, about 4 in every 10 dollars come from borrowing, new shares, partners, chip makers and customers paying up front. So the whole build is a bet that it all happens on time: the buildings get built and powered, and the renters turn up. The midterms put the first half of that bet in doubt. A landlord that has borrowed to build now has to win the town's vote and pay its own power bill before the rent starts, and every month of delay is a month of interest with no rent. Krish: "I think we need to seriously address what's happened with the midterm elections and the impending backlash against data centers, and what that might mean." (10 Nov.) Monday says what the backlash means for the money; Friday's piece (signed 9 Nov) keeps the votes themselves and where AI goes next, and Monday makes no prediction about politics. The viewer should finish feeling "super intelligent and like they can enter their next board meeting with serious knowledge within minutes" (Krish, 10 Nov).

## Arc
- Hook: "Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Meta will spend about $730 billion this year building for AI. In the last three months, they spent more on it than their businesses brought in."
- What a hyperscaler is: a landlord for computers. Amazon, Microsoft and Google rent theirs out; Meta builds for itself; Oracle and CoreWeave are the newer, smaller landlords.
- How it makes money: build first, collect rent for years. The building costs come now; the rent comes later.
- Where the money used to come from: their other businesses (ads, shopping, software). In 2024 under 1 dollar in 10 was borrowed or raised.
- Where it comes from now, line by line: their own profits, then bonds, then new shares, then partners' buildings, then the chip maker's money, then customers paying up front. About 4 in 10 dollars from outside, by one estimate. Only Microsoft still had cash left over last quarter.
- The backlash: the bet needs the buildings built and powered on time. People blame data centres for higher power bills, and in the midterms towns voted to limit or turn down new ones in 40 of 41 votes. The builders are now promising to pay their own power bills. That is a new cost, and a delay, landing on companies that have already borrowed.
- The click: Oracle, where both pressures meet. One step above junk, borrowing again, $75 billion of its build paid for by customers up front, local pushback at its own sites, and positive cash only in 2028. A late building is rent that comes late, and the interest does not wait.
- The use: three numbers to take into your next board meeting, and one question.

## The use
Take three numbers into your next board meeting: about $730 billion is being spent on AI buildings this year; about 4 in 10 of those dollars are now borrowed or raised; and 40 of 41 local votes went against new data centres. Then ask which landlord your own AI runs on, and whether it pays from profits or borrows to build. (Krish, 10 Nov: "Keep the three numbers and the question")
