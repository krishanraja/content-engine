The AI labs still buy software: build-versus-buy research, second round

Research cutoff and price observation date: 17 November 2026. Prepared for piece db9f9f6a-1092-49be-8024-f335e060305a, covering historical stories, price changes from January 2023, and recent AI evidence.

This is an evidence report for the proposed video and enterprise article, not a finished script or an argument that buying always wins. The useful distinction is between writing a first version and taking responsibility for a working business service.

Reading the evidence

CONFIRMED: The bounded claim appears on a primary page. A vendor customer story confirms that the vendor and customer publicly make the claim; it does not turn their performance or savings claim into an independently audited result.

REPORTED: Secondary reporting only. Where the underlying interview, court filing, invoice or board paper was not inspected, that limitation remains.

ESTIMATE: Arithmetic, a hypothetical example or an inference. Inputs and working are shown; these are not observed customer savings.

Not disclosed / not established: No defensible number or conclusion was obtained. Missing project cost is not zero, a customer relationship is not an invoice, and a proposed increase is not a price actually paid.

The earlier wed-research.md and wed-community.md files were not supplied in this conversation. Deduplication is therefore against the named exclusions: Starbucks, the Retool survey, Klarna, the vendor earnings-call synthesis and the community corpus.

Part 1: Engineering builds, escapes and useful counterexamples

There are ten direct home-built-to-vendor cases here, followed by three explicitly adjacent architecture/rewrite cases requested in the brief. Do not describe all thirteen as failures to buy an available SaaS product.

Direct cases

P1-A. Uber: uChat, then Slack. CONFIRMED: Uber's 25 July 2017 engineering account describes uChat as a customised system built using open-source Mattermost, not a chat server invented entirely from scratch; it says the company transition took three months and that the team reached 99.9% availability before retiring the legacy system. ([Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/uchat/)) The Employee Productivity Tools and Infrastructure teams worked on it, with Fullstack Labs helping rewrite mobile applications; engineering headcount, full build duration and project cost are not disclosed, and the article's “millions annually” language covers the broader in-house toolkit rather than a separately audited uChat saving. ([Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/uchat/)) REPORTED: Uber subsequently rolled Slack out to 38,000 employees in February 2020; the later account does not supply a migration business case or contract value, so the sequence alone does not prove the original decision was irrational. ([Business Insider, 21 February 2020](https://www.businessinsider.com/uber-slack-deal-customer-chat-employees-2020-2)) The engineering article provides a first-person account, but a short, individually attributed quotation establishing regret was not found. ([Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/uchat/))

P1-B. Intercom: billing became a standing engineering assignment. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim: Intercom had an internal billing engine and administration interface connected to a third-party invoicing system, then chose Stripe Billing; one or two of seven senior engineers were handling billing errors and operational issues each week. ([Stripe/Intercom](https://stripe.com/customers/intercom)) The first phase took five months, new pricing launched in November 2023, and all subscription customers and revenue had moved by October 2025; the source supplies neither total engineering person-months nor project cost, and expected error reductions must not be presented as measured savings. ([Stripe/Intercom](https://stripe.com/customers/intercom)) VP of engineering Dave Lynch's exact sentence: “We just need to trigger events and Stripe handles the rest.” ([Stripe/Intercom](https://stripe.com/customers/intercom))

P1-C. LegalZoom: a billing system old enough to vote. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim: LegalZoom had maintained its home-grown billing platform for about twenty years before choosing Chargebee; the published migration was phased, with larger subscriptions still to come rather than all 1.8 million subscribers already moved. ([Chargebee/LegalZoom](https://www.chargebee.com/customers/legalzoom/)) Implementation dates, duration, staffing and cost are not disclosed, while an improvement in initial renewal approval rates is qualitative rather than a published percentage. ([Chargebee/LegalZoom](https://www.chargebee.com/customers/legalzoom/)) VP Ana Garcia's exact sentence provides important counterevidence to an irrational-build interpretation: “Solutions like Chargebee just didn't exist, and we built our own as we grew.” ([Chargebee/LegalZoom](https://www.chargebee.com/customers/legalzoom/)) This is a later outsourcing decision, not proof that an equivalent specialist product was available at inception.

P1-D. Octave: buying cost more than the old vendor bill. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim: Octave initially declined Measure, then called Maple, partly because a previous integration supplier had shut down; it built usage metering and billing infrastructure on Stripe and reconsidered after about two years. ([Measure/Octave, 17 February 2026](https://getmeasure.com/customer-story/octave)) The migration took a couple of months and a coordinated 2 a.m. cutover; staffing and internal engineering cost are not disclosed, while the stated annual vendor amounts are approximately $17,000 for Stripe and $20,000 for Measure, with different functionality and no complete invoice-level reconciliation. ([Measure/Octave](https://getmeasure.com/customer-story/octave)) CEO Zach Vidibor's exact sentence: “We want to spend every possible minute of our time building features and capabilities that our customers come uniquely to us for.” ([Measure/Octave](https://getmeasure.com/customer-story/octave))

P1-E. Capture: the home-grown ticket queue acquired a migration project. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim: Capture moved from its internal ticketing system to Zendesk after evaluating Freshdesk, Jira Service Management, HubSpot and Salesforce; the vendor says historical-data cleanup, migration and go-live took twelve weeks. ([Zendesk/Capture](https://www.zendesk.com/customer/capture/)) The company is described as having 23 employees, but implementation staffing, original build duration, original cost and Zendesk contract cost are not provided; the claimed 68% reduction in resolution time lacks a published measurement period and baseline. ([Zendesk/Capture](https://www.zendesk.com/customer/capture/)) Customer speaker Mallindine's exact sentence is: “And the integration with Jira was seamless.” ([Zendesk/Capture](https://www.zendesk.com/customer/capture/))

P1-F. SendinBlue: a marketing-software company bought support software. CONFIRMED vendor claim: Zendesk says SendinBlue replaced a home-grown support solution and completed rollout, including agent onboarding, in about one month; the account does not publish the original build's duration, staffing or cost, or the purchased contract value. ([Zendesk roundup, updated 4 July 2025](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/build-vs-buy/)) Chief Customer Care Officer Guillaume Rieu's exact sentence is: “We could immediately provide better follow-up to clients on their requests.” ([Zendesk roundup](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/build-vs-buy/)) This is evidence of a migration and a testimonial, not a controlled productivity comparison. ([Zendesk roundup](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/build-vs-buy/))

P1-G. GitHub: building developer tools did not oblige it to build ticketing. CONFIRMED vendor claim: Zendesk dates GitHub's move from home-grown support-request systems to Zendesk to 2012 and describes subsequent custom applications, APIs and Slack ticketing integration. ([Zendesk roundup, updated 4 July 2025](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/build-vs-buy/)) Neither original build duration nor migration duration, project staffing, contract cost or a measured saving is supplied; a suitable involved-person quotation was not obtained from this account. ([Zendesk roundup](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/build-vs-buy/)) The useful lesson is hybrid, not zero-code procurement: the purchased system still had custom integration work. ([Zendesk roundup](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/build-vs-buy/))

P1-H. Hy-Vee: payroll was an integration estate, not just a calculation. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim: Hy-Vee adopted Workday HCM in 2022 after a maintenance-heavy home-grown payroll system; Workday says it consolidated six payroll processes and seven timekeeping processes into one environment. ([Workday/Hy-Vee](https://www.workday.com/en-us/customer-stories/a-h/hy-vee-the-best-place-to-shop-is-also-best-place-to-work.html)) The story gives no implementation duration, project staffing or price; broader reported process and mobile-adoption gains should not be translated into cash savings without additional data. ([Workday/Hy-Vee](https://www.workday.com/en-us/customer-stories/a-h/hy-vee-the-best-place-to-shop-is-also-best-place-to-work.html)) The customer's exact sentence is: “With everything in Workday, it’s just seamless now.” ([Workday/Hy-Vee](https://www.workday.com/en-us/customer-stories/a-h/hy-vee-the-best-place-to-shop-is-also-best-place-to-work.html))

P1-I. Texas A\&M: 35,000 lines of payroll COBOL. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim: The university system replaced fragmented legacy HR/payroll applications, including a 35,000-line COBOL payroll engine and code up to 35 years old, with Workday across twenty member organisations in one tenant. ([Workday/Texas A\&M](https://www.workday.com/en-us/customer-stories/q-z/texas-a-m-moves-to-cloud-automates-finance-hr.html)) The page reports payroll execution falling from ten to twelve hours to under three hours and an on-time, under-budget deployment, but supplies no migration dates, budget, actual cost or implementation staffing. ([Workday/Texas A\&M](https://www.workday.com/en-us/customer-stories/q-z/texas-a-m-moves-to-cloud-automates-finance-hr.html)) No short complete quotation was independently checked for this report; use the attributed metrics rather than inventing a colourful line. ([Workday/Texas A\&M](https://www.workday.com/en-us/customer-stories/q-z/texas-a-m-moves-to-cloud-automates-finance-hr.html))

P1-J. Dollar Shave Club: maintaining the shop became a second business. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim: Dollar Shave Club moved its custom ecommerce platform to Shopify with implementation partners and applications, including subscription tooling; the source says roughly 40% of its technology resources had gone into maintaining the old platform. ([Shopify/Dollar Shave Club](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/dollar-shave-club)) The headline's maintenance-resource reduction is not an audited 40% reduction in total technology cash expenditure; dates, headcount, build cost, contract value and full migration duration are not disclosed. ([Shopify/Dollar Shave Club](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/dollar-shave-club)) Ecommerce VP Kyle Iwamoto's exact sentence: “With Shopify, we’ve been able to drive and spin up numerous landing pages instantly.” ([Shopify/Dollar Shave Club](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/dollar-shave-club))

Adjacent cases: do not mislabel these

P1-K. Segment: the microservices reversal. CONFIRMED participant/company account, 10 July 2018: Three full-time engineers were spending most of their time keeping the integration system alive before Segment consolidated destination microservices into a monolith and built supporting infrastructure. ([Segment Engineering](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/best-practices/goodbye-microservices)) The author reports improved productivity and less operational burden, but also weaker fault isolation; no purchased replacement, overall migration duration or dollar cost is established, so this is architecture versus architecture, not build versus buy. ([Segment Engineering](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/best-practices/goodbye-microservices)) The author's exact sentence: “It felt like magic.” ([Segment Engineering](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/best-practices/goodbye-microservices))

P1-L. Netscape: a genuine rewrite warning, not a clean causal experiment. CONFIRMED participant estimate, 31 March 1999: Jamie Zawinski described approximately one hundred full-time Netscape developers and thirty part-time outside contributors and estimated that the Gecko/Raptor rewrite set the project back six to ten months; he disclosed no dollar budget. ([Zawinski's resignation/postmortem](https://www.jwz.org/gruntle/nomo.html)) Joel Spolsky's 6 April 2000 essay is influential commentary about the release gap and rewrite risk, not a participant-verified demonstration that one rewrite alone killed Netscape. ([Spolsky](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/)) Neither source establishes an off-the-shelf browser engine that Netscape should simply have bought on equivalent terms; the procurement counterfactual remains unproven. ([Zawinski](https://www.jwz.org/gruntle/nomo.html), [Spolsky](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/))

P1-M. Discord: sometimes building the awkward connector is sensible. CONFIRMED company account, 6 March 2023, describing a May 2022 cutover: Discord moved from Cassandra to ScyllaDB and built Rust data services and migration tooling; its account compares 177 Cassandra nodes with 72 ScyllaDB nodes. ([Discord Engineering](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-stores-trillions-of-messages/)) Extending the Rust library reduced a migration estimate from three months to nine days; the actual transfer finished after several days, and the source provides no headcount or dollar cost. ([Discord Engineering](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-stores-trillions-of-messages/)) Bo Ingram's exact sentence: “In an afternoon, we extended our data service library to perform large-scale data migrations.” ([Discord Engineering](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-stores-trillions-of-messages/)) This is a mixed buy/adopt-and-build success story, not proof that all custom engineering is waste. ([Discord Engineering](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-stores-trillions-of-messages/))

Part 2: Executive ambition and programme economics

Only the first case below is a new, recent CEO claim of AI replacing a SaaS contract. The remaining eight are deliberately labelled programme/procurement cases: they illustrate executive accountability, customisation, scope and migration risk, but do not establish that eight named commercial executives personally ordered engineers to reinvent an available SaaS product.

A recent AI replacement claim

P2-A. Curative, Fred Turner: a $600,000 contract is not a $600,000 net saving. REPORTED, 21 July 2026: Turner said Curative replaced Salesforce with an AI-assisted internal CRM built in two months and planned to cut approximately 80% of company-wide SaaS spending; a company spokesperson confirmed cancellation. ([Business Insider](https://africa.businessinsider.com/news/curative-ceo-says-company-ditched-a-dollar600k-a-year-salesforce-contract-after/q14nc90)) His exact reported sentence is: “We just recently canceled our Salesforce contract because we have an internal CRM that was vibecoded.” ([Business Insider](https://africa.businessinsider.com/news/curative-ceo-says-company-ditched-a-dollar600k-a-year-salesforce-contract-after/q14nc90)) The same account says maintenance was challenging, but discloses no builder headcount, build cost, compliance cost, operating cost or audited net saving; it reports no reversal, and this research did not establish longer-term performance through the cutoff. ([Business Insider](https://africa.businessinsider.com/news/curative-ceo-says-company-ditched-a-dollar600k-a-year-salesforce-contract-after/q14nc90)) The underlying podcast was identified by the reporting but not inspected as the primary evidentiary record here, so the claim remains REPORTED rather than promoted to CONFIRMED.

Eight programme and procurement cases

P2-B. BBC Digital Media Initiative: supplier build, then in-house rescue, then cancellation. CONFIRMED: Parliament says the BBC brought development in-house in September 2009 after Siemens' contract ended, when the project was already eighteen months late; it was cancelled in May 2013 after missing a July 2011 completion promise. ([Public Accounts Committee, 10 April 2014](https://www.parliament.uk/external/committees/committee-news-pre-oct-2020/2014/april/bbc-dmi-report-substantive/)) The NAO records £125.9 million gross spending less £27.5 million of Siemens settlement value, leaving £98.4 million net cost; these are not two separate failures to add together. ([NAO, 28 January 2014](https://www.nao.org.uk/briefings/memorandum-on-the-bbcs-digital-media-initiative/)) Parliament says the resulting catalogue had 163 regular users and cost £3 million a year versus £780,000 for the old system, and DMI was used to make one programme, Bang Goes the Theory. ([Public Accounts Committee](https://www.parliament.uk/external/committees/committee-news-pre-oct-2020/2014/april/bbc-dmi-report-substantive/)) Auditor Amyas Morse's exact sentence: “The BBC Executive did not have sufficient grip on its Digital Media Initiative programme.” ([NAO](https://www.nao.org.uk/briefings/memorandum-on-the-bbcs-digital-media-initiative/))

P2-C. FBI Virtual Case File: stop, then start another procurement. CONFIRMED: The DOJ Inspector General records that the FBI ended the three-year, $170 million VCF effort in March 2005 and announced Sentinel; that is the VCF amount, not Sentinel's final cost. ([DOJ OIG](https://oig.justice.gov/archives/reports/FBI/a0614/exec.htm)) The inspected document is a pre-acquisition Sentinel audit and does not establish its later outcome, VCF's original comparable budget, a calculable VCF schedule overrun, or a named executive's suitable short quotation; do not backfill those from this source. ([DOJ OIG](https://oig.justice.gov/archives/reports/FBI/a0614/exec.htm))

P2-D. Queensland Health payroll: commercial components did not remove integration risk. CONFIRMED, inquiry dated 31 July 2013: The state programme used SAP and Workbrain and shifted to IBM as prime contractor; the $98 million IBM contract and October 2008 $181 million completion forecast covered a changing, broader scope and are not a clean final payroll-only overspend series. ([Commission of Inquiry](http://www.healthpayrollinquiry.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0014/207203/Queensland-Health-Payroll-System-Commission-of-Inquiry-Report-31-July-2013.pdf)) The payroll goal of 31 July 2008 became a 14 March 2010 go-live, approximately 19.5 months late, ESTIMATE from those dates; the inquiry estimated the resulting system would cost about A$1.2 billion over the next eight years, not that the initial build alone cost that much. ([Commission of Inquiry](http://www.healthpayrollinquiry.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0014/207203/Queensland-Health-Payroll-System-Commission-of-Inquiry-Report-31-July-2013.pdf)) The report describes ten aborted delivery attempts and about one thousand staff processing fortnightly pay; a complete, short executive quotation was not verified, and contractor/reviewer warnings should not be attributed to the CEO. ([Commission of Inquiry](http://www.healthpayrollinquiry.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0014/207203/Queensland-Health-Payroll-System-Commission-of-Inquiry-Report-31-July-2013.pdf))

P2-E. Birmingham City Council: buying Oracle, then customising, then reimplementing. REPORTED, 29 January 2026: The council's SAP-to-Oracle programme began in 2018; reporting describes an initially out-of-the-box intention followed by customisations, including a failed banking-reconciliation system, and a fresh Oracle implementation. ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/29/birmingham_oracle_latest)) The initial £19 million plus £1 million contingency compares with a £144.4 million forecast through 2027/28, not a final actual; ESTIMATE: £124.4 million above the £20 million baseline, or 622%, calculated as . P2-F. Rural Payments Agency: the workflow was harder than the software plan. CONFIRMED, 18 October 2006: Implementation costs rose from a £76 million budget to £122 million by March 2006, with more expected; ESTIMATE: £46 million, or 60.5%, above budget at that snapshot, not final outturn. P2-I. Lidl: bought software can become bespoke software too. What this part does not prove

The recent AI-cancellation sample is too thin to estimate a success or reversal rate. It would be misleading to add Klarna again to reach a quota, relabel engineering founders as non-engineering executives, or claim government integrator failures prove that an off-the-shelf package would have worked unchanged.

Amounts remain in original currencies. Dollar-converting historical sterling or euro programme numbers without an explicitly chosen exchange date would add false precision.

Part 3: Price rises, changing meters and renewal traps

The ranked percentage table at the end contains comparable price movements and clearly flagged customer reports. The following cases explain why the biggest economic changes often cannot honestly be represented by one universal percentage.

Licensing and packaging changes

VMware/Broadcom: CONFIRMED vendor announcement: perpetual-product sales and support/subscription renewals were being ended in favour of subscription/term offers; Broadcom also said it halved the previous VMware Cloud Foundation subscription list price, without supplying a customer-equivalent old/new dollar comparison. ([Broadcom](https://www.broadcom.com/company/news/articles/cloud/vmware-by-broadcom-business-transformation)) REPORTED: Beeks described a tenfold licence bill and moved most of more than twenty thousand VMs to OpenNebula; AT\&T disputed a proposed 1,050% increase and later reached a settlement whose economic terms were not disclosed. ([Ars Technica, 2 December 2024](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2024/12/company-claims-1000-percent-price-hike-drove-it-from-vmware-to-open-source-rival/), [CIO Dive, 22 November 2024](https://www.ciodive.com/news/broadcom-att-vmware-settlement-licensing-support-lawsuit/733763/)) Do not call either customer anecdote a universal list-price rise, or AT\&T's disputed proposal a realised invoice.

Oracle Java: CONFIRMED, 1 March 2023 price list: Java SE Universal Subscription is $15 per employee per month for 1–999 employees, $12 for 1,000–2,999, $10.50 for 3,000–9,999, $8.25 for 10,000–19,999 and $6.75 for 20,000–29,999; the quantity includes defined supporting contractors and is not merely the count of Java users. Maintenance, migration, training and negotiation: what is actually measured?

Lifetime maintenance share: No defensible universal current percentage was established. The 1981 Lientz/Swanson study covered 487 data-processing organisations, but the abstract's approximately 60% concerns common problem variance explained by user knowledge, not 60% of lifetime software cost. CONFIRMED study scope, not a maintenance-cost benchmark. ([ACM paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/358790.358796))

Historical maintenance review: A 19 June 2003 review repeats a textbook statement of maintenance at 50–75% of system-development cost and discusses heterogeneous historical studies; that is not a newly measured share of lifetime total cost, nor a safe input for a 2026 enterprise model. CONFIRMED review content; secondary historical synthesis. ([Research review](https://static.aminer.org/pdf/PDF/000/364/724/estimating_the_costs_of_software_maintenance_tasks.pdf))

Observed migration durations, not a universal average: Named accounts give approximately one month for SendinBlue, twelve weeks for Capture, a couple of months for Octave, and a five-month first phase for Intercom; scope differs and no comparable migration-dollar dataset accompanies them. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claims, dates as specified in Part 1. ([Zendesk roundup](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/build-vs-buy/), [Capture](https://www.zendesk.com/customer/capture/), [Octave](https://getmeasure.com/customer-story/octave), [Intercom](https://stripe.com/customers/intercom))

Training home-built versus familiar software: No measured, generalisable estimate of additional new-hire onboarding hours or days was found. The model therefore budgets training as an explicit assumption on both sides, not a fabricated home-built penalty.

Build/switch negotiation leverage: Tropic publishes negotiated uplift data, but no causal percentage specifically attributable to showing a credible in-house replacement was established; concessions on the increase are not the same as cutting the entire bill. CONFIRMED publisher claim with missing causal attribution, observed November 2026. ([Tropic](https://www.tropicapp.io/reports/software-spending-trends-2025))

If all two hundred use one account's allowance, add the $27/year ESTIMATE calculated above plus any integration labour. ([Cognito rate](https://aws.amazon.com/cognito/pricing/))

No separate SOC 2 audit per app: assumed existing company-level audit, with application readiness labour budgeted instead. If the build introduces an incremental audit, add the scoped audit quote rather than assuming either zero enterprise compliance or a full audit for every tool. ([Audit scope/price anchor](https://auditsuisse.com/soc-2-pricing))

Switching: both options must deliver changed workflow requirements. The build replaces an existing purchased tool and retains it for three assumed months while data and users move; buy retains and reconfigures the reference service, with its full-year fee already included. The buy-side transition allowance covers data cleanup and refresher training, not cross-system migration; the build-side allowance covers migration and retraining. An unchanged renewal without this assumed reconfiguration/training project would cost less than the buy scenario shown.

Year definitions: year 3 means the annual run rate in year 3, not the sum of three years; cumulative three-year total is , with assumed. No inflation, discounting, revenue upside or productivity windfall is assumed.

Functional boundary: these are scoped contact/opportunity CRM, human-agent ticketing and permission-based email-campaign tools, not recreations of every vendor feature. Advanced AI agents, enterprise telephony, marketplace apps, high-volume integrations, sophisticated attribution, unusual regulation and premium support are excluded unless explicitly budgeted; a real procurement must test feature equivalence.

The marketing subscription is especially uncertain: Mailchimp displays $350/month for twelve months, but the inspected page does not reliably expose the post-promotion price. Keeping $350/month in years 2–3 is an ESTIMATE assumption, not a verified renewal quote; the model also assumes that displayed dollar price applies to a US purchase. ([Mailchimp offer](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/premium-details/))

Sensitivity and decision use

The models are not a finding that these are typical build durations. Their purpose is to reveal what must be true for a build to pay back.

Three-year engineering break-even, ESTIMATE: the CRM model's non-engineering build costs are $98,050.20, leaving $150,600 of the \*\*$248,650.20\*\* buy total available for all engineering over three years, about 0.735 FTE-years total at $205,000; the support and marketing models leave no positive engineering budget after their assumed non-engineering build costs. This is a scenario calculation, not a universal threshold; the exact checked working is preserved in the companion calculation file.

AI acceleration, ESTIMATE: halving only initial build engineering does not halve total ownership cost, because maintenance, security, business time, migration and operations remain. In the CRM case, that removes $153,750 from the model's build total, not half of every line.

Value can justify building: if the custom workflow creates a commercially material capability unavailable from a vendor, total cost alone is not the decision. Record that benefit separately rather than disguising it as free engineering.

The ten funniest true details

These are sourced details, not embellished punchlines. The humour should not erase a missing cost, a vendor's incentive or a genuine reason for the original decision.

The digital-media programme made one programme: Parliament says BBC DMI was used for Bang Goes the Theory, after a net £98.4 million cost; the title is a gift, but the cost is the programme's net cost, not a production budget for that episode. CONFIRMED, parliamentary account dated 10 April 2014. ([Public Accounts Committee](https://www.parliament.uk/external/committees/committee-news-pre-oct-2020/2014/april/bbc-dmi-report-substantive/))

The expensive catalogue was also more cumbersome: DMI's catalogue had 163 regular users and annual running costs of £3 million, compared with £780,000 for the old system. CONFIRMED, 2014 parliamentary account. ([Public Accounts Committee](https://www.parliament.uk/external/committees/committee-news-pre-oct-2020/2014/april/bbc-dmi-report-substantive/))

Billing needed its own weekly engineering rota: one or two of Intercom's seven senior engineers dealt with billing errors and operational issues each week before the Stripe consolidation. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim, migration completed by October 2025. ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/customers/intercom))

Buying looked more expensive if you only read the invoices: Octave disclosed approximately $17,000/year for Stripe versus a $20,000/year Measure contract, after two years of owning the engineering burden. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim, February 2026 account. ([Measure](https://getmeasure.com/customer-story/octave))

The escape still required a 2 a.m. cutover: Octave's migration was a couple of months of engineering work, not a credit-card swipe that made the old complexity disappear. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim, February 2026 account. ([Measure](https://getmeasure.com/customer-story/octave))

The payroll engine was thirty-five thousand lines of COBOL: Texas A\&M's Workday story describes that legacy engine and code up to thirty-five years old, without disclosing what it cost to replace. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim, undated page observed November 2026. ([Workday](https://www.workday.com/en-us/customer-stories/q-z/texas-a-m-moves-to-cloud-automates-finance-hr.html))

Microservices produced a three-person keep-it-alive assignment: Segment's own account says three engineers were spending most of their time keeping the system alive before its monolith move. CONFIRMED company account, July 2018. ([Segment](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/best-practices/goodbye-microservices))

A razor company was maintaining a software platform: Dollar Shave Club says roughly forty per cent of technology resources went into maintaining its home-grown shop; do not turn that into an audited cash-loss claim. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim, undated page observed November 2026. ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/dollar-shave-club))

Oracle's own example includes five thousand people beyond its direct workforce: the price-list example bills twenty-eight thousand defined employees, including agents, contractors and consultants, for $2.268 million/year. CONFIRMED, 1 March 2023 price list; this is Oracle's illustration, not a named company's invoice. ([Oracle](https://www.oracle.com/a/ocom/docs/corporate/pricing/java-se-subscription-pricelist-5028356.pdf))

The lab building Claude bought the customer-service agent: Anthropic's support leaders describe selecting Fin, deploying in under a week and then doing ongoing content optimisation themselves. CONFIRMED vendor/customer claim, undated case observed 17 November 2026; the bill is not public and buying did not eliminate upkeep. ([Fin/Anthropic](https://www.intercom.com/customers/anthropic))
