# Narrative lock: Fri 20 Nov mind.the.gap - the future of AI after the midterms

Publishes: 2026-11-20

## Surface story
In the US midterms on 3 November, towns in 10 states voted on new data centres 41 times, and in 40 of those votes people chose to limit them or turned them down. Within days OpenAI promised to pay the full cost of powering its own data centres.

## Deeper story
The midterms turned AI into a kitchen-table vote about electricity bills. Whether AI gets built in America, and how fast and how cheaply, is now being decided by voters and local politicians, not by Washington or the tech companies. So the builders are looking for ways round, and those ways will change what AI looks like. In Krish's words, the piece is "about the future of AI after the midterms", and he would "rather not make predictions about politics".

## Arc
- Hook: towns across America held 41 votes on new AI data centres, and 40 of them said no.
- Why people vote no: a big data centre needs a lot of power, and people fear it pushes up their electricity bill.
- The midterms took that fight onto the ballot, and AI became a kitchen-table issue.
- So the builders look for a way round. Three of them: AI builds its own power, AI spreads out into small sites, AI shrinks onto your own devices.
- Each way round has a catch: gas plants bring their own fights, small sites do less, small models do less.
- The trend: AI gets smaller, closer to you, and runs on its own power. Where it is built is now part of what you are buying.
- The action: ask your AI supplier where its new computing is being built.

## The fun
The working idea, held open until Krish sees it moving ("Sounds good, but let's wait till we see what it looks like in real life."): one running image. A giant data centre is turned away from town after town, then shrinks to get back in: first as its own power station, then as a box beside a solar farm, then inside the phone in your pocket. Every line is said plainly, the way you would say it to a friend; no clever phrasing (Krish, 9 Nov: "It's really important we aren't trying to cryptically confuse everyone.").

## The use
Ask your AI supplier where its new computing is being built. Krish: "I think it's just asking where its new computing is being built. The leader can probably figure out the rest from there."

## His close
No prediction about politics. The close follows the three alternatives to big data centres (their own power, small sites, your own devices; space left out) to the trend they point to. Krish: "I think it's worth digging into what the alternatives are to data centers and, therefore, what AI trends we might see as a result. I'd rather not make predictions about politics." His own closing line is said by him on camera, in his words.
