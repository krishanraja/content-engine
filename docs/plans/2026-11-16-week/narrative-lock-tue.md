# Narrative lock: Tue 17 Nov mind.the.gap - the future of AI after the midterms

piece_id: b019dbf8-8051-4cab-9b5e-2ad77b3f5118
Publishes: 2026-11-17

Rewritten 17 Nov to Krish's note on the board: "Make this 11 out of 10: fun, tongue-in-cheek, and surprisingly informative for any layperson." The spine, The use and His close are his from 9 Nov and are unchanged; the tone, the arc's beats, The fun and the facts are new.

## Surface story
America has a new least-wanted neighbour. In a Gallup poll this spring, 71% of Americans said they did not want an AI data centre built near them; only 53% said the same about a nuclear power plant. Then on 3 November, towns in 10 states held 41 votes on data centres, 21 of them in Ohio, and 40 went against the data centre. Within days OpenAI promised, again, to pay the full cost of powering its own data centres: a promise it first made in January.

## Deeper story
The midterms turned AI into a vote about the electricity bill. Whether AI gets built in America, and how fast and how cheaply, is now being decided by voters and local councils; Washington and the tech companies no longer get the last word. So the builders are looking for ways round, and those ways round will change what AI looks like and where it lives. In Krish's words, the piece is "about the future of AI after the midterms", and he would "rather not make predictions about politics".

## Arc
- Hook: more Americans would rather live next to a nuclear power plant than an AI data centre. Then 41 towns got a vote, and 40 said no.
- What a data centre actually is, in one breath: a warehouse full of computers that runs the AI on your phone, and the big ones use as much electricity as a small city.
- Why people vote no: that power has to come from the same grid as theirs, and they think they will pay for it on their bill.
- The midterms put that fight on the ballot. AI stopped being a tech story and became a kitchen-table one.
- OpenAI's answer: "we'll pay for our own power". It said the same thing in January. Saying it twice is what you do when nobody believed you the first time.
- So the builders look for a way round. Three of them, each smaller than the last: bring your own power station, move into lots of small sites, move into the phone in your pocket.
- Each way round has a catch: a gas plant is its own fight with the neighbours, a small site does less, a small model does less.
- The trend: AI gets smaller, closer to you, and runs on its own power. Where it is built is now part of what you are buying.
- The action: ask your AI supplier where its new computing is being built.

## The fun
One running joke, carried the whole way: the data centre as the neighbour nobody wants. It opens with the ranking (less welcome than a nuclear plant, and the pictures show both side by side with the two numbers). It keeps getting turned away, town after town on the map. So it tries to get back in by shrinking: first it turns up with its own power station, like a guest who brings their own generator to the party; then it splits into small boxes parked beside solar farms; last it moves into your pocket, where nobody gets a vote. The tongue-in-cheek lives in what happens and in Krish's delivery, never in clever wording: every line is said plainly, the way you would say it to a friend (Krish, 9 Nov: "It's really important we aren't trying to cryptically confuse everyone."). The surprise for a layperson is that each joke carries a real fact they did not know: the nuclear comparison, the 40 of 41, the promise made twice, the gas plants. The look stays open until Krish sees it moving ("Sounds good, but let's wait till we see what it looks like in real life.").

## The use
Ask your AI supplier where its new computing is being built. Krish: "I think it's just asking where its new computing is being built. The leader can probably figure out the rest from there."

## His close
No prediction about politics. The close follows the three ways round big data centres (their own power, small sites, your own devices; space left out) to the trend they point to. Krish: "I think it's worth digging into what the alternatives are to data centers and, therefore, what AI trends we might see as a result. I'd rather not make predictions about politics." His own closing line is said by him on camera, in his words.
