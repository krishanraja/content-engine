## The five stories

1. **Vocality** (The Canadian Press via MTL Blog, 11 Nov 2026, 4:50 PM).
   https://www.mtlblog.com/woman-with-als-hears-her-own-voice-again
   - Anne-Marie Poirier, Montreal, has ALS; "She began losing her voice in October 2025".
   - Vocality "allows her to turn a few keywords into complete sentences, spoken aloud in a voice resembling her own".
   - "Using recordings of their speech, technology developed by AI company ElevenLabs can create a synthetic voice that
     Vocality uses to read messages aloud."
   - "More than 100 people across North America now use the application daily, according to Houlachi."
   - "Vocality is now available in six languages". Built by "two young Montreal entrepreneurs" (Thomas Houlachi, CEO;
     David Ruiz, CTO). Paid subscription.
   - Photo: The Canadian Press / Christinne Muschi, Poirier with both co-founders and her iPad, 10 Nov 2026 (real).
2. **Thatsign** (Techpoint Africa, 12 Nov 2026, Asukwo Oduo).
   https://techpoint.africa/feature/thatsign-to-bridge-communication-gap/
   - "Thatsign works as a two-way translation"; speech to signs in real time, and the child's signs "back to text or
     speech for the parent".
   - "The current prototype covers BSL"; "Thatsign, a prototype developed by Techies Africa, a Lagos- and London-based
     technology company"; "expected to go live around mid-2027"; "knows 13 everyday signs".
   - Jephthah Adeleke, a deaf teacher, "rates it 9 out of 10".
   - NDCS (UK) "estimates that more than nine in 10 deaf children are born to hearing parents".
   - Photo: "Confidence Ugolo working on Thatsign dashboard | Source: Supplied" (real; the founder, not users).
3. **Glide** (Glidance, press release dated Seattle 9 Nov, posted 10 Nov 2026).
   https://www.glidance.io/news/glide-wins-2027-ces-innovation-awards-in-two-categories
   - "the first AI-powered intelligent guide for people who are blind or have low vision"; CES Innovation Awards 2027
     Honoree in two categories "as it rolls out to its first customers".
   - "Glide's two all-terrain wheels steer and brake ... guides users around obstacles and hazards"; "users can ask Glide
     to describe what's around them".
4. **Google glasses, Guided Vision** (Google blog, 12 Nov 2026).
   https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/
   - Guided Vision "offers conversational audio descriptions to people who are blind, have low vision ... as they read
     small print or find everyday items".
   - Pre-orders open; on sale "starting on December 10". Google: "not a medical device ... mobility aid ... (such as white
     cane)"; at launch only with a compatible Android phone. The card claims describing and reading only.
5. **Visionably** (The Better India, 16 Nov 2026, 19:18 IST, Nishtha Kawrani).
   https://thebetterindia.com/innovation/ai-tool-makes-pdfs-accessible-for-the-blind-gurugram-visionably-yash-bhatnagar-12814698
   - "17-year-old Yash Bhatnagar to build Visionably, an AI tool turning inaccessible PDFs into documents screen readers
     can actually understand"; "Why should a blind person need someone else to read their own bank statement?"
   - "the engine now succeeds on around 95% of the PDFs it is tested on"; "more than 1,000 test cases".

## The wider trend (outside the story)

World Health Organization, fact sheet "Assistive technology".
https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/assistive-technology
- "Globally, more than 2.5 billion people need one or more assistive products."
- "With an ageing global population and a rise in noncommunicable diseases, an estimated 3.5 billion people will need
  assistive technology by 2050."
- "In many countries, most people who need assistive technology do not have access to it."
- Barriers include "low awareness, high costs, limited physical access".
