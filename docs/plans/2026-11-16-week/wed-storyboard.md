## The one idea, and the look

**Headline against reality.** Every big claim arrives as a headline, loud and kinetic. Then a terminal runs the check, and
brackets lock onto what actually happened. The comedy lives in the gap between the two, played straight. The only
recurring props are a small set: the HEADLINE / WHAT HAPPENED readout, the rent meter, and the 2am phone. The Crocodile
Hunter is now a cameo for his intro and two short callbacks.

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Look | `kinetic-movement`, his press on 17 Nov: fast phrase cuts, words bouncing in, constant motion. In the 5-minute cut it carries the headlines and the jokes, and the evidence beats slow down so a page can be read. |
| Motion | `hud-terminal`, his press: IBM Plex Mono on ink, mint for the result line, lock-on brackets that slap onto the one value that matters, numbers that roll. It is the "what happened" voice of the whole piece. |
| Captions | `rack-focus`, his press: focus travels along the line, and the hero word arrives big from a deep blur. Captions never repeat a line that is on screen as a page (F42), and the marks land on the words they name (F41). |
| Krish | Full frame for the intro, his asides ("Honestly…", "Brilliant!", "I reckon…") and the close. Elsewhere he sits in a small rounded window, bottom right on the wide cut and bottom left on the tall cut. |
| Evidence | Real pages and real logos (Krish, 6 Oct), each marked on the exact words it proves, with its source and date. Evidence words are at least 40 px tall on 9:16 (F44). |
| Title | The spec's `title_line` is "Build or buy your tools in an AI world?". As he says it, the caption words lift out of the caption bar, grow into the title card, hold, then fall back into the captions (Krish, 17 Nov). |
| The HEADLINE / WHAT HAPPENED readout | A two-row terminal. The top row is the headline in bold, and it bounces in. | The rent meter | A small taxi-style meter labelled RENTED SOFTWARE. It rolls up on the bills, and keeps running when Klarna "switches off" its software. |
| The 2am phone | A phone on a bedside table showing 02:00. It rings for whoever owns the tool: your house when you build, and the vendor's desk when you buy. |
| Music | NO CURVES (Instrumental), `no-curves-instrumental`, one bed shape (F40). |

## Facts the fact gate checks before render (new in these scripts)

**Klarna**
- The 2024 headlines said Klarna had replaced Salesforce and Workday with AI. Sources: the 2024 coverage cited in `wed-research.md`. REPORTED.
- Klarna's boss on X, 4 March 2025: "we did not replace SaaS with an LLM". Source: x.com/klarnaseb. CONFIRMED.
- Klarna uses Deel, another rented product, for HR, and other rented tools for CRM. It still uses Slack, which Salesforce owns. Source: CX Today, citing a Klarna statement. REPORTED. Check "still" is current.
- Klarna built an internal data layer to bring its data together. Source: the same X thread. CONFIRMED.

**Price rise and Curative**
- Some VMware customers say their bill went up ten times after Broadcom bought it. Source: Ars Technica, Dec 2024 (Beeks). REPORTED, and customer-specific.
- Curative cancelled a roughly $600,000 a year Salesforce contract after a two-month vibe-coded CRM build, and keeping it running is hard. Source: Business Insider, 21 Jul 2026. REPORTED.

**The sums and the surveys**
- A help desk costs about six times as much to build as to buy over three years: $395,312 against $64,150 for a 200-person company. Source: our own cost model, filed on the piece. ESTIMATE, and said as one on camera.
- Teams have swapped small tools such as dashboards and admin pages for ones they built. Sources: Retool (817 builders, 35%) and McKinsey (1,719 respondents, 32%). REPORTED.

**Anthropic**
- Anthropic runs finance and people on Workday. Source: Workday release, 13 Oct 2026. CONFIRMED.
- Anthropic runs customers and deals on Salesforce. Source: Anthropic Greenhouse job 5413374008. CONFIRMED.
- Anthropic chose Fin, from Intercom, and was live in under a week; Fin uses Claude; the team says it keeps them focused. Source: intercom.com/customers/anthropic. Vendor story.
