Same $20 a month. One plan gets you about five times more AI than the other,
and you never chose that. Someone behind the counter did. This piece is about
who that someone is, why the deal is that good, and when it stops.

## The story, told once

Opening question, said out loud: **"When you pay $20 for AI, who decides how
much brain you actually get?"**

1. **The receipt.** SemiAnalysis worked out what a month of each $20 plan
   would cost if you paid by the token: about $1,178 for Claude Pro and $211
   for ChatGPT Plus, comparing Claude Opus 5.5 with GPT-6.1 Sol. Same price,
   about five times the AI.
2. **The twist: it is a gift, and gifts end.** Per the same report,
   subscriptions use about 42% of Anthropic's computing power and bring in
   about 10% of its money. A heavy user on the $200 plan costs Anthropic far
   more than they pay. Factual parallel: Uber rides in its early years, when
   investors paid part of your fare (source to file). The $20 is a price
   tag. What you get for it is someone else's choice, and it can change.
3. **The counter-evidence, early, so nobody reads it as an advert.** (a) At the
   top tier, SemiAnalysis calls the two companies' limits "quite similar":
   the five times is a middle-plan result. (b) In June, SemiAnalysis's own
   numbers pointed the other way at $200. (c) Artificial Analysis puts the
   cost of one finished task at $5.98 on Opus 5.5 and $0.72 on GPT-6.1 Sol.
   More dollars of AI does not always mean more work done.
4. **The guy behind the counter changes the scoop without telling you.**
   Keep the old ice cream shop. OpenAI's switcher broke in August 2025 and
   "GPT-5 seemed way dumber" (already passed). Anthropic's own write-up of
   23 April 2026: Claude Code's default thinking effort was lowered from
   high to medium on 4 March to save time, and Anthropic called it "the wrong
   tradeoff". Then in June, Fable 5 was built to quietly give worse answers
   to people it thought were building rival AI; after about 48 hours of
   backlash Anthropic said invisible safeguards were "the wrong tradeoff"
   and now shows you when it moves you to Opus 4.8.
5. **What it means for you, the user.** Three things a reader can do on
   Monday: ask the app which brain answered (OpenAI promised to say); watch
   the limits meter, because that is the real price; choose the plan for the
   job, and re-check it, because the deal moves every few months.
6. **What it means if you build on AI.** Do not price your product on a
   subsidised price. Pick the brain per task yourself, the way the 5 to 10
   times saving the Cognition boss described (already passed). Build so you
   can switch, as Walmart did with a tool that swaps between companies'
   brains (source to file).
7. **How the old way is changing, and who gets paid.** Software used to be a
   price for a product. Now the price is fixed and the product underneath it
   moves. Pricing power is moving to buyers (CNBC, passed), and for now the
   companies' investors are paying part of everyone's bill.
8. **Answer to the opening question, then the call, then the outro.** The
   answer: the company does, every day, and it is cheapest for them when you
   do not notice. The call stays as Krish approved it: by 30 September 2027,
   at least two of OpenAI, Anthropic and Google will have their software pick
   the brain automatically, by default, for businesses that build on their
   AI. 75%. Outro: "That's the gap. I'm Krish, this is mind.the.gap, make
   your mind up."

The three forks (Vending Machine, Secret Menu, Group Project) stay, cut to a

## Ten ways to make it far better to read and watch

1. **Open on money, the reader's own.** Everyone pays $20. Starting on the
   receipt makes it about their own wallet.
2. **One picture that does the job of a paragraph: the receipt.** Two till
   receipts side by side, Claude and ChatGPT logos at the top, "You paid:
   $20" and "What it would cost by the token: $1,178" against "$211", and a
   torn-off strip at the bottom: "Who pays the rest: investors, for now".
   It is our receipt device from Monday, and the cover and LinkedIn picture
   build from it with Krish's face.
3. **One interactive on the web page: the plan slider.** Move between $20,
   $100 and $200 and watch the two bars. At $20 and $100 Claude is about five
   times bigger; at $200 the gap shrinks to "quite similar". The point a
   paragraph struggles with, "the five times is only true in the middle",
   lands in one drag.
4. **Say the catch before a critic can.** The counter-evidence comes in the
   first third. It is what makes this mind.the.gap, and it
   matters more because this publication is made with Claude. Say that in
   one line.
5. **Both companies get caught.** OpenAI's switcher broke; Anthropic lowered
   effort and hid a safeguard. Nobody is the villain; the setup is.
6. **The ice cream shop comes back.** Readers of piece 2 already met the guy
   behind the counter; now he also decides the scoop size. Continuity is a
   reward for subscribers.
7. **Three things to do on Monday,** as a box the reader can screenshot.
8. **The video tells the story in its own words.** 75 to 90
   seconds, tall: cold open holding up two printed receipts, "Same price.
   One of these is worth five times the other. Why?", then the twist, the
   catch, the three things, the call, the outro. Logo for the first three
   seconds.
9. **A carousel that is the receipt torn into five strips:** what you paid,
   what you got, who pays the rest, the catch, the call.
10. **Put the call on the scoreboard** on makeyourmindup.ai as "Not due yet"
    the day it publishes, so the reader can come back and check us.
