I've been watching what's coming with AI, before everyone notices. I'm Krish.

Let me start by saying this. The menu of AIs in your apps is a mess, and getting rid of it is a good idea. Open Claude Code and you can pick from more than a dozen. Each one is a different AI engine under the app. Nobody wants to choose from that list. So the apps are starting to choose for you. But who does that choice really serve?

I think the way they're doing it quietly hands the biggest choice to the people selling it to you.

Let me explain. Imagine you run a small business. On Monday you ask your AI app to check a contract. It's brilliant. It spots a clause you missed. On Thursday you ask the same thing, the same way. This time it's sloppy. Nothing changed on your screen. Same app, same name at the top.

What changed is something you can't see. There's now a router inside the app. It reads your question, guesses how hard it is, and sends it to a big engine or a cheap one. On Thursday it guessed easy.

So the menu hasn't gone. It's moved behind the counter. You used to order. Now the kitchen orders for you, and it also sets the price.

This already happened once, in public. In August 2025, OpenAI launched GPT-5 with a router that picked for you. On day one the router broke. Sam Altman, who runs OpenAI, said GPT-5 "seemed way dumber". People were so cross that the old engine came back the next day.

And the amount you get for your money already moves around a lot. One research firm compared the two big plans. On the everyday engines, Claude's plans gave about five times the value of ChatGPT's. Same kind of plan. Very different amounts of brain.

Now take the names away. On a busy day, I'd bet you get the cheaper engine, and you'd never know.

There is another way. An app could print which engine answered, and what it cost, under every reply. That would switch the power back to you.

My prediction. By the end of 2027, the big AI apps won't show you a list of engines unless you ask. You'll get one box.

So we finally learned all their names, just in time for them to disappear.
