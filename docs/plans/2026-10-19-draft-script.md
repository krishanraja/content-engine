I've been following how money is changing because of AI. I'm Krish.

So I've been reading the small print on Gemini 4 Argon, Google's newest AI. It doubles in price on 1 December, and that matters if your business uses it. So I wanted to break it down for you. What does the doubling do to your bill, and what should you check?

To be fair, Google told everyone. It's in a footnote.

Google charges for the words its AI reads and writes. Reading about 700,000 words costs $2 now. From December, it's $4. Writing that much goes from $10 to $20.

Now, there's a trick that makes part of the bill much cheaper. Picture a photocopier. Every customer gets the same 40 pages, then asks one short question. Google's AI spots those same pages. And it charges you 95% less for them. I just call it reuse.

But here's the catch. Lots of businesses think reuse keeps them safe. It doesn't. The cheap part doubles too.

Take a founder who sells a help bot for a flat monthly fee. Say the AI costs them $980 a month. That's with reuse working. From December, it's $1,960. Their customers still pay the same. So the founder pays the gap.

So who gets squeezed? Anyone on Google's normal price who barely reuses. Anyone selling a fixed price product built on it. And anyone whose contract doesn't lock the price.

And Google? My read is that the low price got people building. Once you've built on one AI, moving is hard. From December, it costs the same as a big rival.

So should you switch to a cheaper AI? Maybe. But a cheap one that needs three tries can cost you more. Test it on ten real jobs first. Then count the cost of each finished job.

So my prediction is that Google keeps the low price, or offers a new discount, before 1 December. I'd only give that 15%. Google wrote the footnote, and I think it sticks to it.

So here's what to do on Monday. Find whoever runs your AI. Ask how much of what you send gets reused. Then ask if your contract locks the price, or follows Google's price from 1 December. Get both answers before you change anything.
