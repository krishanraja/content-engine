I've been following how money is changing because of AI. I'm Krish.

So I've been reading the details on Gemini 4 Argon, Google's newest AI. Its price doubles on 1 December. That matters if your firm uses it, so I wanted to break it down for you. What does that do to your AI bill, and what should you check?

To be fair, Google said so on launch day, in a note at the bottom of its post.

Now, here's how the bill works. Firms pay Google every time its AI reads or writes words. Reading about 700,000 words costs $2 today. From December, it's $4. Writing that many words goes from $10 to $20.

Now, there's a discount. Take a help bot. Every time a customer asks it something, it first rereads the same long rulebook. And Google spots that the rulebook is a repeat. So it charges 95% less for the repeated part. I call that reuse.

But here's the catch. Lots of firms think that discount protects them from the price rise. It won't. The discount price doubles too.

Take a founder who sells a help bot. Their customers pay the same amount every month. Say Google charges the founder $980 a month, even with the discount. From December, it's $1,960. And the customers still pay the same. So the founder loses the difference.

So who loses most? Firms paying the price on Google's website, with little discount. Firms selling AI products for a set monthly price. And firms whose deal with Google doesn't fix the price.

What about Google? I think the low price got people to build on its AI. And once you've built on one AI, switching is slow and costly. And from December, it costs the same as Claude Opus, a rival AI.

So should you switch to a cheaper AI? Maybe. But a cheaper one that needs three tries can cost you more. So test it on ten real jobs first. Then add up what each finished job costs.

So my prediction is that Google keeps the low price, or offers a new discount, before 1 December. I think there's only a 15% chance of that. I reckon Google sticks to its plan.

So here's what to do on Monday. Find whoever runs AI where you work. Ask how much of your bill gets that 95% discount. Then ask if your deal with Google fixes the price, or goes up on 1 December. Get both answers before you change anything.
