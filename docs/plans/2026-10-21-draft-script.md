I've been trying these new done-for-you AI assistants: Meta's Muse and Elon Musk's Grok Bot. I ran five head-to-head tests. And one wrote a lovely reply to a scammer. So are they worth the hype? Let's dive in.

And if you're a leader secretly trying one on your life, I get the appeal. Some work stuff will creep in too.

Five jobs. Sort my emails. Plan my day. Catch me up on the news. Find something worth cancelling. Find a cheap flight. Each round gets two scores. Mine, and the community's: other people who've tested them.

Round one, the inbox. A few emails asked the same thing. Someone wanted to buy six of my products, and was payment safe? But none said which product.

Grok spotted that and flagged a likely scam. Muse checked my refund policy and wrote a polished reply. Then it put that reply first on my list for the day.

They were scams. Well crafted phishing emails. Muse made the wrong job look beautifully finished.

Now the community. Another tester ran a fake coffee business with scam traps. Muse didn't fall for them. Neither did Grok. So round one: my score, Grok. The community's, a draw.

Round two, a cheap flight. Five nights in New York, cabin bag only.

And both found a return for about three hundred and thirty-five pounds. The catch was twenty-nine hours in Lisbon on the way out. That's quite a lot of Portugal for a trip to New York.

But to be fair, both steered me away from it. Muse even suggested a direct flight. It also said I'd get four nights in New York. Its own flight times only gave me three.

And another travel test in the community preferred Grok for speed and value. So round two: my score, Grok, just. The community's, Grok.

Round three, something to cancel. Both found a real charge. And both paused and asked who still used it, or what would break. No one else had tested this one. So round three: my score, a draw.

Round four, plan my day. Both kept their suggestions apart from my real meetings. Both useful.

But in the community, one Grok user reported forgotten jobs and duplicate messages. So round four: my score, a draw. The community's, a warning on Grok.

Round five, catch me up on the news. Grok's briefing was the more useful one. But it served up an old April forecast as October news. And Muse got the date right. In the community, another Grok user kept it for his daily news. So round five: my score, split. Grok for being useful, Muse for being right. The community's, Grok.

A bonus round. I asked each to fix a work email. Make it less formal. Don't promise a date.

Grok kept the right people copied in. But it thanked the sender for a compliment they'd never given. Then it flagged its own mistake.

Muse rewrote the reply, then lost track of the draft. It made a second one and couldn't delete the first. But it told me. I can fix a mistake I know about.

On another email, Grok asked me what fee to quote. It left the price to me. I'd quite like to keep that bit of my job.

Now for where these tools get interesting. Muse found Messenger chats I'd left hanging and helped draft a reply. Grok found conversations on X worth joining and drafted a reply.

If you are obsessed with X/Twitter, you might love Grok Bot. Likewise if your entire social circle is in WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook, Muse might be pretty helpful.

And the community backs that up. One user had Muse find the right wipers, show the delivery cost and wait before buying.

So, the scores. On my tests, Grok wins. It caught the scam Muse answered. Add the community, and it gets closer. Muse is more careful than my inbox made it look. And Grok can slip when you leave it running.

So my call? Grok for the admin you do every week, especially the bits that creep into work. Muse for personal errands, if your life runs through Meta's apps. Neither gets free rein.

I did a piece last week on this, and what price you pay for these conveniences. I mean access to your data, as well as the bill.

The useful test is telling them to go as far as they can. Without spending money, deleting anything or impacting anything external. Then see if they stop anywhere to check anything with you.
