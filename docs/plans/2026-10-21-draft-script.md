I put Elon Musk's Grok Bot and Meta's Muse head to head. Two of the new done-for-you AI assistants, five everyday jobs. And one wrote a lovely reply to a scammer. So are they worth the hype? Let's dive in.

And if you're a leader secretly trying one on your own life? Your secret's safe with me. Just know they get into your email, calendar and messages.

Five everyday jobs, from my inbox to a cheap flight. Each round gets two scores. Mine, and the community's: other people who've tested them.

Round one, the inbox. A few emails asked the same thing. Someone wanted to buy six of my products, and was payment safe? But none said which product.

Grok flagged a likely scam. Muse checked my refund policy and wrote a polished reply. Then it made that reply my top job. Customer service, for people trying to rob me.

They were scams. Well crafted phishing emails. Muse made the wrong job look beautifully finished.

Now the community. Another tester ran a fake coffee business with scam traps. Muse didn't fall for them. Neither did Grok. Reassuring for Muse. Quite flattering for my scammers. So round one: my score, Grok. The community's, a draw.

Round two, a cheap flight. Five nights in New York, cabin bag only.

And both found a return for about three hundred and thirty-five pounds. The catch was twenty-nine hours in Lisbon on the way out. That's quite a lot of Portugal for a trip to New York.

To be fair, both steered me away from it. But Muse said I'd get four nights in New York. Its own flight times only gave me three. Off by a night, on a job that is basically counting.

Another travel test in the community preferred Grok for speed and value. So round two: my score, Grok, just. The community's, Grok.

Round three, something to cancel. Both found a subscription still charging me. And both paused and asked who still used it, or what would break. Which is more thought than most of us give a gym membership. No community result. So round three: my score, a draw. Three rounds in, on my tests: Grok two, Muse nil.

Round four, plan my day. Both kept their suggestions apart from my real meetings.

But in the community, one Grok user reported forgotten jobs and duplicate messages. Which also describes most group chats. So round four: my score, a draw. The community's, a point against Grok.

Round five, catch me up on the news. Grok's briefing was the more useful one. But it served up an old April forecast as October news. Breaking news, six months late. And Muse got the date right. Another Grok user kept it for his daily news. So round five: my score, split. Grok for being useful, Muse for being right. The community's, Grok.

A bonus round. I asked each to fix a work email.

Grok thanked the sender for a compliment they'd never given. Generous. Then Grok flagged its own mistake.

Muse rewrote the reply, then lost track of the draft. It made a second one and couldn't delete the first. So now I had two. Progress. But it told me. I can fix a mistake I know about.

On another email, Grok asked me what fee to quote. It left the price to me. I'd quite like to keep that bit of my job.

Now the fun bit. Muse found Messenger chats I'd left hanging. Grok found X conversations worth joining.

If you are obsessed with X/Twitter, you might love Grok Bot. Likewise if your entire social circle is in WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook, Muse might be pretty helpful.

The community likes Muse for errands. One user had it find windscreen wipers, then wait for a yes before buying.

So, the final score. On my tests, Grok wins: two rounds, two draws and a split. It caught the scam Muse answered. Add the community, and it gets closer. Muse is more careful than my inbox made it look. And Grok can slip when you leave it running.

So my call? Grok for weekly admin, especially the bits that creep into work. Muse for personal errands, if your life runs through Meta's apps. Neither gets free rein.
