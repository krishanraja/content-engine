Key Takeaways
Key Takeaways
Media Nov Records removed a creator’s folk song recording using automated DMCA tools without proven ownership.
YouTube rejected two counter-notifications before a journalist’s inquiry prompted Media Nov to withdraw its complaint.
Structural asymmetry lets claimants file takedowns instantly while creators bear the burden of proving their own ownership.
Samantha Shokin recorded herself singing a traditional Bulgarian folk song. Four years and roughly 125,000 views later, a company she had never heard of had it removed.
During the pandemic, Shokin recorded an approximately 50-second a cappella version of “Bre Petrunko,” a traditional Bulgarian folk song described by 404 Media as having no known writer. She uploaded it to SoundCloud, then to YouTube, where it accumulated roughly 125,000 views over four years, according to 404 Media.
In October, YouTube removed the video after a copyright complaint from Media Nov Records.
What Media Nov Records Appears to Be
404 Media described a company built around AI imagery, automated rights tools, and a claimed history that corporate records did not support.
According to 404 Media, Media Nov described itself as a music-distribution and rights-management company operating approximately 500 channels. The outlet reported seeing AI-generated imagery and repetitive electronic music across the channels it examined.
Media Nov’s website referenced automated DMCA takedowns, YouTube Content ID management, and AI-powered content recognition. Corporate records indicated a recent founding, according to 404 Media, despite website claims suggesting a much older history.
Two Counter-Notifications. Both Declined.
Shokin followed YouTube’s established process and was turned away twice before a journalist’s inquiry changed the outcome.
Shokin submitted two counter-notifications to YouTube. The platform declined both.
The video remained offline until 404 Media contacted Media Nov for comment. The company then asked Shokin to supply original audio files, recording evidence, and publication dates to verify her ownership of the recording she had made herself.
Minutes later, Media Nov withdrew the complaint. YouTube restored the video roughly 24 hours after the withdrawal, with a spokesperson confirming the restoration followed the claimant’s withdrawal, as reported by 404 Media.
A Discrepancy Worth Noting
Media Nov’s explanation for withdrawing the complaint conflicts directly with the view count reported by 404 Media.
Media Nov’s stated reason for withdrawal was that the video had generated no revenue or views. 404 Media reported the view count at approximately 125,000.
That contradiction is a matter of documented record. What it reflects about Media Nov’s intent has not been established.
The Copyright Layers Involved
The dispute involves at least three distinct legal interests, and no court has resolved any of them.
The legal picture involves multiple layers. 404 Media described “Bre Petrunko” as an old folk song with no known writer; the status of particular arrangements may differ by jurisdiction and version.
Musician and conductor Philip Koutev popularized a specific arrangement of the song in 1963, according to 404 Media. Media Nov argued that rights in that arrangement must be distinguished from rights in any individual sound recording.
Claiming an interest in an arrangement does not automatically establish ownership over a separate performance recorded independently by another artist. Media Nov stated the withdrawal does not waive any rights and said it intends to pursue legal proceedings.
The System Working as Designed
This case exposes a structural asymmetry: initiating a takedown requires far less than reversing one.
The deeper problem this case surfaces is structural. A claimant can initiate a platform-level removal without first obtaining a court judgment or demonstrating ownership to any independent authority.
In this case, Media Nov asked Shokin to provide original audio files, recording evidence, and publication dates to recover content she had personally made. Automated audio-matching tools can identify similarities between recordings, but they are poorly suited to resolving layered questions about traditional songs, copyrighted arrangements, and independently owned recordings that share the same chain. Hollywood Actors and other entertainers face similar digital content ownership risks in an era of automated rights enforcement.
YouTube restored the video after the journalist’s inquiry preceded the claimant’s withdrawal. The underlying rights questions were not resolved by that outcome.
The Burden Runs Backward
Shokin’s video is back online; the rights dispute, by Media Nov’s own account, is not over.
Shokin’s video is back online. The underlying rights dispute, per Media Nov’s own statements, remains open.
This case illustrates a recurring asymmetry for creators working with traditional material: a takedown can be filed quickly, the counter-notification process can stall, and the creator may be asked to prove ownership of work she personally produced.
