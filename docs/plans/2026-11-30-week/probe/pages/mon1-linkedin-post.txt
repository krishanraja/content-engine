Some amazing Anthropic settlement news. Wizards of the Coast is forgoing its share of the Anthropic payouts and giving all the money to the authors. Letters are going out to writers now, and they note: "Because Wizards is the registered owner of the Works, Wizards filed the claims in its name. While Wizards is the copyright holder, our priority here is to recognize the creators who built these amazing worlds. Dungeons & Dragons became what it is because of the people who wrote for it. So as a thank-you to you and the other authors who helped build this world, Wizards has decided to voluntarily forgo its share of the proceeds and pass its share of the Settlement payments for the Works on to you and the other authors."

Collecting and routing the money will be a bit complicated. Some payments will go out through the Anthropic settlement mechanism. Some of the money Wizards will collect and then pass on.

This is remarkable and amounts to hundreds of thousands of dollars that WotC could have collected going to the writers. And Wizards is doing this at a time when the larger publishers are scrambling to grab as much settlement money as possible from authors. Amazing. This payout will help a lot of writers--folks who certainly deserve to be rewarded for their work.

Conversations at Wizards about the Anthropic settlement have been ongoing for months. (I've been part of these conversations on and off, but the real heavy lifting was done by folks with more clout.) This all came about because some of the writers used what influence they possessed to raise the issue and because the people inside Wizards, up to the highest levels, were willing to consider the situation from more than a financial perspective.

Thanks to all the writers who stepped up here. I don't think this would have happened without those author-prompted discussions, especially the efforts of R.A. Salvatore. Huge credit to the folks at Wizards, in particular Dan Ayoub, who were open to the discussions and then did the generous (and right) thing here. And it was the right thing to do.

Japanese coverage framed this settlement more bluntly than most English outlets. Asahi's headline put it as 海賊版でAI学習, "AI training on pirated copies," and reported that the court found Anthropic had downloaded around 7 million books from pirate sites. Training itself was treated as fair use. The money exists because of how the books were obtained, so it is really compensation for taking them, and passing it to the people who wrote them matches what the payment is for. 

The contrast with the rest of games is stark. The Black Ops I and II re-releases reportedly brought in an estimated $435 million in one month, while original developers had just been laid off and saw nothing from it. Under work-for-hire, the registered owner collects by default. The same default covers game translators: when a localized script is reissued, nothing flows back to the person who wrote it. Wizards chose not to hide behind that default, and that choice is the real news here.
