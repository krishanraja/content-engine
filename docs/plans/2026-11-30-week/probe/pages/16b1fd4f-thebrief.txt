Ubisoft has denied claims that its developers must use generative AI, the type of software that creates text, images or other content from a written prompt.
The company said projects are not required to use generative AI. In a statement reported by Video Games Chronicle, Ubisoft said it encourages its teams to explore where AI can be useful and bring value to their work.
Ubisoft said each team needs to decide whether and how AI technologies make sense for the game it wants to make, and for the experience it wants players to have. The choice, in that description, sits with the project rather than being set across the company.
The denial came alongside news of a new subsidiary, a separate company owned by Ubisoft. Its job is to use AI for gameplay innovation, meaning new ways for game systems and play itself to work. Ubisoft said the subsidiary's team is made up of staff who worked on Teammates, the company's AI shooter technology demo.
A technology demo is a short prototype built to show what a technology can do, rather than a game meant for sale. Teammates showed AI-driven characters in a shooting setting. The new group is built from the people who made it.
For players, the practical question is what this means for the games they buy. Ubisoft's statement describes AI as an option for each team to weigh, not a rule every project must follow. The company has not, in the statement, listed which of its games use AI or how.
Generative AI has become a sensitive subject across the games industry. Many players and developers have raised concerns about its use in art, writing and voice work. Ubisoft's answer is that it wants teams to decide case by case.
