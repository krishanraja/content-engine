Warner Music Group (WMG) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript
Revenue hit $1.864 billion as margin expansion and AI licensing deals fuel 2027 outlook.
By Motley Fool Transcribing – Updated Aug 13, 2026 at 10:08AM EST
+Fool.comonShare

Summarize with AI

Image source: The Motley Fool.

DATE
Thursday, Aug. 6, 2026 at 4:30 p.m. ET
CALL PARTICIPANTS
Chief Executive Officer - Robert Kyncl
Global Controller and Chief Accounting Officer and Acting Chief Financial Officer - Lou Dickler
Head of Investor Relations - Kareem Chin
Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

TAKEAWAYS
Revenue -- $1.864 billion, an increase of 10.4% year over year, or 12.1% when adjusted for digital settlement and distribution termination impacts.
Adjusted OIBDA -- $433 million, representing 16.1% growth driven by strong operating performance, revenue mix, and cost-savings delivery.
Adjusted OIBDA Margin -- 23.2%, expanding 110 basis points due to operating leverage and restructuring benefits.
Adjusted EPS -- $0.51, an increase of 21% from $0.42 in the prior-year quarter.
Recorded Music Revenue -- $1.488 billion, growing 9.9% reflecting strength in subscription streaming and physical sales.
Recorded Music Subscription Revenue -- $758 million, an increase of 12.5% reported or 13.5% when adjusted for the termination of the BMG distribution agreement.
Pricing Impact -- 3.5 percentage points of growth in subscription streaming revenue, resulting from contractual wholesale price increases across major digital service providers.
Recorded Music Ad-supported Revenue -- $243 million, increasing 10.0% reported or 12.0% on an adjusted basis, supported by ad spending related to the FIFA World Cup.
Recorded Music Physical Revenue -- $137 million, rising 15.1% due to catalog success and major releases including Madonna and Don Toliver.
Music Publishing Revenue -- $377 million, growing 12.2% behind market expansion and the impact of new deals and renewals.
Music Publishing Streaming Revenue -- $231 million, an increase of 14.4% reflecting positive industry trends.
Music Publishing Mechanical Revenue -- $19 million, rising 18.8% primarily due to the timing of royalty distributions.
Operating Cash Flow -- $142 million, an increase of 209% from $46 million in the prior-year quarter.
Free Cash Flow -- $114 million, improving from $7 million in the year-ago period behind strong operating results and a 28% decrease in capital expenditures.
Cash and Debt -- $618 million in cash and equivalents against a total debt of $4.710 billion, resulting in net debt of $4.092 billion.
Cost Savings Initiatives -- $200 million in realized savings year to date, with the company remaining on track to reach $300 million in annualized savings by 2027.
Bain Capital Joint Venture -- $650 million deployed for catalog acquisitions out of a total capacity of $1.65 billion.
Operating Cash Flow Conversion -- 55% over the last nine months, meeting management's long-term target range of 50% to 60%.
Market Share -- 0.3 percentage point increase in U.S. streaming share and 0.8 percentage point increase in U.S. new release share year to date.
Fiscal 2026 Guidance -- Management expects margin expansion at the high end of its target range of 150 to 200 basis points.
Capital Expenditures -- $28 million, a decrease of $11 million from the prior-year quarter driven by lower technology and finance transformation costs.
Artist Services and Expanded Rights Revenue -- $224 million, an increase of 14.9% driven by higher merchandising and concert promotion in Japan.
SUMMARY
Warner Music Group (WMG +0.18%) management reported strong financial results for the third quarter of 2026, highlighting a strategy centered on increasing the value of music and improving operational efficiency. The company completed its alignment with major digital service providers on price increases through a renewed deal with Apple, which management expects will contribute to resilient subscription streaming growth. Strategic initiatives include expanding the company's distribution capabilities via the Revelator acquisition and leveraging a joint venture with Bain Capital for disciplined catalog acquisitions. Management confirmed the company remains on track to achieve its long-term financial targets while integrating technology-driven tools to optimize marketing and back-office functions.
CEO Kyncl noted the completion of the Apple deal "completes alignment across all of our major DSP partners around contractual PSM increases."
Acting CFO Dickler projected that AI licensing deals, including a partnership with Suno, would begin providing "material revenue contributions" in fiscal 2027.
The company is using proprietary AI tools to optimize a catalog of over 1 million songs, with Kyncl stating these tools enable care and attention "that was humanly impossible a year ago."
Kyncl explained that the company’s share growth has been "largely organic," distinguishing its performance from competitors who relied more heavily on acquisitions.
The company's joint venture with Bain Capital is targeting a 20% return on investments, focusing on "iconic high-margin catalogs with growth potential."
Kyncl highlighted that the company's U.S. new release streaming share rose despite what he described as "lumpiness to the business quarter-to-quarter."
Management is integrating AI across functional departments, including finance, legal, and HR, to streamline workflows and reduce spend while accelerating decision-making.

INDUSTRY GLOSSARY
ADA: Alternative Distribution Alliance, the independent music distribution arm of Warner Music Group.
Adjusted OIBDA: Adjusted Operating Income Before Depreciation and Amortization, a non-GAAP financial measure used to evaluate core operating performance.
Constant Currency: A financial calculation that excludes the effects of foreign exchange rate fluctuations to compare performance across different periods.
DSP: Digital Service Provider, such as Spotify, Apple Music, or YouTube Music.
Mechanical Revenue: Royalties paid to songwriters and publishers for the right to reproduce a musical composition.
MLC: Mechanical Licensing Collective, a nonprofit organization that administers blanket mechanical licenses to streaming services.
PSM: Per-Subscriber Minimum, a contractual rate that ensures a rights holder receives a minimum amount of revenue for each paying subscriber.
Sync Revenue: Synchronization revenue generated from licensing music for use in visual media, including films, television shows, and advertisements.
