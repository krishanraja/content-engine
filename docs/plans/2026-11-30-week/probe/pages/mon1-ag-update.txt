Anthropic Settlement Update: Payments Start November 23

November 23, 2026

Share

 on Twitter
 (opens in a new tab)

 on Facebook
 (opens in a new tab)

 on Linkedin
 (opens in a new tab)

 via email

Update, November 23, 2026: The first payments from the Bartz v. Anthropic settlement are expected to go out this week.

Key Takeaways

Claims listed in the portal as undisputed after November 20 will be paid out this week:

Only approximately 70 percent ($2,203.56) will be paid out now. This is just the first installment per work. The remainder will be paid out at a later time.

The settlement requires Anthropic to add money to the settlement fund within 30 days of its IPO, which happened last week. Once Anthropic has deposited the remaining funds, we expect the 30 percent balance to go out quickly.

Claims that show up as disputed in the portal will be paid out later:

In cases where the dispute is due to errors or where the only disputes exist on the publisher side (such as two competing publishers or imprints of the same publisher), they will likely be paid in December.

In cases where actual disputes among the claimants remain, you still have time to work them out informally. If the parties cannot resolve a dispute between themselves, you will be able to take it to the Special Master.

We have much more information about payments below, and members can submit a legal request or email staff@authorsguild.org with questions.

Update, November 18, 2026: There is still a great deal of confusion around the notice that authors received last Friday in the Anthropic settlement and this Friday’s deadline. We’ve explained the basics of what you need to know about Friday’s November 20 deadline here.

November 16, 2026: The first payments from the Bartz v. Anthropic settlement are expected to go out during the week of November 23. These first payments will be only about 70 percent of the total amount payable to you because Anthropic has not fully funded the settlement fund, and the remainder will be paid in future payments.

On Friday, November 13, the Settlement Administrator emailed claimants a summary of their allocations and the undisputed claim payments scheduled to go out on November 23. All authors who filed a timely claim should have received this email. If you filed a timely claim and did not receive an email, contact the Settlement Administrator and Class Counsel immediately at the contacts listed below.

When you log in to the portal with your claim and pin numbers, you will see a pop-up which says that it shows all submissions received by November 2 and that you should identify any works that should not be included in the first distribution.

This email update has caused some confusion and concern among authors. The Guild had a meeting with class counsel this weekend and got clarity on many of these issues. Here is what you need to know:

The first payments will only cover claims where the portal shows that all claimants agree on the split (referred to as “Undisputed Claims”).

Check your claims in the portal now. If a work shows as undisputed but you don’t agree with the split (including if you haven’t yet submitted a dispute), you must enter that information in the allocation portal by Friday, November 20, in order to stop the payment. This is what the portal’s pop-up means when it asks you to identify works that should not be included in the first distribution. For instance, if rights reverted to you, but the portal still shows a 50/50 split with the publisher, you need to advise the Settlement Administrator through the portal by or on this Friday, November 20, to retain your right to dispute the publisher’s claim for 50 percent. THIS IS YOUR LAST CHANCE TO CHANGE AN UNDISPUTED CLAIM TO A DISPUTE. YOU CANNOT LATER CHALLENGE THE PAYMENT. Your only recourse at that point will be to get any additional share you believe you are owed from the other party (e.g., publisher). You don’t need to explain or prove your position to stop the payment; you can work it out afterward. But if you do nothing, the payment will go out as shown.

Claims with outstanding disagreements will not be paid out in the first disbursement. Class counsel and the Settlement Administrator are proceeding with the first distribution cautiously; counsel reports that they would rather delay a payment than make an incorrect one, which would be very difficult to claw back. So, if there is any possibility of a dispute over a work—even where the claimants may feel that they have resolved the dispute—the Administrator is not paying those out in this round. That money will be paid once the dispute is resolved and the agreement is communicated to the Settlement Administrator. Class counsel and the Settlement Administrator are working to resolve remaining issues as expeditiously as possible. Disputed claims will not be closed, and the current informal dispute resolution process will continue.

The portal update from November 13 does not appear to reflect all of the agreements and understandings reached with publishers and other rightsholders before November 2. Some authors have reported that they resolved their claims with the publisher, but the correct allocation is not yet reflected on the platform (likely because the Settlement Administrator was not provided with the information in time). Other claims show as disputed even though no one disagrees with the author’s share—for example, where a publisher and its imprint filed separately so the system sees an extra publisher and registers a disagreement, or where an author filed under a pen name and a co-author or publisher filed under the legal name (or vice versa) so the system sees an extra claimant. Changes to the portal do not always appear right away, so continue checking, and contact the Settlement Administrator and class counsel. Guild members can contact us as well.

The first payment will be approximately 70 percent of the amount payable to you.

Initial Payments

The payment going out the week of November 23 is an installment of approximately 70 percent of each claimant’s share, not the full amount. The rest will follow once Anthropic makes its final payment into the settlement fund—about $450 million. That payment is due once Anthropic goes public or receives a significant investment (per the settlement terms, either triggers payment within about 30 days), and in all events no later than September 2027. See our Athropic settlement page for more on the settlement fund.

Payments will be made by the method you chose on your claim form: ACH, Zelle, or check. If you’ve moved or changed bank accounts since you filed, update your information with the Settlement Administrator now.

Settlement payments are taxable income. If the Settlement Administrator has asked you to provide or update your Social Security number or Tax ID Number (TIN), please do so. This is not a scam; the Administrator needs the information to report the payments to the IRS. If you are a foreign claimant, please contact the settlement administrator for tax documentation you will need to submit.

Disputed Claims

Where an author and a publisher (or another claimant) entered conflicting splits for the same work, the claim is marked disputed. November 20 is not a deadline for resolving disputed claims; the parties can still reach agreement after that date without having to wait to go to the Special Master. Do not worry if your work is still marked as disputed and you are not paid the week of November 23. While we don’t yet have a date for the next distribution, class counsel and the administrator are prioritizing disbursal of further payments for claims that are already resolved between the parties or easily resolvable.

The Settlement Administrator is still facilitating communications between claimants to help resolve disputes. If the parties still can’t agree, either one will be allowed to send the dispute to the court-appointed special master at a later date. The special master will decide the split based on the contract and the evidence each side submits.

A number of outstanding disputes are related to out-of-print works where the publisher is claiming that rights have not reverted. The Guild has publicly called on publishers to withdraw their claims from books that have been long out of print (at least 10 years), regardless of whether there is proof that rights have formally reverted under the contract. While some publishers are complying and voluntarily withdrawing claims, many are sticking by the contractual definition of out of print. In these situations, an author can continue the dispute and take it to a special master (knowing, however, that the special master will look at the contract language), or they can accept the 50/50 split before the November 20 deadline to get paid sooner. The Guild will continue to press publishers and help authors make a case for receiving the full amount from works that are long out of print, but we also want to be clear that this may prolong the payment.

“Message from Publisher Co-Claimant” Emails

Some authors have received emails from the Settlement Administrator’s address with the subject line “Anthropic Copyright Settlement – Message from Publisher Co-Claimant.” These pass along a message from a publisher. Passing messages between claimants is part of the Administrator’s role, and it relays messages from authors to publishers as well.

The publisher messages we have seen say the publisher’s records show it is “actively publishing” the author’s book. They describe the 50/50 split as what the settlement contemplates and suggest that authors who claimed 100 percent may have meant to select “sole author” rather than “sole owner.” Many authors and publishers did make that mistake, and tens of thousands of claims have been corrected. But not every author who claimed 100 percent made a mistake, and there are genuine cases where the author should get the 100 percent based on a reversion or out of print status of a book.

It is very important to note that assertions about the in-print or publication status of a work are the publisher’s, and not a determination by the Settlement Administrator. The Administrator does not verify whether a book is in print, and it is not deciding whether rights have reverted. That depends on your contract. In at least one case we know of, the message went to an author whose book has been out of print for decades.

You are not required to change your claim. Publishers’ records on reversions are often unreliable, especially for older books. A publisher may have reverted rights without updating its system, or may never have acknowledged a reversion that was valid under the contract.

We’ll keep updating this page as payments go out and disputes are resolved. Authors Guild members may submit a legal help request for assistance by our team.
