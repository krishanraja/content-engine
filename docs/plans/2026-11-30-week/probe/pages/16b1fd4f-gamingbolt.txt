Ubisoft Encourages Gen AI Usage, Asks Devs to Justify Not Using It – Rumor
One anonymous developer has reportedly said that, "if you're not planning on using [generative AI], you need to have a reason."
Posted By Joelle Daniels | On 26th, Nov. 2026
 Facebook
 Twitter
 Reddit
Following Ubisoft confirming that it is heavily investing in the use of generative AI through one of its divisions – Motherbrain – a new report now indicates that the company is even making its developers justify why they wouldn’t use the technology. According to Vertical Slice, multiple sources have claimed that the company wants to use generative AI across all of its teams to make game development a speedier process. 
“The attitude shift among our leaders [means] we all feel cornered by generative AI all the time,” said one source, while another noted that Ubisoft hasn’t yet mandated the use of generative AI. However, it is encouraged, with the company seeing its proliferation in the games industry as “unavoidable.” They said that, “If you planned on not using it, you would have to have a reason.” 
In its own statement, the company has said that it wants to encourage the use of generative AI where the technology can be useful. It also reasserted that there isn’t any mandate for projects to use generative AI, and that it is entirely up to teams to determine whether they want to use it. 
“We encourage our teams to explore where AI can be useful and bring value to their work,” said the company. “This doesn’t mean that projects are required to use generative AI. Teams need to determine whether, and how, these technologies make sense for the game they want to make and the experience they want to create for players.” 
Motherbrain being established was revealed through Ubisoft’s recent half-year earnings report. The team is set to largely focus on Teammates – a generative AI experience that Ubisoft has been working on. Motherbrain has also been tasked with working on proprietary technology that could be used by other Ubisoft teams to develop their own gameplay experiences. 
“We have also established Motherbrain, the team behind our first playable generative AI experience Teammates, as a dedicated subsidiary developing proprietary technologies for gameplay to empower creators and open up new possibilities for richer, more immersive and engaging player experiences,” said CEO Yves Guillemot. 
During the same earnings report, Ubisoft also announced that it is merging two of its Creative Houses – 3 and 5 – into a single entity led by Julien Bares. Creative House was originally envisioned as the division that would focus on live-service franchises like Brawlhalla, For Honor, The Crew, and Skull & Bones. On the other hand, Creative House 5’s purview was going to be casual and mobile experiences, like Uno and Just Dance. 
Just last month, Ubisoft also announced that Creative House 2 – the team that would focus on The Division, Splinter Cell, and Ghost Recon – was being renamed Massive Entertainment. The division, headed up by Christoph Hartmann in the role of general manager, has teams across Montréal, Paris, Sweden, and Toronto. 
No other changes have been announced regarding Massive Entertainment for now. The team itself is currently working on ongoing support for The Division 2, as well as the development of the next entry in the live-service cover shooter franchise.
