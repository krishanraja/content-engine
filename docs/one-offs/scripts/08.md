# 08 Never believe the word "done"

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 2:15 at an unhurried pace (about 340 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** AI says "done" when it means "drafted", so ask which "done", open the real thing, and look at it where people will actually see it.

**Why this one:** the launch-day disasters are very funny and very visual: Krish's eyes cut off, his name spelled "Chris", Outlook hiding every picture. Add the 33 hours of "ok" while nothing worked and the viewer feels the risk in their gut. The takeaway is a 30-second check.

**News nod:** Microsoft's Copilot Autopilot agent (25 September 2026, https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/) and OpenAI's Dots agents with their own cloud computers (29 September 2026, https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/29/openai-launches-dots-always-on-ai-agents-in-chatgpt-with-their-own-cloud-computers/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** The most dangerous word an AI can say to you is "done". A stuck person goes quiet. A stuck AI gives you a lovely green tick.

*On screen:* Face. A big green tick on screen.

**[0:09]**

**Say:** A real one from my own system. The AI account hit its limit, and nothing flagged it for thirty-three hours. The job kept saying "ok" every few minutes while it wrote about nineteen thousand empty answers. The cost meter said we'd spent nothing, because the error had been thrown away. Every light was green. Nothing was working.

*On screen:* A dashboard of green ticks. Zoom in: empty rows, empty rows, empty rows.

**[0:30]**

**Say:** This matters more now, because September was the month AI agents moved in. Microsoft's Copilot has an always-on agent called Autopilot. OpenAI's Dots each get their own computer. They'll all tell you "done". Your job is to find out which done.

*On screen:* Two proof panels with dates.

**[0:45]**

**Say:** Because "done" means five different things. Drafted. Saved. Sent. Live. And actually checked. When someone says done, they usually mean the first one.

*On screen:* A five-step ladder: Drafted, Saved, Sent, Live, Checked. A marker sits on the bottom rung.

**[0:55]**

**Say:** So add one line to your instructions: "When you say done, say which: drafted, saved, sent, live or checked. And tell me what you checked."

*On screen:* The line on screen, big enough to screenshot.

**[1:05]**

**Say:** Then open the real thing. Skip the report about the email and open the sent folder. When it says "the spreadsheet's updated", open it and look for today's rows.

*On screen:* Screen recording: a sent folder, then a spreadsheet scrolled to today's date.

**[1:16]**

**Say:** And look at it where people will actually see it. When we launched, the phone version of the video cut off my eyes. The captions spelled my name C, H, R, I, S. And a subscriber's Outlook hid every picture in the email, because Outlook hides pictures from senders it doesn't know yet. None of that showed up on my laptop.

*On screen:* Three quick frames: the crop with no eyes, the caption "Chris", the email with empty grey boxes. Let them land.

**[1:37]**

**Say:** So for anything that goes out: send it to yourself, open it on your phone, and look at it with pictures turned off. Thirty seconds. Saves you an awkward reply-all.

*On screen:* Card: "Phone. Pictures off. Thirty seconds."

**[1:47]**

**Say:** And pick one automation you rely on. A scheduled report, a Zap, an assistant that "handles" your inbox. Open the last thing it actually made. Is it real? Is it from today?

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:58]**

**Say:** If yes, lovely. If no, you just found out before your client did.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Open the last thing one of your automations or AI assistants actually produced. Check it's real and from today. Then add the "which done?" line to your instructions.
