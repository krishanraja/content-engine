# 15 The name-swap test

- **Theme:** Ways of thinking about AI
- **Length:** about 2:10 at an unhurried pace (about 330 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** competent AI writing now costs almost nothing, so what's worth anything is the sentence only you could have written, and the name-swap test finds out whether it's there.

**Why this one:** a quick, funny quality check with a big idea under it: judgement is the scarce thing now. The two deletions (first line, last paragraph) give a visible before-and-after on screen. The Preacher-Catcher line is already public on makeyourmindup.ai.

**News nod:** at OpenAI's DevDay on 29 September 2026, GPT-6.1 Sol was priced at a fifth of its top model, Astra (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/09/29/openai-devday-2026-live-updates.html).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Here's the quickest quality check for anything AI writes for you. Swap the name. If the email still works for a different client, a different company, a different person, it's a template with your name on it.

*On screen:* Face. An email with the client's name crossed out and three other names swapped in. It still reads fine every time.

**[0:14]**

**Say:** And templates just got even cheaper. At OpenAI's developer day on the twenty-ninth of September, its new mid-range model was priced at a fifth of its top one. A competent first draft now costs almost nothing. Which means a competent first draft is worth almost nothing.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the pricing line, source and date.

**[0:31]**

**Say:** So what's left that's worth something? Judgement. Knowing which of ten decent drafts is right for this client, this week. AI's read everything about your industry. It's never sat in your meetings.

*On screen:* Ten drafts fanned out. One gets picked.

**[0:44]**

**Say:** So before you send anything AI helped with, find the one sentence only you could have written. The thing you know about this customer. The detail from last week's call. The number from your own business. If there isn't one, add it.

*On screen:* The email again. One sentence highlighted in mint: the specific one.

**[1:00]**

**Say:** Then make two deletions. Delete the first sentence. AI loves a warm-up: "I hope this finds you well", "In today's fast-moving world". If nothing's lost, leave it deleted.

*On screen:* The first line struck through.

**[1:12]**

**Say:** Then delete the last paragraph. AI loves a little sermon at the end. "Ultimately, it's all about trust." Read it again without. It's almost always better. My own system has a judge whose only job is catching those. Its catchphrase is: "That last line wants to be a poster."

*On screen:* The last paragraph struck through. A little card: "The Preacher-Catcher".

**[1:30]**

**Say:** And learn the tells. Every sentence the same length. Lists of three just for the rhythm. And that "this isn't about X, it's about Y" line. Once you spot it, you'll see it in every LinkedIn post you scroll past.

*On screen:* Three tells listed. The last one gets a stamp.

**[1:43]**

**Say:** Readers are spotting machine-made writing faster too. The moment it sounds generic, people start skimming, and your best idea goes unread.

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:51]**

**Say:** So, next email that matters: swap the name, find your sentence, cut the first line and the last paragraph. Two minutes. It goes from something anyone could have sent to something only you could have sent.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Before your next important email, swap the name. If it still works, add one sentence only you could have written. Then delete the first sentence and the last paragraph, and read it again.
