# 15 The name-swap test

- **Theme:** Ways of thinking about AI
- **Length:** about 3:09 at an unhurried pace (about 472 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** competent AI writing now costs almost nothing, so what's worth anything is the sentence only you could have written, and the name-swap test finds out whether it's there.

**The question it answers:** how can you tell if something AI wrote for you is worth sending?

**Why this one:** a quick, funny quality check with a big idea under it: judgement is the scarce thing now. The two deletions (first line, last paragraph) give a visible before-and-after on screen. The Preacher-Catcher line is already public on makeyourmindup.ai.

**News nod:** at OpenAI's DevDay on 29 September 2026, GPT-6.1 Sol was priced at a fifth of its top model, Astra (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/09/29/openai-devday-2026-live-updates.html).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** How can you tell if something AI wrote for you is worth sending? Here's the quickest check I know. Swap the name.

*On screen:* Face. Card: "Is it worth sending?"

**[0:09]**

**Say:** Take the email. Cross out the client's name and put in a different one. Then another. If it still works for a different client, a different company, a different person, it's a template: a fill-in-the-blanks letter with your name on top.

*On screen:* An email with the client's name crossed out and three other names swapped in. It still reads fine every time.

**[0:25]**

**Say:** And templates just got even cheaper. At OpenAI's big developer event on the twenty-ninth of September, its new mid-range model was priced at a fifth of its top one. A decent first draft now costs almost nothing. Which means a decent first draft is worth almost nothing.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the pricing line, source and date.

**[0:44]**

**Say:** So what's still worth something? Judgement. That means knowing which of ten decent drafts is right for this client, this week. AI has read everything about your industry. It's never sat in your meetings.

*On screen:* Ten drafts fanned out. One gets picked.

**[0:58]**

**Say:** So before you send anything AI helped with, find the one sentence only you could have written. The thing you know about this customer. The detail from last week's call. The number from your own business. If there isn't one, add it. That's the sentence that breaks when you swap the name.

*On screen:* The email again. One sentence highlighted in mint: the specific one.

**[1:18]**

**Say:** Then make two cuts, to clear away the bits that make it sound like a machine. First, delete the opening sentence. AI loves a warm-up: "I hope this finds you well", "In today's fast-moving world". If nothing's lost, leave it deleted.

*On screen:* The first line struck through.

**[1:35]**

**Say:** Second, delete the last paragraph. AI loves a little sermon at the end. "Ultimately, it's all about trust." Read it again without. It's almost always better.

*On screen:* The last paragraph struck through.

**[1:45]**

**Say:** I check my own writing with a panel of AI judges, and one of them has a single job: catching those sermons. I call it the Preacher-Catcher. Its favourite line is: "That last line wants to be a poster."

*On screen:* A little card: "The Preacher-Catcher".

**[2:01]**

**Say:** And learn the other tells. Every sentence the same length. Lists of three, just for the rhythm. And the line that tells you what something isn't, only so it can tell you what it is. Once you spot them, you'll see them in every LinkedIn post you scroll past.

*On screen:* Three tells listed. The last one gets a stamp.

**[2:20]**

**Say:** Why bother? Because readers are spotting machine-made writing faster too. The moment it sounds generic, people start skimming, and your best idea goes unread.

*On screen:* Face.

**[2:30]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. How can you tell if something AI wrote is worth sending? Swap the name. If it breaks, because there's a sentence in there only you could have written, send it. If it doesn't, it's a template, so add that sentence first.

*On screen:* Face. The email with the mint sentence, the name swapped, the sentence now wrong.

**[2:48]**

**Say:** So, next email that matters: swap the name, find your sentence, cut the first line and the last paragraph. Two minutes. It goes from something anyone could have sent to something only you could have sent.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

**[3:03]**

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. There's more like this, free, at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.

*On screen:* Full face, then the end card.

## Do this today

Before your next important email, swap the name. If it still works, add one sentence only you could have written. Then delete the first sentence and the last paragraph, and read it again.
