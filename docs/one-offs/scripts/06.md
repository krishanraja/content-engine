# 06 Make your call before you ask the AI

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 2:20 at an unhurried pace (about 350 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** write your own answer, how sure you are and what would change your mind before you ask AI, then use the gap to learn.

**Why this one:** this is the Make Your Mind Up philosophy as a habit anyone can do. It should feel like a quiet superpower: you stay sharper than your tools. Keep it optional and practical. Krish ranked "commit first" as a forced step last in his own product, so here it's a habit people choose.

**News nod:** Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on 22 September 2026 and says it puts the important part first (TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/anthropic-releases-opus-5-5-with-lower-prices-and-fable-level-performance/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Here's a habit that keeps you sharper than your AI. Before you ask it anything that matters, write down your own answer first.

*On screen:* Face. A notepad and pen beside the laptop.

**[0:08]**

**Say:** Because the answers are getting very convincing. Anthropic's new model, out on the twenty-second of September, is built to put the important part first. Clear, confident, nicely laid out. Lovely. And the moment you read a confident answer, your own opinion quietly packs its bags.

*On screen:* Proof panel with the date. Then a little suitcase icon labelled "your opinion" walking off.

**[0:25]**

**Say:** It's called anchoring. Whatever you see first pulls you towards it. Read the AI first and you'll mostly end up agreeing with it, and then tell yourself you'd have thought of it anyway.

*On screen:* Face. Caption punch word: ANCHORING.

**[0:37]**

**Say:** So: three lines, before you ask. My call. How sure I am, as a percentage. And what would change my mind.

*On screen:* Card: My call / How sure (%) / What would change my mind.

**[0:46]**

**Say:** Say you're deciding whether to hire another salesperson. My call: yes. How sure: sixty percent. What would change my mind: if the last two hires took more than six months to pay for themselves.

*On screen:* The card filled in, handwritten.

**[1:00]**

**Say:** Now ask the AI. Then compare. The gap between you and the machine is the most useful thing on the screen. Where you agree, crack on. Where you disagree, that's the bit worth ten minutes.

*On screen:* The handwritten card next to the AI answer. Mint highlight where they disagree.

**[1:14]**

**Say:** Sometimes it's spotted something you missed, and you change your mind for a real reason. Sometimes it's got a fact wrong, and you catch it because you'd already thought it through. And sometimes it's just given you the textbook answer, because it's never sat in your meetings.

*On screen:* Three quick cards: "It saw something." / "It got a fact wrong." / "Textbook answer."

**[1:31]**

**Say:** Bonus test. Want to know if an AI tool is any good for your business? Give it a decision you made a year ago, with only what you knew then, and hide how it turned out. If you get the textbook answer, it doesn't know you yet.

*On screen:* Card: "The replay test: an old decision, outcome hidden."

**[1:48]**

**Say:** I build AI systems for a living and I still do this. The day I stop writing my call first is the day my AI makes the decisions and I just sign them.

*On screen:* Face, straight.

**[1:59]**

**Say:** So, next decision that matters: three lines on paper first. Your call, how sure you are, and what would change your mind. Then let the AI argue with you.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Before your next important AI question, write three lines: my call, how sure I am, what would change my mind. Then ask, and compare.
