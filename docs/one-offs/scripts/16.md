# 16 Who can actually answer this?

- **Theme:** Ways of thinking about AI
- **Length:** about 3:14 at an unhurried pace (about 485 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** every decision has questions the web can answer, questions only you can answer, and questions nobody can answer yet, and AI should only answer the first kind.

**The question it answers:** when you're stuck on a decision, who can actually answer each question: AI, you, or nobody yet?

**Why this one:** it's the deepest thinking tool in the set, made simple: three columns. It stops people taking confident internet answers about their own business, and it turns "nobody knows" into a cheap test instead of a guess.

**News nod:** Google released Gemini 4 Argon on 30 September 2026, calling it its most powerful model yet (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/google-gemini-4-argon-ai.html).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Ask an AI "will my customers pay more for this?" and you'll get a lovely, confident answer. It's guessing. It can't know. And it won't tell you that unless you ask. So before you ask AI anything about a decision, ask this: who can actually answer this question? AI, you, or nobody yet?

*On screen:* Face. A chat answer full of confident bullet points. A small stamp: GUESS. Card: "Who can actually answer this?"

**[0:21]**

**Say:** And the models keep getting bigger. Google released Gemini 4 Argon on the thirtieth of September, and called it its most powerful model yet. It still doesn't know your customers. No model does, however big it gets.

*On screen:* Proof panel: Google's own line, dated, "most powerful" highlighted.

**[0:36]**

**Say:** Here's why that matters. Every decision is made of three kinds of question. Sort them before you ask AI anything.

*On screen:* Three empty columns appear.

**[0:44]**

**Say:** First: things the web can answer. What do competitors charge? What does the law say? What did that company announce? AI's good at these, as long as it shows you the source and you click through.

*On screen:* Column 1 fills: "The web can answer".

**[0:58]**

**Say:** Second: things only you can answer. Is our data any good? Can my team actually take this on? How much risk can we stomach? The internet has no idea. If AI answers these, it's answering about an average company that isn't yours.

*On screen:* Column 2 fills: "Only I can answer".

**[1:15]**

**Say:** Third: things nobody can answer yet. Will customers pay more? Will this market be here in two years? These are guesses about the future. If a model says it "checked" one, it's faking it.

*On screen:* Column 3 fills: "Nobody knows yet".

**[1:29]**

**Say:** So here's the move. Take a decision you're stuck on. Draw the three columns. Put every question in one.

*On screen:* A real-looking decision broken into questions, dropped into the columns.

**[1:36]**

**Say:** The first column, give to AI, and check its sources. The second is your homework: one conversation with your team, or one look at your own numbers. The third, you test, or you make the decision easy to undo.

*On screen:* Each column gets a label: "AI, with sources" / "My homework" / "Test it".

**[1:52]**

**Say:** And testing is cheaper than you think. "Will customers pay more?" Ring three customers. Put up a page with the price on it. Run a one-month trial. A week of real answers beats a month of confident guesses.

*On screen:* Three small icons: phone, web page, calendar.

**[2:07]**

**Say:** Stuck on which column a question belongs in? AI can help you sort them, as long as it doesn't answer them. Try this prompt: "Split my decision into separate claims. Label each one fact, assumption or forecast. Then tell me the one assumption that would sink the whole thing if it's wrong." A fact goes in the first column. An assumption is something you're taking as true, so check it yourself. A forecast is a guess about the future, so test it.

*On screen:* The prompt on screen, big enough to screenshot.

**[2:40]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. Who can actually answer this? The web can answer some of it, so let AI do that and check its sources. Only you can answer some of it, so do that homework yourself. And nobody can answer the rest yet, so test it, cheaply.

*On screen:* Face. The three columns, labelled.

**[3:00]**

**Say:** So, this week: take one decision you're stuck on, draw the three columns, and pick one cheap test for the third.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. There's more like this, free, at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.
