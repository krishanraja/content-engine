# 05 Write down why you said no

- **Theme:** Build your AI brain
- **Length:** about 3:15 at an unhurried pace (about 480 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** your reasons are the most valuable thing you know, so keep a four-line note for each decision that your AI reads before it suggests anything.

**The question it answers:** why does your AI keep suggesting things you've already said no to?

**Why this one:** it lifts the AI brain from "my writing style" up to "how I decide", which is where the business value is. It should feel like a sharp management insight first and an AI tip second. The four lines are the takeaway.

**News nod:** OpenAI launched always-on agents called Dots at DevDay on 29 September 2026 (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/09/29/openai-devday-2026-live-updates.html).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Why does your AI keep suggesting things you've already said no to? To answer that, start with people, because they do it too. When a senior person leaves, the most expensive thing they take with them is the reason they said no. Handover notes have the passwords and the client list. Hardly any of them say "we never give that customer a discount, and here's why".

*On screen:* Face. Caption: "Why does your AI keep suggesting what you already ruled out?" Then a handover document scrolling past: passwords, contacts, and no reasons anywhere.

**[0:26]**

**Say:** So six months later, someone gives that customer a discount. And you find out the hard way why the old boss didn't.

*On screen:* Face. Beat.

**[0:35]**

**Say:** Your AI has the same problem, every single day. You make a call, you explain it once in a chat, the chat closes, and the reason's gone. So next week the AI cheerfully suggests the thing you already ruled out. It never saw your reasons, so it can't use them.

*On screen:* Chat bubble: "Have you considered offering them a discount?"

**[0:55]**

**Say:** And this is about to matter a lot more. An AI agent is an AI that goes off and does jobs for you on its own. On the twenty-ninth of September, OpenAI launched always-on agents called Dots. Each one gets its own computer and keeps working when you don't. An agent that doesn't know your reasons can reopen every decision you thought was closed, while you're asleep.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the DevDay report, dated, "always-on" highlighted.

**[1:22]**

**Say:** So the fix is to write your reasons down, somewhere your AI can read them. I call it a decision note. Four lines. The situation. What you decided. Why. And what would change your answer.

*On screen:* Card building: Situation / Decision / Why / What would change it.

**[1:36]**

**Say:** For example. Situation: a big client asks for ten per cent off to renew. Decision: no. Why: they renewed last year without a discount, and giving one now resets the price for good. What would change it: if they doubled the contract.

*On screen:* The four lines filled in.

**[1:53]**

**Say:** That fourth line is the clever bit. It stops the decision going stale, and it tells the next person, or the next AI, exactly when it's fair to reopen it.

*On screen:* Mint highlight on the fourth line.

**[2:05]**

**Say:** Then make sure your AI actually reads it. Most AI tools have a box for instructions it reads before every chat. Put one line in there: "Check my decision notes before you suggest anything or ask me anything." I added that after I had to tell an AI agent, "I already answered your questions." I had. It just hadn't read them.

*On screen:* The line on screen, big enough to screenshot. Then Krish, deadpan.

**[2:29]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. Why does your AI keep suggesting things you've already said no to? Because your reasons only ever lived in a chat that closed. Write them down where it can read them, and it stops.

*On screen:* Face. Caption: "Your reasons lived in a chat that closed."

**[2:45]**

**Say:** Here's your job this week. Pick one decision you make again and again: pricing, hiring, which clients you take on. Write the four lines for the last time it came up, and save them where every AI you use can read them. Your reasons stay in the building, whoever, or whatever, is doing the work.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card: "One recurring decision. Four lines."

**[3:07]**

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. There's more like this, free, at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.

*On screen:* Full face, then the end card.
