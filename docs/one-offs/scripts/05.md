# 05 Write down why you said no

- **Theme:** Build your AI brain
- **Length:** about 2:20 at an unhurried pace (about 350 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** your reasons are the most valuable thing you know, so keep a four-line note for each decision that your AI reads before it suggests anything.

**Why this one:** it lifts the AI brain from "my writing style" up to "how I decide", which is where the business value is. It should feel like a sharp management insight first and an AI tip second. The four lines are the takeaway.

**News nod:** OpenAI launched always-on agents called Dots at DevDay on 29 September 2026 (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/09/29/openai-devday-2026-live-updates.html).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** The most expensive thing a senior person takes with them when they leave is the reason they said no. Handover notes have the passwords and the client list. Hardly any of them say "we never discount for that customer, and here's why".

*On screen:* Face. A handover document scrolling past: passwords, contacts, and no reasons anywhere.

**[0:15]**

**Say:** Six months later, someone gives that customer a discount. And you find out why the old boss didn't.

*On screen:* Face. Beat.

**[0:22]**

**Say:** The same thing happens with your AI every day. You make a call, explain it once in a chat, the chat closes, and the reason's gone. Next week the AI cheerfully suggests the thing you already ruled out.

*On screen:* Chat bubble: "Have you considered offering them a discount?"

**[0:35]**

**Say:** And AI agents are about to make more of these calls for you. OpenAI launched always-on agents called Dots on the twenty-ninth of September, each with its own computer that keeps working when you don't. An agent that doesn't know your reasons can reopen every decision you thought was closed.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the DevDay report, dated, "always-on" highlighted.

**[0:53]**

**Say:** The fix is a decision note. Four lines. The situation. What you decided. Why. And what would change your answer.

*On screen:* Card building: Situation / Decision / Why / What would change it.

**[1:03]**

**Say:** For example. Situation: a big client asks for ten percent off to renew. Decision: no. Why: they renewed last year without a discount, and giving one now resets the price for good. What would change it: if they doubled the contract.

*On screen:* The four lines filled in.

**[1:20]**

**Say:** That fourth line is the clever bit. It stops the decision going stale, and it tells the next person, or the next AI, exactly when to reopen it.

*On screen:* Mint highlight on the fourth line.

**[1:30]**

**Say:** Then put one line in your AI instructions: "Check my decisions note before you suggest anything or ask me anything." I added that after I had to tell an AI agent, "I already answered your questions." I had. It just hadn't read them.

*On screen:* The line on screen. Then Krish, deadpan.

**[1:47]**

**Say:** Start small. One decision you make again and again: pricing, hiring, which clients you take on. Write the four lines for the last time it came up. That's the page of your AI brain that's worth the most.

*On screen:* Card: "One recurring decision. Four lines."

**[2:02]**

**Say:** So, this week: one decision, four lines, saved where every AI you use can read it. Your reasons stay in the building, whoever, or whatever, is doing the work.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Pick one decision you make again and again. Write the four lines (situation, decision, why, what would change it) for the last time it came up.
