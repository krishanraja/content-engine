# 03 When one comment becomes a rule

- **Theme:** Build your AI brain
- **Length:** about 2:15 at an unhurried pace (about 340 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** AI memory takes things you said once, about one situation, and turns them into rules about you forever, so check it and give every rule its situation back.

**Why this one:** the most common way an AI profile goes wrong, told through a story that makes people wince and laugh. The viewer should go straight to their AI's memory settings afterwards. The client stays completely anonymous.

**News nod:** Meta launched its Muse agent on 8 September 2026 (PBS NewsHour, https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/meta-launches-personal-ai-agent-muse-to-help-with-everyday-tasks). OpenAI launched Dots on 29 September 2026 (SiliconANGLE, https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/29/openai-launches-dots-always-on-ai-agents-in-chatgpt-with-their-own-cloud-computers/). Both keep working after you close the app.

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** A CEO I worked with said this about one project: "We're very uncorporate, we don't want a deck." Fair enough. Later, an AI profile built from his notes said, in capital letters: never make decks for him. He wants decks all the time. His review of that file was one word, and I can't say it here.

*On screen:* Face. Card with "FORBIDDEN: decks" in big type, then a bleep.

**[0:19]**

**Say:** Nothing was misquoted. Every word was his. The AI was still wrong, because it took something he said about one situation and turned it into a rule about everything. That's the most common way AI memory goes wrong, and the person it's about finds out at the worst moment.

*On screen:* The quote on screen with "about one project" highlighted in mint.

**[0:37]**

**Say:** Your AI might be doing this to you right now. Most of the big tools remember things about you. Say you're knackered and it can file you as a tired person. Ask for bullet points once and you can be a bullet-point person for life.

*On screen:* A mock memory list: "Is tired." "Loves bullet points." "Hates decks."

**[0:52]**

**Say:** And it matters more now. The agents launched in September, like Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots, keep working after you close the app. The more an AI does for you, the more a wrong idea about you costs.

*On screen:* Two proof panels with launch dates, "keeps working" highlighted.

**[1:06]**

**Say:** Here's the fix. Open your AI's saved memory or custom instructions. Search for four words: always, never, must, forbidden. For each one, ask where it came from, and add the situation back. "Never make decks" becomes "for progress updates on one project, skip the deck".

*On screen:* Screen recording: a search for "never", then the rule rewritten with its situation.

**[1:27]**

**Say:** Then delete anything that isn't a fact about you. Moods. Wishes. One-off formatting requests. And anything about another named person. "My finance director is slow to reply" is gossip with a very long memory.

*On screen:* Lines being deleted one by one.

**[1:42]**

**Say:** Last thing. When it doesn't know something about you, it should say so. Add this line: "If you don't know something about me, ask me or say you don't know. Never guess." A gap gets filled next week. A wrong guess spreads into everything.

*On screen:* The line on screen, big enough to screenshot.

**[1:58]**

**Say:** So: ten minutes, four search words, and one line about guessing. Your AI keeps what's true about you, and forgets what you said once on a bad day.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Search your AI's memory for "always", "never", "must" and "forbidden". Give each rule back its situation, or delete it. Add the "never guess" line.
