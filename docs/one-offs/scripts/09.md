# 09 Reading is free. Sending needs a yes.

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 3:00 at an unhurried pace (about 450 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** let AI do anything that stays inside the building, and make anything that leaves it (send, post, pay, sign, delete) wait for your yes, one action at a time.

**The question it answers:** if an AI can now spend your money, what should it be allowed to do without asking you first?

**Why this one:** it's the most timely script in the set. Agents that pay at checkout arrived in September and a US regulator is now asking questions. It gives people one rule they can apply to every tool, and it's the rule Krish runs his own system on. It should calm people down rather than scare them.

**News nod:** Meta's Muse launched on 8 September 2026 and can check out for you (PBS NewsHour, https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/meta-launches-personal-ai-agent-muse-to-help-with-everyday-tasks). Amazon began blocking it on about 20 September (Bloomberg, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/amazon-blocks-meta-s-muse-ai-agent-from-its-retail-site). The US Federal Trade Commission opened an inquiry into AI companies over risks to consumers on 30 September 2026 (Semafor, https://www.semafor.com/article/09/30/2026/ftc-to-investigate-openai-anthropic-for-potential-consumer-harms).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Meta's new AI helper, Muse, can fill in forms and pay at the checkout for you. It launched on the eighth of September. Within two weeks, Amazon was blocking it. And on the thirtieth, the US consumer watchdog, the FTC, started asking the big AI companies what their agents, the AIs that act for you, might do to consumers.

*On screen:* Three proof panels in a row, each with its date in mint.

**[0:24]**

**Say:** So here's the question. If an AI can now spend your money, what should it be allowed to do without asking you first? My answer is one rule, and I run my whole business on it. Reading is free. Sending needs a yes.

*On screen:* Face. Card: "What can it do without asking?" Then: "Reading is free. Sending needs a yes."

**[0:41]**

**Say:** Picture your work as a building. Inside the building is everything only you see. Let AI do all of that. Read the documents. Sort the inbox. Draft the reply. Crunch the numbers. Make the slides. If it gets something wrong, you bin it and nobody ever knows.

*On screen:* A building outline. Inside it: read, sort, draft, analyse.

**[1:00]**

**Say:** Anything that leaves the building waits for you. Sending an email. Posting. Paying. Signing. Deleting. Booking. That's where a mistake costs you money or a client, and you can't take it back.

*On screen:* The same building. At the door: send, post, pay, sign, delete, each with a padlock.

**[1:12]**

**Say:** Unsure which side a task sits on? Ask three questions. Does it reach someone outside? Can it be undone? And does it set the standard, like a price list or a template the whole team copies? Any yes, and a person decides. All no, and the AI can crack on. Just check a sample of its work each week.

*On screen:* Card: Reaches outside? / Can't undo? / Sets the standard?

**[1:36]**

**Say:** And keep the yes pile small. If you approve everything, you stop reading what you approve. A hundred approvals a day turns into a rubber stamp. Fewer approvals, each one read properly, is safer.

*On screen:* A rubber stamp thudding down, faster and faster.

**[1:50]**

**Say:** One more thing about the yes. It covers one action. "Yes, send that email" covers that email, and nothing after it. My own AI once refused to mark an article as approved, even after I'd said I was happy with it. Why? The fact check hadn't been run on the final words. Annoying at the time. It was right.

*On screen:* Face, grudging nod.

**[2:13]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. What should an AI be allowed to do without asking? Anything that stays inside the building. Anything that leaves it waits for your yes: sending, posting, paying. And each yes covers one action.

*On screen:* Card: Inside: free. Leaving: needs a yes. One yes, one action.

**[2:28]**

**Say:** Your job this week: open the settings on any AI tool or automation you use. Find anything that can send, spend, post or delete without asking you. Switch it to "ask me first".

*On screen:* Screen recording: a toggle switched to "Ask before acting".

**[2:42]**

**Say:** Then write two lists in your AI's instructions. Things it can do without asking. Things it must always ask you about first. Your AI gets faster, and you sleep better.

*On screen:* Face. The do-this card.

**[2:54]**

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. There's more like this, free, at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.

*On screen:* Full face, then the end card.

## Do this today
