# 09 Reading is free. Sending needs a yes.

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 2:15 at an unhurried pace (about 340 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** let AI do anything that stays inside the building, and make anything that leaves it (send, post, pay, sign, delete) wait for your yes, one action at a time.

**Why this one:** it's the most timely script in the set. Agents that pay at checkout arrived in September and a US regulator is now asking questions. It gives people one rule they can apply to every tool, and it's the rule Krish runs his own system on. It should calm people down rather than scare them.

**News nod:** Meta's Muse launched on 8 September 2026 and can check out for you (PBS NewsHour, https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/meta-launches-personal-ai-agent-muse-to-help-with-everyday-tasks). Amazon began blocking it on about 20 September (Bloomberg, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/amazon-blocks-meta-s-muse-ai-agent-from-its-retail-site). The US Federal Trade Commission opened an inquiry into AI companies over risks to consumers on 30 September 2026 (Semafor, https://www.semafor.com/article/09/30/2026/ftc-to-investigate-openai-anthropic-for-potential-consumer-harms).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Meta's new AI agent, Muse, can fill in forms and pay at the checkout for you. It launched on the eighth of September. Within two weeks Amazon was blocking it. And on the thirtieth, the US consumer watchdog, the FTC, started asking the big AI companies what their agents might do to consumers.

*On screen:* Three proof panels in a row, each with its date in mint.

**[0:18]**

**Say:** So before you hand an AI agent your card, here's the one rule I run my whole AI system on. Reading is free. Sending needs a yes.

*On screen:* Face. Card: "Reading is free. Sending needs a yes."

**[0:27]**

**Say:** Let AI do anything that stays inside the building. Read the documents. Sort the inbox. Draft the reply. Crunch the numbers. Make the slides. If it gets it wrong, you bin it and nobody ever knows.

*On screen:* A building outline. Inside it: read, sort, draft, analyse.

**[0:40]**

**Say:** Anything that leaves the building waits for you. Sending an email. Posting. Paying. Signing. Deleting. Booking. That's where a mistake costs money or a client, and you can't take it back.

*On screen:* The same building. At the door: send, post, pay, sign, delete, each with a padlock.

**[0:52]**

**Say:** Three questions sort any task. Does it reach the outside world? Can it be undone? Does it set the standard everyone else follows? Any yes, and a person decides. All no, and let the AI crack on, and check a sample each week.

*On screen:* Card: Reaches outside? / Can't undo? / Sets the standard?

**[1:09]**

**Say:** And the line matters both ways. If you approve everything, you stop reading what you approve. A hundred approvals a day turns into a rubber stamp. Fewer approvals, each one read properly, is safer.

*On screen:* A rubber stamp thudding down, faster and faster.

**[1:22]**

**Say:** Second rule: a yes covers one action. "Yes, send that email" covers that email and nothing after it. My system once refused to mark an article approved after I'd said I was happy with it, because the fact check hadn't been run on the final words. Annoying at the time. It was right.

*On screen:* Face, grudging nod.

**[1:42]**

**Say:** So, this week: open the settings on any AI tool or automation you use. Find anything that can send, spend, post or delete without asking you. Switch it to "ask me first".

*On screen:* Screen recording: a toggle switched to "Ask before acting".

**[1:53]**

**Say:** Then write two lists in your AI instructions. Things you can do without asking. Things you must always ask me about first. Your AI gets faster, and you sleep better.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Find any AI tool or automation that can send, spend, post or delete without asking. Switch it to ask first. Write your two lists: "do without asking" and "always ask first".
