# 11 New job, new chat

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 2:15 at an unhurried pace (about 340 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** long chats drift and forget your early instructions, so start a fresh chat for each job and carry the decisions over in a five-line summary.

**Why this one:** everyone has typed "I already told you that" at a chatbot. This names the feeling, explains it in one breath, and gives the cheapest fix there is. It's also where the "where we are" note comes in, which is how Krish runs his own business across tools.

**News nod:** this autumn's agents (Meta's Muse, 8 September; OpenAI's Dots, 29 September; Microsoft's Autopilot, 25 September) are built to keep working for hours. Sources as in scripts 03, 08 and 10.

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** There's a moment in every long AI chat when you type, "No. I already told you that." That's the moment to stop. Close it.

*On screen:* Face. The typed message, with a sigh.

**[0:08]**

**Say:** Long chats forget. The instructions you gave at the start slowly fade as the conversation grows, so by message forty it's mostly working from whatever you said last. You're not imagining it. It's a well-known weakness of long conversations.

*On screen:* A long chat scrolling. The first message fades to grey as it scrolls away.

**[0:22]**

**Say:** This autumn's big launches, Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, Microsoft's Autopilot, are agents built to keep working for hours. Maybe one day they'll remember everything. The everyday chat window you use still drifts in a long thread.

*On screen:* Three launch headlines with dates, small.

**[0:35]**

**Say:** So: one job, one chat. New task, new conversation. And watch for the words that mean you've drifted: "also", "while you're at it", "one more thing". That's a new job. Give it a new chat.

*On screen:* The three phrases appear one by one, each with a little warning light.

**[0:49]**

**Say:** When a chat goes wrong, start again with a summary. Ask it: "Summarise what we've decided so far in five lines." Copy that. Open a fresh chat. Paste it in. Carry on. It's quicker than three rounds of "no, what I said was..."

*On screen:* Screen recording: the summary request, copy, new chat, paste.

**[1:05]**

**Say:** For bigger projects, keep a "where we are" note. Half a page: what this is, what's done, what's waiting on you, what's next. Paste it at the start of each new chat, and ask the AI to update it at the end. I run my whole business this way. Any tool can pick up the work in a minute, because nothing important lives only in a chat.

*On screen:* Card: What this is / Done / Waiting on me / Next.

**[1:27]**

**Say:** One more. Keep exploring and doing apart. Brainstorm in one chat. When you've decided, open a new one to do the work, with just the decisions. Mix the two and you get an AI that builds the idea you rejected twenty messages ago.

*On screen:* Two chat windows labelled "Thinking" and "Doing".

**[1:43]**

**Say:** The good news is this costs nothing. No new tool, no subscription. Just the habit of closing a chat before it goes stale.

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:51]**

**Say:** So, next time you type "I already told you", ask for the five-line summary, open a new chat and paste it in. Notice how much sharper the answers get.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today
