# 11 New job, new chat

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 2:55 at an unhurried pace (about 440 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** long chats drift and forget your early instructions, so start a fresh chat for each job and carry the decisions over in a five-line summary.

**The question it answers:** what do you do when your AI forgets what you told it?

**Why this one:** everyone has typed "I already told you that" at a chatbot. This names the feeling, explains it in one breath, and gives the cheapest fix there is. It's also where the "where we are" note comes in, which is how Krish runs his own business across tools.

**News nod:** this autumn's agents (Meta's Muse, 8 September; OpenAI's Dots, 29 September; Microsoft's Autopilot, 25 September) are built to keep working for hours. Sources as in scripts 03, 08 and 10.

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** There's a moment in every long AI chat when you type, "No. I already told you that." So what do you do when your AI forgets what you told it? Short answer: close the chat. Here's why, and here's how to do it without losing anything.

*On screen:* Face. The typed message, with a sigh.

**[0:18]**

**Say:** Long chats forget. The instructions you gave at the start slowly fade as the conversation grows, so by message forty it's mostly working from whatever you said last. This happens to everyone. It's a well-known weakness of long AI conversations.

*On screen:* A long chat scrolling. The first message fades to grey as it scrolls away.

**[0:34]**

**Say:** Won't the new tools fix it? This autumn's big launches, Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, Microsoft's Autopilot, are agents: AIs built to keep working on a task for hours. Maybe one day they'll remember everything. The everyday chat window you use today still drifts in a long thread.

*On screen:* Three launch headlines with dates, small.

**[0:53]**

**Say:** So the fix is simple: one job, one chat. New task, new conversation. And listen for the words that mean you've drifted: "also", "while you're at it", "one more thing". That's a new job. Give it a new chat.

*On screen:* The three phrases appear one by one, each with a little warning light.

**[1:09]**

**Say:** But what about everything you've already agreed? You don't want to lose that. So before you close a chat, ask it: "Summarise what we've decided so far in five lines." Copy that. Open a fresh chat. Paste it in. Carry on. It's quicker than three rounds of "no, what I said was..."

*On screen:* Screen recording: the summary request, copy, new chat, paste.

**[1:30]**

**Say:** For bigger projects, five lines isn't enough. So keep a "where we are" note. Half a page: what this is, what's done, what's waiting on you, what's next. Paste it at the start of each new chat, and ask the AI to update it at the end. I run my whole business this way. Any tool can pick up the work in a minute, because nothing important lives only in a chat.

*On screen:* Card: What this is / Done / Waiting on me / Next.

**[1:58]**

**Say:** One more. Keep thinking and doing apart. Brainstorm in one chat. When you've decided, open a new one to do the work, with just the decisions. Mix the two and the AI forgets which ideas you threw out. You end up with it building the idea you rejected twenty messages ago.

*On screen:* Two chat windows labelled "Thinking" and "Doing".

**[2:19]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. What do you do when your AI forgets what you told it? Close the chat. Ask for a five-line summary first, open a new one, and paste it in. One job, one chat. And it costs nothing: no new tool, no subscription.

*On screen:* Face. Card: One job, one chat.

**[2:38]**

**Say:** Your job this week: next time you type "I already told you", do exactly that. Summary, new chat, paste. Notice how much sharper the answers get.

*On screen:* Face. The do-this card.

**[2:48]**

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. There's more like this, free, at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.

*On screen:* Full face, then the end card.
