# 02 Correct it once, with the reason

- **Theme:** Build your AI brain
- **Length:** about 3:10 at an unhurried pace (about 470 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record (version 2: reworked to the five-point story check)

**The point in one line:** a correction left in a chat dies with the chat, so write it down once with its reason, for the whole family of mistakes.

**The question it answers:** why does your AI keep making the same mistake, even after you've corrected it?

**Why this one:** this is Krish's best true story about his own system, and he's the butt of the joke: he taught his AI the habit he'd banned. Self-mocking and specific. The viewer should leave with a corrections note and a three-line footer they can paste in tonight.

**News nod:** Microsoft relaunched Copilot with an always-on agent called Autopilot on 25 September 2026 (Microsoft blog, https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Why does your AI keep making the same mistake, even after you've corrected it? I found out the embarrassing way. Every week I told my AI to stop writing one kind of line. You know the one: it knocks down an idea nobody had, just to sound clever. Half the LinkedIn posts you've scrolled past this year. I banned it. It kept doing it. In one rewrite it did it about seven times.

*On screen:* Face. Then the banned shape typed on screen and crossed out.

**[0:29]**

**Say:** So I went looking for where it learned it. Before it writes anything, my AI reads a set of saved notes about how I write. Those notes held that exact line up as my signature move, with examples. I'd been teaching it the habit and banning it at the same time. I fixed the notes, and the next rewrite went from seven of them to one.

*On screen:* Face, slightly embarrassed. Card: "Seven hits. Fix the notes. One hit."

**[0:56]**

**Say:** So that's the first answer. Sometimes it keeps doing it because you taught it. Check what you've fed it: the instructions you've saved, the examples you've given it, and what it remembers about you. One bad example beats ten "please don't"s.

*On screen:* A settings screen with "Custom instructions" and "Memory" highlighted.

**[1:12]**

**Say:** The second answer is simpler. A correction you type into a chat dies with the chat. Close it, and next Tuesday you're making the same correction again. So keep a corrections note. Three lines each time: what it did, what you wanted, and why.

*On screen:* Card: What it did / What I wanted / Why.

**[1:30]**

**Say:** The why is the bit everyone skips, and it's the bit that matters. A correction without a reason teaches it what to say once. A correction with a reason teaches it how to decide.

*On screen:* Face. Mint highlight on "how to decide".

**[1:43]**

**Say:** And write each one for every mistake of that kind. Say it misspells a client's name. The small fix is "spell Lauren right". The big fix is "check every name against what I gave you before you use it". One stops one mistake. The other stops a hundred.

*On screen:* Two cards side by side: "Spell Lauren right" against "Check every name against what I gave you".

**[2:02]**

**Say:** You can even get the AI to help. Paste three lines at the bottom of those saved instructions. When you get something wrong, tell me what broke and why. Suggest one rule that stops that kind of mistake. Add it to my notes when I say yes.

*On screen:* The three lines on screen, big enough to screenshot.

**[2:21]**

**Say:** This matters more this month. Agents are AI that do jobs for you without you watching, and they're arriving fast. Microsoft's new Copilot has an always-on agent called Autopilot. An agent that repeats your least favourite mistake can repeat it all night.

*On screen:* Proof panel: Microsoft's announcement, dated 25 September, "Autopilot" highlighted.

**[2:38]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. Why does your AI keep making the same mistake? Either you taught it, or your correction died with the chat. So check what you've fed it, and write each correction down once, with the reason. This week: open a note called "My AI rules". Add the last three things you corrected, each with one line of why.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

**[3:03]**

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. More like this, free, at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.

*On screen:* Full face, then the end card.
