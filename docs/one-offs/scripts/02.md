# 02 Correct it once, with the reason

- **Theme:** Build your AI brain
- **Length:** about 2:30 at an unhurried pace (about 370 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** a correction left in a chat dies with the chat, so write it down once with its reason, for the whole family of mistakes.

**Why this one:** this is Krish's best true story about his own system, and he's the butt of the joke: he taught his AI the habit he'd banned. Self-mocking and specific. The viewer should leave with a corrections note and a three-line footer they can paste in tonight.

**News nod:** Microsoft relaunched Copilot with an always-on agent called Autopilot on 25 September 2026 (Microsoft blog, https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Every week I was telling my AI the same thing. Stop writing that "it's not this, it's that" line. You know the one. Half the LinkedIn posts you've scrolled past this year. I banned it. It kept doing it. I showed it the exact sentences. In one rewrite it did it about seven times.

*On screen:* Face. Then the banned shape typed on screen and crossed out.

**[0:17]**

**Say:** So I went looking for where it learned it. It learned it from me. My own saved notes about how I write held that line up as my signature move, with examples. I'd been teaching it the habit and banning it at the same time. I fixed the notes, and the next rewrite went from seven of them to one.

*On screen:* Face, slightly embarrassed. Card: "Seven hits. Fix the notes. One hit."

**[0:37]**

**Say:** Lesson one: when AI keeps doing something you hate, check what you've fed it. Your saved instructions, your style examples, your memory settings. One bad example beats ten "please don't"s.

*On screen:* A settings screen with "Custom instructions" and "Memory" highlighted.

**[0:50]**

**Say:** Lesson two: a correction left in a chat dies with the chat. You'll be making it again next Tuesday. So keep a corrections note. Three lines each time: what it did, what you wanted, and why.

*On screen:* Card: What it did / What I wanted / Why.

**[1:04]**

**Say:** The why is the bit everyone skips, and it's the bit that matters. A correction without a reason teaches it what to say once. A correction with a reason teaches it how to decide.

*On screen:* Face. Mint highlight on "how to decide".

**[1:16]**

**Say:** And write it for the whole family of mistakes. Say it misspells a client's name. The small fix is "spell Lauren right". The big fix is "check every name against what I gave you before you use it". One stops one mistake. The other stops a hundred.

*On screen:* Two cards side by side: "Spell Lauren right" against "Check every name against what I gave you".

**[1:35]**

**Say:** Then paste three lines at the bottom of your AI instructions. When you get something wrong, tell me what broke and why. Suggest one rule that stops that kind of mistake. Add it to my notes when I say yes.

*On screen:* The three lines on screen, big enough to screenshot.

**[1:51]**

**Say:** This matters more this month, because the new AI agents don't wait for you. Microsoft's new Copilot has an always-on agent called Autopilot. An agent that repeats your least favourite mistake can repeat it all night.

*On screen:* Proof panel: Microsoft's announcement, dated 25 September, "Autopilot" highlighted.

**[2:06]**

**Say:** So: open a note called "My AI rules". Paste in the last three things you corrected, each with one line of why. Now your AI learns you, and keeps what it learned.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.
