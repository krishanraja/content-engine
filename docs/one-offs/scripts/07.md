# 07 Never let AI mark its own homework

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 2:15 at an unhurried pace (about 340 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record (version 2: two lines rewritten to make sense out loud)

**The point in one line:** the chat that wrote something can't check it, so split finding from fixing, give the checker the evidence, and trace every number to a sentence.

**Why this one:** two true stories from Krish's own system carry it: a $900 million mistake that another AI scored 9 out of 10, and the same draft scoring 3 and then 9 depending on whether the checker could see the sources. Both are funny and both are proof. The three habits are the takeaway.

**News nod:** OpenAI delayed GPT-6.1 Astra on 29 September 2026. Its head of safety systems said it "didn't quite meet the bar" (Euronews, https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/29/openai-postpones-release-of-latest-ai-model-over-security-concerns-as-the-industry-faces-n; OPB, https://www.opb.org/article/2026/09/29/openai-delays-latest-model-over-security-concerns/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** My AI system once turned nine hundred million dollars into "close to a million dollars". The right source was sitting right there. Then I had another AI check the facts, and it gave the piece nine out of ten.

*On screen:* Face. Proof panel: "$900 million annually" in the source, then the AI's line "close to a million dollars a year", mint on both numbers.

**[0:14]**

**Say:** That's AI marking AI's homework, and it gets worse when it's the same chat. Ask the chat that wrote something whether it's good, and it mostly agrees with itself. It's like asking a student whether their essay is any good. They wrote it. Of course it's good.

*On screen:* Face. Caption punch word: HOMEWORK.

**[0:29]**

**Say:** Even the people building AI get someone else to check. On the twenty-ninth of September OpenAI held back its next top model because, in its safety lead's words, it "didn't quite meet the bar". The makers don't let the model sign itself off. You shouldn't either.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the quote, source and date, "didn't quite meet the bar" highlighted.

**[0:45]**

**Say:** Three habits fix most of it. One: split the jobs. Open two chats. In the first: "List the problems only, and say where they are. Don't rewrite anything." Paste that list into a second chat: "Fix these. Change nothing else."

*On screen:* Two chat windows labelled "Finder" and "Fixer".

**[1:03]**

**Say:** A reviewer that writes the fix and then marks it is marking itself.

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:08]**

**Say:** Two: give the reviewer the evidence. My system had an AI checking the facts, and nobody had given it the sources. So it called a properly sourced draft "invented" and gave it three out of ten. Then we pasted the sources in. Same draft. Nine out of ten. The only thing that changed was what it could see.

*On screen:* Two score cards: 3 out of 10, then 9 out of 10. Caption: "Same text."

**[1:27]**

**Say:** So when you ask "is this any good?", paste the brief and the sources first, then the draft.

*On screen:* Card: Brief, then sources, then draft.

**[1:34]**

**Say:** Three: for anything with numbers in it, make it show its working. "List every number, date and name in here, with the exact sentence from the source behind it. Say NOT FOUND where there isn't one." Then check the NOT FOUNDs yourself.

*On screen:* The prompt on screen, big enough to screenshot. A list with two "NOT FOUND" lines highlighted.

**[1:52]**

**Say:** And after any rewrite, check the whole thing again. AI fixing AI often swaps one mistake for a brand new one.

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:59]**

**Say:** So: two chats, the sources pasted in, and every number traced to a sentence. Ten minutes. A lot cheaper than explaining a wrong number to a client.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Take your last important AI draft. In a fresh chat, ask for every number, date and name with the exact source sentence, and "NOT FOUND" where there isn't one.
