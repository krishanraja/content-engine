# 04 Show your AI a yes and a no

- **Theme:** Build your AI brain
- **Length:** about 3:00 at an unhurried pace (about 450 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record (version 2: reworked to the five-point story check)

**The point in one line:** AI can't learn your taste from adjectives, but it can from one thing you loved, one thing you binned, and a sentence on the difference.

**The question it answers:** how do you teach an AI your taste, when words like "punchy" don't work?

**Why this one:** taste is the thing people think they can't hand to AI. The relief here is that they don't need to describe it, only show it. The hotel-website gag is the hook, and the binned-drafts idea is the one people will remember.

**News nod:** Anthropic says Claude Opus 5.5 (22 September 2026) uses less jargon and puts the important part first (9to5Mac, https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/22/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-opus-5-5-model-details-here/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Tell an AI to make your writing "punchy, premium and on brand" and you'll get something that sounds like a hotel website. Those words mean something to you. To the AI they mean the average of everyone who's ever typed them. So how do you teach an AI your taste, when words like "punchy" don't work?

*On screen:* Face. Then a cheesy hotel-website paragraph appears, with "luxurious" and "elevate" highlighted.

**[0:22]**

**Say:** And don't wait for the AI companies to sort it. They're working on taste too. Anthropic says its new model, out on the twenty-second of September, uses less jargon and puts the important part first. That's their taste. Yours is still your job.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the announcement, "less jargon" highlighted in mint.

**[0:40]**

**Say:** Here's the good news. You're much better at spotting good work than describing it. Ask someone to describe their style and you get "clear and professional", which helps nobody. Show them two of their own emails and they know in a second which one they'd send.

*On screen:* Two emails side by side. A finger points at one.

**[0:58]**

**Say:** So let the AI spot it too. Give it a yes and a no. Find one thing you wrote that you're proud of. Find one you'd never send, or an AI draft you binned. Then write one sentence on the difference. "The good one opens with the decision. The bad one spends the first line saying hello."

*On screen:* Card: Yes / No / One sentence on the difference.

**[1:21]**

**Say:** That sentence is gold. A yes on its own only tells it what you liked. The no shows it where the line is. Which means your binned drafts are some of the best teaching material you've got, and most of us empty the bin.

*On screen:* A recycle bin icon. Then a hand pulling a draft back out.

**[1:38]**

**Say:** Do it three times. Save the good ones, the bad ones and the one-line reasons in one place, and attach them whenever you ask for writing.

*On screen:* A folder called "My taste" with six files and a notes file.

**[1:49]**

**Say:** One warning, which I learned the hard way. Only put in work you wrote or properly edited yourself. I use AI tools that write drafts for me, and they nearly saved their own drafts as examples of my taste. Leave that running and your AI slowly learns to copy itself. Everything drifts to the same bland middle. Beige.

*On screen:* Face. A colour swatch fading from bright to beige.

**[2:12]**

**Say:** And a one-minute habit to keep it growing. Next time AI gives you something, ask yourself: would I send this today? Yes or no, plus one line why. Do that for a week and you've written down your taste without ever having to describe it.

*On screen:* Card: "Would I send this today? Yes or no. One line why."

**[2:30]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. How do you teach an AI your taste when you can't put it into words? You show it. One yes, one no, and one sentence on the difference. This week: find your yes and your no, write the sentence, and save all three. Your AI stops guessing what "punchy" means and starts writing like you.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

**[2:54]**

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. There's more like this free at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.

*On screen:* Full face, then the end card.
