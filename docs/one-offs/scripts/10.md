# 10 Every blank in your brief becomes a bug

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 2:25 at an unhurried pace (about 360 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record (version 2: the 78% story rewritten to make sense out loud)

**The point in one line:** give AI a five-part brief, make it list what's missing before it starts, and leave a placeholder wherever your own judgement goes.

**Why this one:** this is the most "steal this template" video in the set. It should feel like a pro's checklist handed over for free. The 78% story is the moment: the AI quietly made a call that was Krish's to make.

**News nod:** Microsoft relaunched Copilot with Autopilot, an agent that runs in the background, on 25 September 2026 (Microsoft blog, https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** Every blank in your brief becomes a bug in the answer. Don't say who it's for, and the AI guesses. Don't say how long, and it guesses. And AI guesses with total confidence.

*On screen:* Face. A brief with blank boxes. Each blank turns into a little bug.

**[0:12]**

**Say:** Microsoft relaunched Copilot on the twenty-fifth of September with an agent that works in the background inside Office. Brilliant, if you tell it what you want. Vague in, vague out, and now it runs all day.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the announcement, dated.

**[0:24]**

**Say:** So before anything that matters, give it five things at once. The goal: what's this for? The context: who's it for, and what do they already know? The format: how long, what shape. The limits: what it mustn't do. And the finish line: "You've done this right when..."

*On screen:* Card building: Goal / Context / Format / Limits / Finish line.

**[0:44]**

**Say:** That last one is the one nobody writes, and it's the most useful. "You've done this right when my finance director can read it in two minutes and sign off the budget." Now the AI knows what good looks like.

*On screen:* Mint highlight on the finish line.

**[0:58]**

**Say:** Second trick. Make it tell you what's missing before it starts. Add: "Before you begin, list anything missing from this brief that you'd otherwise guess. Don't start until I answer." The gaps are exactly where your judgement was needed. An AI that quietly fills them is making your decisions for you.

*On screen:* The line on screen. Then the AI's reply: three questions.

**[1:17]**

**Say:** Third trick, for anything with your opinion in it. Leave a placeholder. Write "me" in square brackets wherever your number goes: the price, the recommendation, how confident you are. My system once wrote an article where I'd told it to leave one number for me: how sure I was about our prediction. It filled it in anyway. Seventy-eight per cent. Nobody asked it.

*On screen:* "[ME]" in a sentence. Then a cut to "How sure we are: 78%", crossed out.

**[1:39]**

**Say:** Tell the AI to leave every "me" exactly as written, then fill them in yourself.

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:44]**

**Say:** Here's the difference. Lazy brief: "Write a proposal for Acme." Proper brief: goal, win a three-month trial. Context, they've been burned by a big consultancy before. Format, one page. Limits, no jargon, no prices. Finish line, their operations director can forward it to the boss without changing a word.

*On screen:* Two briefs side by side, the lazy one tiny, the proper one filled in.

**[2:05]**

**Say:** So, next prompt that matters: five headings, the "what's missing" line, and a "me" wherever your judgement goes. One minute of thinking saves an hour of rewriting.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.
