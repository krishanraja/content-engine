# 10 Every blank in your brief becomes a bug

- **Theme:** Level yourself up
- **Length:** about 3:35 at an unhurried pace (about 540 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record (version 2: the 78% story rewritten to make sense out loud)

**The point in one line:** give AI a five-part brief, make it list what's missing before it starts, and leave a placeholder wherever your own judgement goes.

**The question it answers:** why does AI give you a confident answer that misses the point?

**Why this one:** this is the most "steal this template" video in the set. It should feel like a pro's checklist handed over for free. The 78% story is the moment: the AI quietly made a call that was Krish's to make.

**News nod:** Microsoft relaunched Copilot with Autopilot, an agent that runs in the background, on 25 September 2026 (Microsoft blog, https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** You ask AI for something. It comes back sounding totally sure of itself, and it's still wrong for you. So why does AI give you a confident answer that misses the point? Usually, it's the gaps in your brief, the instructions you give it. Every blank in your brief becomes a bug in the answer. Leave out who it's for, and the AI guesses. Leave out how long, and it guesses. And it guesses with total confidence.

*On screen:* Face. A brief with blank boxes. Each blank turns into a little bug.

**[0:31]**

**Say:** This matters more now. Microsoft relaunched its AI helper, Copilot, on the twenty-fifth of September, with an agent that keeps working in the background inside Office. Brilliant, if you tell it what you want. Vague in, vague out, and now it runs all day.

*On screen:* Proof panel: the announcement, dated.

**[0:48]**

**Say:** So before anything that matters, fill the blanks. Give it five things at once. The goal: what's this for? The context: who's it for, and what do they already know? The format: how long, what shape. The limits: what it mustn't do. And the finish line: "You've done this right when..."

*On screen:* Card building: Goal / Context / Format / Limits / Finish line.

**[1:09]**

**Say:** That last one is the one nobody writes, and it's the most useful. "You've done this right when my finance director can read it in two minutes and sign off the budget." Now the AI knows what good looks like.

*On screen:* Mint highlight on the finish line.

**[1:25]**

**Say:** Second trick. Even a good brief has gaps you can't see. So make the AI find them before it starts. Add this line: "Before you begin, list anything missing from this brief that you'd otherwise guess. Don't start until I answer." Those gaps are exactly where your judgement was needed. An AI that quietly fills them is making your decisions for you.

*On screen:* The line on screen. Then the AI's reply: three questions.

**[1:50]**

**Say:** Third trick, for anything with your opinion in it. Some blanks should stay blank until you fill them. So leave a placeholder. Write "me" in square brackets wherever your call goes: the price, the recommendation, how confident you are.

*On screen:* "[ME]" in a sentence.

**[2:05]**

**Say:** I learned this one the hard way. My AI once wrote an article for me, and I'd told it to leave one number blank: how sure I was about our prediction. It filled it in anyway. Seventy-eight per cent. Nobody asked it. That was my call, and it made it for me, with total confidence.

*On screen:* A cut to "How sure we are: 78%", crossed out.

**[2:27]**

**Say:** So tell the AI to leave every "me" exactly as written, then fill them in yourself.

*On screen:* Face.

**[2:34]**

**Say:** Here's all of it on one job, for a made-up client called Acme. Lazy brief: "Write a proposal for Acme." Proper brief: goal, win a three-month trial. Context, they've been burned by a big consultancy before. Format, one page. Limits, no jargon, no prices. Finish line, their operations director can forward it to the boss without changing a word.

*On screen:* Two briefs side by side, the lazy one tiny, the proper one filled in.

**[2:57]**

**Say:** So, back to the question. Why does AI sound so sure and still miss the point? Because it fills every blank you leave with a guess. Fill the five blanks yourself, make it list the ones you missed, and keep the ones that are yours for you.

*On screen:* Card: Fill the blanks. Ask what's missing. Keep the [ME].

**[3:16]**

**Say:** Your job this week: take your next prompt that matters. Write it under the five headings, add the "what's missing" line, and put a "me" wherever your judgement goes. One minute of thinking saves an hour of rewriting.

*On screen:* Face. The do-this card.

**[3:31]**

**Say:** That's makeyourmindup. There's more like this, free, at makeyourmindup.ai. I'm Krish. Make your mind up.

*On screen:* Full face, then the end card.
