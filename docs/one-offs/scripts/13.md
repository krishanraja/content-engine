# 13 Hand over the work. Keep the understanding.

- **Theme:** Ways of thinking about AI
- **Length:** about 2:25 at an unhurried pace (about 360 spoken words), plus the 4-second end card
- **Format:** to camera, tall and wide
- **Status:** draft for Krish to read, change and record

**The point in one line:** let AI do the middle of the job, keep the framing at the start and the judging at the end, and check you can still explain the work.

**Why this one:** this is the strongest line in the Mindmake canon, turned into a one-minute test anyone can run. It's about agency, so it must never tip into "AI will make you obsolete" fear. The feeling is "I'm still the one who knows", with an honest note that some skills fade if you never use them.

**News nod:** Apple shipped iOS 27 with the rebuilt Siri on 14 September 2026 (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/apple-releases-ios-27-redesigned-siri-ai.html). Microsoft's Copilot Autopilot launched on 25 September 2026 (link in script 10).

## Script

**[0:00]**

**Say:** You can hand AI the work. The understanding stays with you, or you're in trouble.

*On screen:* Face. Card: "Hand over the work. Keep the understanding."

**[0:06]**

**Say:** Here's the test. Pick something AI did for you this week. A report, an analysis, an email. Now explain out loud, in one minute, why it's right. If you can't, your judgement went out the door with the work.

*On screen:* A one-minute timer starts.

**[0:20]**

**Say:** And that matters. If you can't explain the report, you can't spot when it's wrong, you can't ask the next question, and you can't defend it when the boss pushes back. That's the point where AI stops making you faster and starts making you a passenger.

*On screen:* Face. Caption punch word: PASSENGER.

**[0:35]**

**Say:** The tools are getting better at doing everything for you. Apple's rebuilt Siri arrived on the fourteenth of September. Microsoft's Copilot now has an agent working in the background. The more they do, the easier it is to stop understanding what they did.

*On screen:* Two proof panels with dates.

**[0:50]**

**Say:** So here's how I split it. AI does the middle. I keep the start and the end.

*On screen:* A bar in three parts: a small slice, a big middle, a small slice.

**[0:56]**

**Say:** The start is framing. What are we trying to do, and what does good look like? Five minutes, in my own words, before any prompt.

*On screen:* The first slice lights up: "Frame it".

**[1:04]**

**Say:** The middle is the grind. Research, drafts, number crunching. AI's brilliant at it. Let it go.

*On screen:* The middle lights up: "AI".

**[1:10]**

**Say:** The end is judging. Is it right? Is it true? Would I put my name on it? That's where the quality lives, and it's where your skill stays sharp.

*On screen:* The last slice lights up: "Judge it".

**[1:20]**

**Say:** Roughly the first fifth and the last fifth are yours. Skip them and you become a very fast rubber stamp.

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:27]**

**Say:** There's an honest catch. Skills fade if you never use them. Most of us got worse at finding our way once satnav did it for us. So pick the skills you want to keep, and use them by hand now and then. If you manage someone junior, have them do one task this week without AI, and explain their thinking to you.

*On screen:* A satnav screen. Then a person with a paper map, looking smug.

**[1:47]**

**Say:** That's how people get good enough to judge AI in the first place.

*On screen:* Face.

**[1:52]**

**Say:** So, this week: one thing AI made for you, one minute out loud on why it's right. If you can explain it, it's yours. If you can't, you've got some reading to do.

*On screen:* Full face. The do-this card.

## Do this today

Pick one thing AI made for you this week. Explain out loud, in one minute, why it's right. If you can't, read it properly before you use it.
