What changed, and what didn't

This is the same brand, grown up. Ink, cream and mint stay. So do Anton, the Fraunces human line, the grain and the operating table where we take AI claims apart. What goes is everything that read as toy: offset shadows, stickers, the highlighter swipe, halftone, tilt, the felt robot, day colours and series badges. Premium comes from restraint, real evidence and better photographs. Joy comes from colour, wit and motion, never from decoration.

Decisions, in Krish's words:

"I want an evolution and coming of age of my existing branding, not a complete overhaul."

"just because we are upscaling in premium feel does not mean out motion animations or design or copy become boring"

"we've lost some element of joy which i suspect went away with all the colour. I didn't want to make this completely homogenuos"

"lets remove all badges like mind.the.gap from socials, and just have the wordmark left aligned with the wording at the bottom"

"it all needs to work in tandem, having just one small decision at a time is not helpful to me"

"s3h is great, lock v5"

The full look

One system on every surface. The board below is the locked reference: if a surface does not match it, the surface is wrong.

The icon

The icon is two half-discs, cream and mint, one beat out of register: two minds, one decision. It is the avatar on every platform, the favicon and the app icon. On the podcast cover it is the O in YOUR. It never sits beside a headline and it is never a watermark.

The wordmark

The wordmark lives on ink only, cream with a mint UP. It has four forms. V4, one line, sits under every headline and shares its left edge. V6, two lines, fills square spaces. V9 puts the icon beside V4 for wide headers. V10 puts the icon above V6 for the closing card. It is always built from the master files, never typed or redrawn.

Colour codes the climb

Ink is the page. Cream is the reading colour and the light page. Mint is the answer. Coral, butter and lilac belong to the climb, not the weekday: the first why is coral, the second butter, the third lilac, then round again. Each frame uses one colour family, used big, as a flat block with the same fine grain as the ink. Type on a colour block is always ink.

Swatches (name, hex, job):

Ink, #0C1512, the page, always with 7% grain

Cream, #F4EFE4, reading colour and light sections

Mint, #7EF0C0, the answer; the one primary button on web

Coral, #FF6A4D, why 1

Butter, #FFD84D, why 2

Lilac, #B7A6FF, why 3

Never a colour gradient, never gradient text. The only gradient anywhere is the burn: a photograph fading into ink.

Three typefaces, three jobs

Anton. The one loud line. Uppercase. One per frame.WHY DID POP SONGS GET SHORTER?

Fraunces. The human line: insight, questions, recaps, captions. Italic marks what is contested or handed back to you.Spotify counts a stream at 30 seconds, so the hook moved to the front.

DM Mono. Labels, tags, sources.DOCUMENTED · Spotify for Artists, How your streams are counted

Never IBM Plex Mono, Newsreader or Instrument Serif. Those belong to other brands or to every AI startup.

The operating table

Our photographs are one world: a theatre lamp, teal surgical cloth, real objects from the creative economy laid out like specimens, coral thread, a mint seam ripper. Deadpan, true colour. Evidence is always real: documents, screenshots and creative work shown as crisp cards. Every card carries real evidence or a photograph, and a company we name appears with its real logo, unaltered. No stock, no cartoon faces, no generated people.

Full-bleed photographs burn into ink over their lower half so type sits on the image with no box.

Words on screen

Copy says the fact plainly. A reader should never have to decode a line. In Krish's words: "Copy like that which is vague and smart arse needs to be banned." On-screen words add a source, number, name or date the narration does not say. No em dashes, no exclamation marks, British spelling.

Examples:

"Spotify pays after 30 seconds"

"Why songs start with the chorus now"

"Who gets paid when AI writes a song?"

"Spotify counts a stream at 30 seconds, so the hook moved to the front."

"Who gets paid. What's real. Where the pitch and the product part ways."

The why-ladder: the signature format

Every piece climbs three to seven whys to one insight. Each why is load-tested on screen: the evidence lands, its strength shows, and a real rival claim gets a fair hearing before it is parked. The rail shows where you are. Strength is the line itself: solid for documented, thinner for inferred, dashed for open.

The floor: every component

The floor is the minimum every generated component must meet. Visual style combos on the board sit on top of it, and anything retired cannot be picked.

Table (component, the floor):

Title frame: animates as the title is said inside a natural sentence near the start; Anton lines land with the words; the one-line wordmark aligned under the title.

Ladder rail: thin seam; current node in the why's colour; finished nodes cream with a 2 to 4 word label, never rotated; future nodes hollow.

Step card: photo or evidence above; the why's colour block below, at least 40% of the frame; WHY n in DM Mono; the question in Anton, ink, three lines at most.

Evidence plate: real, crisp, full brightness, legible on a phone; a margin bar in the why's colour, never a highlighter; one Anton note; one source line.

Callout: a 2 px leader to a boxed DM Mono tag; never crosses text; one per frame at most.

Strength tag: DOCUMENTED solid, INFERRED solid at 70%, OPEN dashed.

Challenger: only a real rival claim with its own source; at most two per piece; parked or forked, struck only on documented evidence.

Recap: cream, ruled; numbers in the why's colour; strength tags; a grid of the real evidence above.

Landing: a flat mint block; the insight in Fraunces; the open question in italic; no verdict, forecast, odds or lesson for leaders; no wordmark.

Captions: Fraunces in sentence case, cream, 2 to 5 words a page, the spoken word in mint; no box, no swipe, no shadow.

Closing card: the only end screen; the takeaway on mint, then an ink band with "Follow for more" and the stacked wordmark with the icon.

The floor: motion

Every move decelerates: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1). Never bounce, overshoot, slap, tilt, typing or a slow drift.

Brisk and full-frame: 6 to 12 frames at 25 fps per entry.

Card to card is seamless: the next block or photograph wipes or pushes in. Never a flash, a snap or a cut through black.

Anton lines rise 60 px over 8 frames, 4 frames apart. Leaders draw in 6 frames, then the tag steps in. Numbers count as their line draws. Colour blocks wipe up in 8 frames. The landing holds a beat before the question.

Socials: one template, every post

The grid should read like a sophisticated media business without losing a laugh. Every per-piece post uses one template: the photograph burns into ink, the Anton headline sits bottom-left inside the crop, the one-line wordmark sits under it on the same left edge. No series badges. Nothing reaches the grid off-template, memes included. Profiles carry the colour: banners climb coral, butter and lilac to mint.

The podcast

The show art is the name in three huge lines with the icon as the O in YOUR, cream on ink with a mint UP. It is the one surface that carries no photograph, because it has to read at 56 pixels on a shelf of other shows.

The newsletter and the site

One weekly edition carries the week's three main pieces and what else happened, each item plain and sourced. The site is one screen: the name, the line, this week's three pieces and the subscribe field.

The Library

Every template we use, and the ones we will need next, built once in this look: video, carousels, social, profiles, podcast, newsletter, web, email and the next formats. Anything not in the Library is not on brand.

Retired

Retired for good, and blocked in the engine so they cannot be picked: brutal offset shadows; stickers and badges; the highlighter swipe; halftone; tilt; translucent panels over photographs; colour gradients and gradient text; day colours; series labels on social images; the felt robot; drawn faces; the decorative cover skins; the old caption themes; toy, paper-cutout, pixel and riso motion; IBM Plex Mono, Newsreader and Instrument Serif; Fraunces carousel headlines; "AI, unpicked."; pop and hustle captions; slap and bounce motion.
