# Art direction: variety by rule, so nothing ever looks like a stream of shapes (26 Nov 2026)

Krish, 26 Nov 2026: "bake enough variety and thoughtfulness into the use of the asset library via the instruction manual (with rotations, or ... other clever ideas), so that my page can never look like just a stream of AI-generated shapes, blocks, and Anton text."

He rejected the first v5 site build as "the most AI slop of AI slop". These rules are why it can't happen again. They apply to every surface the Library feeds: the makeyourmindup.ai Edition, covers and thumbnails, carousels and social. A production session follows them as rules. Picking a different look per piece is not allowed.

## 1. Real-world imagery first, chosen in this order
1. **A real person named in the story:** their licensed Wikimedia Commons photo.
   - Licences allowed: Public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA. NC and ND are never used.
   - Always show the credit "Photo: <artist>, <licence>, via Wikimedia Commons".
   - **Pair it only with that person's own story.** Never put it beside an accusation against their company: a CEO smiling next to a claim about their firm reads as editorialising.
2. **A real company, product, venue or object named in the story:** its Commons photo, or a credited press-kit image the company publishes for media use.
3. **Otherwise:** a photoreal still of the specific thing in the story, made by Codex (Krish, 26 Nov: "Use Codex for the daily image ... 100% photoreal").
   - Editorial photojournalism, natural light, no generated people, no readable logos or text.
   - Reject any still that reads as illustration or CGI.
4. **Never republish a publisher's own article photo.** It is their copyright.
5. **Never use** old music artefacts, dark or "haunted" moods, mascots, generic stock people, or a felt robot.

## 2. Story treatments rotate by rule
- **Treatments:** portrait (person), photo (object or scene), number (set as type in the story, never a big block), quote (the source's own words), evidence (the source sentence typeset with its key clause marked), chart (a sparkline of a named company), type only.
- **Order of preference** per slot: portrait, photo, number, quote, evidence, chart, type only. Take the first one the story's data supports.
- **Cadence rules:**
  - Two neighbours never share a treatment.
  - Photos sit at least three slots apart.
  - No image appears twice on a screen.
  - A chart never repeats a ticker already shown in the same list.
- **Every edition or screen** pairs at least one real person with one object photo when the day's stories allow.
- **The lead** is the newest story with both a photo and a "why it matters" line. Its image is not reused anywhere else on the page.

## 3. Type carries the voice, and Anton stays in its lane
- **Headlines:** Fraunces, in a newspaper register. Asides and "why it matters" lines: Fraunces italic.
- **Interface:** Schibsted Grotesk. **Numbers and labels:** DM Mono.
- **The wordmark is condensed caps, so headlines never use its face.**
  - Anton appears only in ticker numbers, or as a single kicker on a cover, never on titles in a list.
  - Never Archivo, IBM Plex Mono or Source Serif (retired).

## 4. Colour is a signal, never wallpaper
- **Series:** butter for follow.the.money, lilac for under.the.hood, coral for mind.the.gap.
- **Mint** means up, or the answer.
- **Colour appears as a fine rule, text colour or a single field**, never as a row of identical coloured cards. No gradients except the house burn.

## 5. Labels: none
- **"Remove all badges."** (Krish, 26 Nov.) No "machine-written", "panel-checked", "illustrative", glyphs, pills or toasts on items.
- **Each item carries its source as a plain link.** Real photo credits stay as quiet captions, because the licences require them.
- **One plain footer sentence** says how the page is made: "Daily stories are written by machine from the linked sources and checked by a panel; some images are illustrative."

## 6. Mood and cadence
- **Time of day:** morning edition on cream, evening wrap on ink.
- **Each subsector has its own photographic grade:**
  - Music: warm tungsten;
  - Film & TV: cool cinematic;
  - Gaming: saturated stage light;
  - Sport: floodlit dusk;
  - Publishing & Media: daylight paper;
  - Art & Design: gallery neutral.
- **Day of week:** the series of the day leads its own pieces (Mon follow.the.money, Wed under.the.hood, Fri mind.the.gap).

## 7. Covers, thumbnails and carousels follow the same rules
A cover is image-led: one real person or one real thing, large, with the headline in Fraunces or a single Anton kicker. Logos and the colour bars are supporting evidence, never the whole composition. Two consecutive pieces never use the same cover composition. Rotate across:
- portrait-led;
- object-led;
- evidence-led (the real source page);
- number-led.
