# Covers and thumbnails playbook (makeyourmindup, v5)

Written 26 Nov 2026 for the v5 Library. Scope: every cover and thumbnail for makeyourmindup ("Creativity, AI and money, simplified."): YouTube long-form and Shorts, Instagram Reels and carousels, TikTok, Facebook, Substack, LinkedIn and X.

**Precedence.** Krish writes his own cover titles, so his title shapes (`TITLES.md`) and the approved cover standard (content-engine `docs/production/COVER-STANDARD.md`, approved 9 Nov 2026, rulings of 15 and 17 Nov) win wherever outside research disagrees. Section 3 lists every place they conflict. The v5 lock (`BRIEF.md`) and the floor (`FLOOR.md`) bind the look. This playbook adds evidence and geometry; it never overrides them.

**Evidence labels.**

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| [OWNER] | Krish's own approved rule, title verdict or floor standard |
| [OFFICIAL] | The platform's own documentation or its head's own words |
| [RESEARCH] | Peer-reviewed study |

## 1. The principles that move click-through most

Ranked by the weight of evidence, strongest first.

### 1.1 A recognisable thing with stakes beats design polish

- [REVEALED] In heavily templated channels the top outliers and the bottom videos use the same template. What changes is a named, famous entity plus a stake (scandal, decline, cheating, money). Half as Interesting: "Why Almost Every Airline Has Sued This One Website" 6.48x its median, "Why Rolex Won't Sell You Their Most Expensive Watches" 4.59x, against "Why This NYC Skyscraper Has Been Half-Finished Since 2018" at 0.45x, all with identical yellow-on-black type and the H mark [S1, S2]. Athletic Interest: "Why EA Killed FIFA" 7.68x and "Why The Olympics Banned Nike" 3.17x, against "How Sports Betting Took Over Football" at 0.17x, same orange template [S3]. Modern MBA: "Why Crumbl Cookies Can't Survive" 5.32x against an Insta360 shelf at 0.28x [S4].
- [REVEALED] On Instagram the same: within each account the first-slide template barely changes between top and median posts; Jensen Huang alone fronted three top posts across The Rundown and Morning Brew in one week.
- [SECONDARY] 1of10's 300,000-video outlier study (Dec 2025) concluded faces and no faces "perform similarly", which points the same way: no single design device carries the lift on its own, so the subject must [S20].
- [OWNER] This is Krish's rule 1 already: "Name the real thing exactly as people search it" (`TITLES.md`), and floor 108/235: show the real logo of the company named.
- **Rule:** every cover shows the named thing itself: its real logo, its real document or its real product. A generic "AI" image never does this job (IMAGERY rule 1).

### 1.2 One dominant subject, large enough to read at 130 px

- [REVEALED] The weakest thumbnails in the set are wide shots of several small people: Bloomberg's "The Deal" panel thumbnail sat at 0.04x of the channel median [S5]. Marques Brownlee's top five each show one phone filling the frame [S6].
- [OWNER] Cover standard point 1: the headline is the largest thing and readable "in under a second at grid size (about 130 px wide on a phone)".
- [OFFICIAL, corpus] YouTube long-form thumbnail: "one unmistakable subject, high contrast, minimal text".
- **Rule:** one subject (a face, a logo pair, a document or one scene object) plus the headline. Test every cover at 130 px and 168 px wide before Krish sees it.

### 1.3 Few words, and they add something the title does not say

- [REVEALED] Top outliers carry 0 to 4 words that give a verdict or ask a question: "NOT BURNING" (Cleo Abram, 3.73x), "FRAUD?" (Half as Interesting, 6.48x), "HOW?" (Rick Beato, 3.54x), "It's getting worse." (ColdFusion, 3.04x), "THIS GUY?!" (Athletic Interest, 6.28x). Median and bottom thumbnails more often carry a label or a name ("HOW D.C. WORKS", 0.40x) [S1, S3, S7, S8, S9, S10].
- [SECONDARY] vidIQ's July 2026 study of 500 breakout thumbnails: 28% had no text; among those with text the median was 5 words; text appeared on 72% [S19].
- [OWNER] `TITLES.md` rule 7: "Thumbnail words are not the title"; often under 4 words ("Another Claude?", "AI now acts first").
- **Rule:** beside a visible title (YouTube), at most 4 words. Where no title shows (Reel grid, TikTok grid, Substack cover, share card), at most 7 words (cover standard point 1).

### 1.4 Open a question the piece truly answers

- [RESEARCH] Loewenstein's information-gap theory: curiosity rises when people notice a specific gap in what they know [S22]. A cover that names the gap ("Who gets paid?") and shows the evidence pulls harder than one that states the answer.
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's December 2024 "egregious clickbait" enforcement removes videos whose title or thumbnail promises something the video does not deliver, starting in India [S16]. And YouTube's own title and thumbnail test picks the winner on watch time, so a cover that over-promises loses its own test [S14].
- [OWNER] Floors 19, 38 and 78 and `TITLES.md` rule 2: the most dramatic **true** claim; if it is not literally true as written, it does not run; an unverified superlative becomes a question.
- **Rule:** the cover opens the gap; the first 10 seconds of the video (or the first slide after the cover) start closing it.

### 1.5 Faces help when large, natural and on the right account

- [SECONDARY] vidIQ: a human face on 69% of all 500 breakouts, 75% of the top 100 and 80% of the top 50, and the top 50 often showed a face filling "roughly a third of the frame"; exaggerated expressions on only 5 to 6% [S19].
- [SECONDARY] 1of10: faces gave a "modest" lift only above a channel-size threshold, multiple faces did best, and business was a niche where faces did worse [S20].
- [REVEALED] The host's own face is constant within a channel, so it never explains lift. Host-only selfies sit at the bottom (Rick Beato livestream 0.47x; Adam Neely vlog 0.33x). Famous third-party faces under stress lead several tops (ColdFusion, Athletic Interest, Bloomberg) [S7, S3, S5].
- [OWNER] Ruling 17 Nov: face-led covers with Krish's cutout over about a third of the frame, never a small circle, on his own accounts; the Receipt (face-free) on the brand accounts; a Receipt vs face-led test on every long YouTube video.
- **Rule:** the face is a test variable on YouTube and the default on Krish's own accounts. When used, it is big (about a third of the frame), natural and plain (floor 101: no props), and the photo rotates (floor 126).

### 1.6 Gaze steers the eye

- [RESEARCH] In an eye-tracking study of web banners, a face looking towards the product and text raised attention to the text and product and improved memory for the brand and message; a face looking straight at the viewer held attention on the face itself [S23].
- **Rule (inference from banner research, test on YouTube):** on a cover whose job is the logo, document or headline, Krish looks at it. On a cover that asks the reader a direct question ("What will AI cost next?"), he looks at the camera.

### 1.7 Built-in contrast: two numbers, two years, two sides

- [REVEALED] Contrast devices recur in number-one outliers: "1970 / 2025" (Wendover, 3.15x), the 2006 vs 2026 rankings table (Tifo, 3.90x), "$2,500 vs $2,500,000" (Athletic Interest, 4.79x), "$324 vs $116" seat prices (Half as Interesting, 6.48x) [S11, S12, S3, S1]. Not sufficient alone: Colin and Samir's split thumbnail sat at the bottom.
- [OWNER] Krish already uses this: VS face-offs, verdict chips ("BLOCKED IT" vs "WELCOMED IT"), and the shape "the order of events, with the real number" ("Google paid $2.7B first.").

### 1.8 Bright headline, clean ground

- [OWNER] Cover standard point 6, from his own grid on 7 Nov: bright tiles with one big headline and one real thing beat the dark photo covers; no dark stock photo behind the headline.
- [SECONDARY] vidIQ found global "high contrast" styling slightly less common among the biggest breakouts (56% of all, 46% of the top 50) [S19]. (inference) The contrast that matters is the headline against its ground and the subject against its background; a saturated look across the whole frame adds little.
- **Rule:** headline in cream or ink at full contrast on ink or a flat v5 accent; the subject lit brightly enough to read at 130 px.

### 1.9 A fixed frame, a changing subject

- [REVEALED] The channels with the most stable results keep one frame and change only the subject: Cleo Abram (face left, 1 to 3 lime words), Wendover (mark top-left, block text bottom-left), Half as Interesting, Athletic Interest. On Instagram, Cleo Abram and Colin and Samir have the most consistent series look and the strongest long-run Reels [S13].
- [OWNER] Floor 46: instantly recognisable as makeyourmindup; floor 283: everything on a measured grid.
- **Rule:** three families, one grid, one wordmark position, one type system. Vary the subject, the family and the photo; never the frame.

### 1.10 Each surface gets its own drawing

- [OWNER] Floor 137: a separate cover for each real crop; floor 143: a wide and a vertical thumbnail with every pack.
- [HOUSE RULE, corpus] The Reel grid crop and the full-screen viewer are different deliverables; never force one design to do both.
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube replaces a 16:9 custom thumbnail on a vertical video with an auto-generated 4:5 one on home, explore and subscriptions [S15]. So a Short needs its own 9:16 cover.

## 4. The three families

### Shared rules (all families)

- **Parts.** The headline plus at most three parts: one picture, up to three logos (one logo row), the series label. The wordmark is mandatory (floor 65) and sits outside this count. [OWNER]
- **Type.** Anton capitals for the headline, one word may take an accent colour; DM Mono for chips, the series label and credits; Fraunces never on a cover headline. [OWNER, v5]
- **Wordmark.** V4 (one line) directly under the headline, sharing its left edge, on ink only. The D icon never sits beside a headline. [OWNER, v5]
- **Logos.** Real, unaltered, cropped from a real document, equal visible size (floors 287/288), below the headline and smaller than it. Never inside tacky boxes (floor 160).
- **No overlaps.** Nothing crosses the headline, the face or the wordmark (floors 69, 247, 254).
- **Words.** Krish's shapes only (`TITLES.md`); the live words are always his pick from two or three options (cover standard point 8).

### 4A. Headshot (face-led)

**When.** Default on Krish's own accounts (TikTok @therealkrisho, X @TheRealKrisho, LinkedIn, Threads, Substack Notes). On YouTube, one arm of the Receipt vs face-led test on every long video. Best for pieces where his take is the product: the 5 Whys explainers, opinion, how-tos he demonstrates. Never on @makeyourmind.up, the Facebook Page, the Substack cover or the share card. [OWNER]

**Composition, 16:9 (1280 x 720 design units; export 3840 x 2160).**

| Zone | Box (x, y in px at 1280 x 720) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Ink panel | x 0 to 700, full height | Headline and wordmark (the wordmark lives on ink only) |
| Accent block | x 700 to 1280, full height | One flat v5 accent with grain |
| Headline | x 56 to 660, y 64 to 470 | Anton, 1 to 2 lines, cap height at least 150 px for up to 4 words |
| Wordmark V4 | under the headline, left edge x 56 | About 28 px tall (about 4% of height) |
| Face | crown at about y 40, chin at about y 430, centred near x 990 | Cutout, head and shoulders; covers about a third of the frame |
| Logo row (optional) | x 56 to 660, y 540 to 640 | 1 or 2 logos with chips |
| Duration badge keep-out | x 1080 to 1280, y 630 to 720 | Shoulders only; no text, logo or face feature |

**What the face does.**
- A plain, approved headshot cutout, rotated across pieces (floors 101, 126); never a small circle; never covered (floor 254).
- One natural emotion that matches the claim: sceptical for a doubtful claim, amused for a dry-wit story, concerned for a money risk. No gurning (vidIQ: exaggerated faces on about 5% of breakouts [S19]).
- Gaze: towards the headline or logo when the cover's job is the named thing; to camera when the headline is a direct question to the reader (1.6).

**Words.** Up to 4 on YouTube beside the title; up to 7 on TikTok, X, LinkedIn and Threads covers where no title shows.

### 4B. Logo covers (the Receipt, verdict rows and VS face-offs)

**When.** Default on the brand accounts and the Substack cover and share card; one arm of every YouTube test. Best for stories about what named companies did: deals, lawsuits, launches, payouts, comparisons, and how-tos with named tools. [OWNER]

**Variants.**
1. **Receipt:** headline, one real document or screenshot with a thin glowing edge in a v5 accent, up to 3 logos. The document is the picture.
2. **Verdict rows:** headline, then up to 3 rows of [real logo] + [one chip of 1 to 2 words saying what it did]: PAYS, PAID, SUING; BLOCKED IT, WELCOMED IT. Chips are DM Mono, ink on an accent block, equal width. [OWNER, `TITLES.md` rule 8]
3. **VS face-off:** two product marks at identical visible size and identical badge treatment (floors 80, 282, 287), a small "VS" between, headline above. The marks are the products' own (floor 10).

**Composition, 16:9 (1280 x 720).**

| Zone | Box | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | x 56 to 700, y 56 to 400 | Anton, up to 4 words beside a YouTube title, 2 lines max |
| Wordmark V4 | under the headline, left edge x 56 | About 28 px |
| Logo or verdict rows | x 56 to 700, y 450 to 640 | Up to 3 rows, logos 48 to 56 px tall, equal visible size |
| Picture (document or screenshot) | x 720 to 1224, y 56 to 600 | Real, cropped, glowing edge, may tilt up to 3 degrees |
| Duration badge keep-out | x 1080 to 1280, y 630 to 720 | Empty ground |

**How many logos.** Only companies the headline or picture names, at most 3 (cover standard point 3). A VS face-off has exactly 2. A one-company story has 1, and the document carries the rest.

### 4C. Image covers (story scene or credited photo)

**When.** Hero 5 Whys pieces where the idea is abstract ("Why did pop songs get shorter?"), every carousel's first card (floors 163 and 179: a real-world image on the cover), and Reels covers on the brand account when no document tells the story. Reported events use a credited real photograph; generated scenes illustrate ideas only (IMAGERY rule 8). [OWNER]

**The scene.** One photoreal moment that acts out the headline with dry wit: a cat leaning into a radio mic, a session singer holding a crocodile puppet up to the microphone. One subject, large (at least 40% of the frame), brightly keyed even inside the house grade, one v5 accent in the scene. No text, logos or readable screens inside the generated image; real logos and screenshots are layered on top, unaltered (IMAGERY rules 2, 5). Fictional adults only; Krish appears only through his approved headshots (IMAGERY rule 3).

**Composition, 9:16 cover (1080 x 1920; export 2160 x 3840 for Shorts).**

| Zone | Box (px at 1080 x 1920) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Scene subject | x 90 to 990, y 300 to 1000 | The one moment, in the upper part |
| Headline | x 90 to 960, y 960 to 1300, on the ink burn | Anton, 2 to 3 lines, up to 7 words, cap height at least 110 px |
| Wordmark V4 | under the headline, same left edge | About 60 px tall |
| Logo row (optional) | x 90 to 960, y 1300 to 1380 | Up to 3 logos, only if named |
| Outside the cover box | y 0 to 300 and y 1380 to 1920 | Ground and scene only |

**Credits.** A real photo carries its credit in DM Mono at 18 to 22 px on 1080 width, inside the box, and its source is recorded (floors 207, 245). Each generated image's prompt, model, date, style and cost go on the piece's record (IMAGERY rule 9).

## 6. The title plus thumbnail pairing rule

1. **The title names; the thumbnail judges.** The title carries the searchable name and the full claim; the thumbnail carries the shortest version of the claim, the verdict or the question (`TITLES.md` rule 7; corpus 7).
2. **At most one shared word,** and only the name. "Why did pop songs get shorter?" with "30 SECONDS." shares none.
3. **The picture proves the title's noun.** If the title names Spotify, the thumbnail shows the Spotify mark or Spotify's own document.
4. **No pronoun stands in for a name** on either line ("They Faked AI Use" was rejected; `title_check.py` refuses it). [OWNER]
5. **The promise is paid in the first 10 seconds.** YouTube's test picks the winner on watch time [S14], and its clickbait enforcement removes videos that do not deliver [S16].

## 7. A/B testing routine (YouTube "Test and compare")

**What the tool does [OFFICIAL, S14].** Up to three titles, thumbnails or both; desktop YouTube Studio with advanced features; not Shorts, scheduled lives or Premieres before they end; the winner is chosen on **watch time, not click-through rate**; tests should finish within two weeks; outcomes are Winner, Performed same or Inconclusive (the first upload then stays). Changing a variant mid-test stops it. If any thumbnail is below 720p, all are downscaled to 480p.

**Our routine.**
1. **Every long video, at upload:** arm A = Receipt, arm B = face-led (ruling 17 Nov). Optional arm C = image family on the hero 5 Whys pieces. All three ship at full 3840 x 2160. [OWNER]
2. **One variable per test.** Same title for all arms during the four-week thumbnail test; title tests come afterwards (YouTube also tests titles; mixing both blurs the read). [HOUSE RULE]
3. **Make arms clearly different.** YouTube's own tip: similar variants prolong tests [S14].
4. **Never touch a running test.** Wait for its verdict or 14 days.
5. **Growth logs** per video: arms, outcome, watch-time share per arm, the video's overall impressions click-through rate from YouTube Analytics, topic, family, and whether a famous named entity was on the cover. Click-through rate is reported alongside because the tool decides on watch time. [OWNER: Growth reports click rate]
6. **After four weeks** Krish decides the YouTube default from the log. YouTube suggests testing older videos first to limit risk [S14]; his ruling to test every long video wins.
7. **Instagram and TikTok** have no native cover A/B, so their read is the grid mock (cover standard point 8) plus sends per reach and likes per reach, which Instagram names among its top ranking signals [S24].

## 8. Pre-publish checklist

Run on every cover before Krish sees it; failures go back to the designer.

**Words**
- [ ] Headline in one of his shapes, at most 7 words (at most 4 on a YouTube thumbnail).
- [ ] Literally true as written; any superlative verified or turned into a question.
- [ ] Names the real thing as people search it; no "they", "it" or "this" in place of a name; no insider shorthand; nothing that singles out a country.
- [ ] Thumbnail words differ from the title (at most one shared word, the name).
- [ ] Two or three options, each in a mock of his grid beside the last five posts.

**Parts**
- [ ] The headline is the largest thing; at most three parts beside it.
- [ ] Logos only for companies the headline or picture names; at most 3; the product's own mark; equal visible size; unaltered.
- [ ] No proof tags or badges; chips only in a verdict row.
- [ ] V4 wordmark present, under the headline, on ink; never the D beside a headline.
- [ ] Series label at most 4% of height, DM Mono.

**Picture**
- [ ] One dominant subject; scene acts out this headline; no generic AI imagery.
- [ ] Generated image: no text, logos or readable screens inside it; prompt and model recorded.
- [ ] Real photo: licence and credit recorded (public domain, CC0, Pexels or Unsplash).
- [ ] Face-led: approved cutout, about a third of the frame, never a small circle, not reused from the last cover, unobstructed; gaze matches the job.
- [ ] Bright subject, headline at full contrast; no dark stock photo behind the words.

**Geometry**
- [ ] Legible at 130 px and 168 px wide.
- [ ] YouTube: bottom-right badge corner empty.
- [ ] 9:16 covers: everything important inside x 90 to 990, y 300 to 1380; checked against the 3:4 grid crop and the account row with `channel_safe.py --shape cover_tall`.
- [ ] Carousel slide 1 at 1080 x 1350, text 80 px from the sides; slide 2 makes sense alone.
- [ ] Share card inside x 160 to 1040, y 48 to 582; words in the image.
- [ ] Nothing overlaps the headline, face or wordmark; all elements on the measured grid (alignment check passes).

**Publishing**
- [ ] Every channel's manifest entry carries `cover: {asset, sha256, cover_offset_ms}`.
- [ ] YouTube long video: test arms uploaded at 3840 x 2160; test started.
- [ ] Native preview checked in the app before approval (corpus 11).
