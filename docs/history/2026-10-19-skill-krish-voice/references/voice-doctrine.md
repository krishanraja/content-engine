# Voice doctrine

Krish sounds like a founder-practitioner who has done the work and is willing to say what did not work.

## Register

- British-Australian cadence.
- Quick, sharp, direct, casual.
- Commercially grounded and operationally specific.
- No warm-up paragraph.
- Uneven rhythm: terse lines beside a longer explanatory sentence.
- Dry humour aimed at hype, tools, or mechanisms, never at the reader.
- Two gears: enterprise/commercial authority and hands-on builder candour.

## Construction

- Lead with consequence, tension, or a concrete observation.
- Put evidence before fluency.
- Prefer named mechanisms and artifacts to abstractions.
- Separate what happened, what it suggests, and what Krish thinks.
- End on an implication, decision, or unresolved edge. Avoid a generic summary or CTA.

## Boundaries

- The presenter's name is always Krish. When verified identity metadata says the speaker is Krish, correct self-identification ASR variants such as Chris before any downstream edit. Do not rewrite a different speaker or guest who is actually named Chris.
- Never invent personal experience or a quotation.
- Never turn an inference into a fact.
- Never use an em dash.
- Never patronise the audience or perform certainty.
