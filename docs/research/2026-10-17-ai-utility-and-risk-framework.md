AI Utility & Risk
A practical assessment framework for medium-sized businesses
26 dimensions  •  2 independent scores  •  1 readiness assessment
Designed for organisations of approximately 50–1,000 employees evaluating AI models, chatbots, copilots, autonomous agents and AI platforms. Evaluate the technology, the use case and the actual deployment environment separately.
Executive perspective
AI can be highly capable without delivering meaningful commercial value. Equally, a valuable application can introduce unacceptable operational, legal or strategic exposure. Business usefulness and business danger should therefore be scored independently, with organisational readiness assessed as a third lens.
01  /  Business usefulness
Twelve dimensions of potential value, from individual output to enduring competitive advantage.
DIMENSION
 | WHAT IT MEASURES
 | 01  Productivity
 | Time saved, tasks completed, reduced manual effort and administrative burden.
 | 02  Quality
 | Accuracy, consistency, completeness, fewer mistakes and stronger deliverables.
 | 03  Revenue generation
 | New sales, conversion improvement, targeting, upsell and cross-sell.
 | 04  Cost efficiency
 | Operating cost reduction, lower unit costs and improved unit economics.
 | 05  Decision intelligence
 | Forecasting, analysis, recommendations, scenarios and decision support.
 | 06  Speed & responsiveness
 | Shorter cycle times, faster responses and accelerated time to market.
 | 07  Customer experience
 | Service quality, personalisation, availability, satisfaction and retention.
 | 08  Employee enablement
 | Knowledge access, onboarding, learning, creativity and skill development.
 | 09  Innovation
 | New products, services, business models, experimentation and prototyping.
 | 10  Scalability
 | Greater throughput and complexity without proportional headcount growth.
 | 11  Integration & adaptability
 | Fit with systems, workflows, data and evolving business requirements.
 | 12  Competitive advantage
 | Differentiation, defensibility and proprietary strategic capability.
 | 02  /  Business danger
Fourteen dimensions of potential harm, including direct incidents and longer-term erosion of business capability.
DIMENSION
 | WHAT IT MEASURES
 | 01  Inaccuracy
 | False answers, fabricated evidence, misleading conclusions and confident errors.
 | 02  Privacy & confidentiality
 | Exposure of personal data, customer information and commercial secrets.
 | 03  Cybersecurity
 | Prompt injection, credential misuse, unauthorised access and exfiltration.
 | 04  Legal & regulatory
 | Privacy, employment, consumer, sector-specific and AI-law violations.
 | 05  Financial exposure
 | Unexpected spend, fraud, erroneous transactions and financial losses.
 | 06  Operational disruption
 | Automation failure, downtime, process errors and business interruption.
 | 07  Reputational damage
 | Offensive outputs, public mistakes, customer distrust and brand harm.
 | 08  Bias & discrimination
 | Unfair treatment in hiring, pricing, service or employee decisions.
 | 09  Lack of transparency
 | Weak explainability, provenance, auditability and verification.
 | 10  Loss of control
 | Actions outside intended boundaries, excessive permissions and weak oversight.
 | 11  Vendor dependency
 | Lock-in, pricing shifts, model changes, provider failure and poor portability.
 | 12  Workforce degradation
 | Deskilling, overreliance, weaker critical thinking and knowledge loss.
 | 13  Intellectual property
 | Copyright, licensing, ownership uncertainty and proprietary IP leakage.
 | 14  Strategic misalignment
 | Low-value investment, distraction and erosion of differentiation.
 | 03  /  How to score
Score each proposed use case rather than assigning one universal rating to a vendor. Record the assumptions and supporting evidence behind every score.
Usefulness scorecard
For each of the 12 dimensions, score potential impact (0–5), evidence of achievable impact (0–5) and ease of realisation (0–5). Keep the three values visible rather than averaging away uncertainty. Where appropriate, weight dimensions according to business objectives and estimate annualised economic value.
Danger scorecard
For each of the 14 dimensions, score likelihood (0–5), severity (0–5), breadth of exposure (0–5) and strength of existing controls (0–5, where higher means stronger controls). Report inherent and residual risk separately. Apply hard-stop criteria for intolerable exposures, irrespective of aggregate score.
Organisational readiness
Assess data availability and quality, technical integration, ownership, governance, human oversight, change management, employee skills and leadership commitment. Low readiness reduces achievable value and often raises residual risk.
04  /  Decision matrix
DIMENSION
 | WHAT IT MEASURES
 | High usefulness / Low danger
 | Prioritise: pilot, measure outcomes, then scale.
 | High usefulness / High danger
 | Proceed selectively: strengthen controls, constrain permissions and require oversight.
 | Low usefulness / Low danger
 | Deprioritise unless strategically necessary or inexpensive to test.
 | Low usefulness / High danger
 | Avoid or redesign the use case.
 | 05  /  Illustrative use cases
DIMENSION
 | WHAT IT MEASURES
 | Internal knowledge search
 | Potentially high usefulness and lower danger when grounded in approved documents with role-based access and citations.
 | Marketing content drafting
 | Often useful with manageable risk if factual claims, copyright and brand approvals are reviewed.
 | Autonomous finance actions
 | Potentially high value but high danger when the agent can move money or change records; require transaction limits and approvals.
 | Unverified high-stakes advice
 | Risk can outweigh value where inaccurate recommendations have severe legal, financial or safety consequences.
 | Novelty chatbot
 | May be low-risk but also low-value without a clear measurable business outcome.
 | 06  /  Practical assessment sequence
01   Define the business outcome and baseline metric.
02   Describe the exact AI task, data inputs, users, tools and permissions.
03   Score all 12 usefulness dimensions and quantify likely value.
04   Score all 14 danger dimensions before and after controls.
05   Assess organisational readiness and implementation effort.
06   Run a constrained pilot with measurable success and stop criteria.
07   Reassess after deployment, model changes or expanded autonomy.
Final decision principle
Ask three questions: How valuable could this be? How dangerous could this be? How ready are we to use it? A defensible AI investment decision needs all three answers, backed by evidence and explicit trade-offs.
Reference frameworks: NIST AI Risk Management Framework (airc.nist.gov) and ISO/IEC 42001 (iso.org/standard/42001). This 26-dimension commercial taxonomy is an original assessment structure, not an official standard.
