Who picksyour AI?

Two years ago you chose which AI answered you. Now, more and more, a piece of software chooses for you. Here's how that happened, and three ways it could go.

The ice cream counter

Two years ago, picking an AI brain was like picking an ice cream flavour. Chocolate or vanilla. You chose.

Two years ago

Tap one.

Now it's like walking into an ice cream shop with forty flavours, three sizes, a "thinking" scoop that costs extra, and a guy behind the counter who sometimes just picks for you without asking.

Now · forty flavours

The guy behind the counterPicks for you

Example order: you asked for a medium Big Brain Fudge. The counter gave you Mini Brain Vanilla.

That guy behind the counter is the whole story. Here's how he got there, and where he's taking you next.

Then · 2024

What it used to be

Quick jargon check: OpenAI and Anthropic call their AIs "models". We'll call them brains. In September 2024, OpenAI gave people on its paid ChatGPT Plus and Team plans two new brains, o1-preview and o1-mini, and you picked them yourself from a menu called the model picker. They were built to spend more time thinking before they answered, like a kid who reads the whole maths question before scribbling. OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT, even said it was "working to" get ChatGPT to automatically choose the right model for you. It hadn't yet. You were the one holding the menu.

ChatGPT's model picker, September 2024
Model pickerSept 2024

Drawn from OpenAI's release notes, 12 September 2024: "Both o1-preview and o1-mini can be selected manually in the model picker."

Anthropic, which makes Claude, sold its brains the same way: three sizes, small, middle and big (Claude 3 Haiku, Sonnet and Opus), so you could pick your own balance of brains, speed and price. A bit like choosing your bike gear yourself instead of the bike doing it.

Your gear, your choice.

Anthropic, Introducing the next generation of Claude: "three state-of-the-art models in ascending order of capability: Claude 3 Haiku, Claude 3 Sonnet, and Claude 3 Opus."

Now · August 2025 to today

What it is now

7 Aug 2025

On 7 August 2025, OpenAI made GPT-5 the new default brain in ChatGPT and called it "a single auto-switching system". Translation: you stop picking. The app decides which brain answers each question: a quick one for easy stuff, or one that takes extra time to "think" for hard stuff.

8 Aug 202512 Aug 2025

It broke. The next day OpenAI's boss, Sam Altman, said the "autoswitcher was out of commission for a chunk of the day", and the result was that "GPT-5 seemed way dumber". People lobbied hard to get the old brain back, and by 12 August 2025 OpenAI had put GPT-4o back on the menu for paying users. The waiter had grabbed the wrong dish from the kitchen, and half the restaurant sent it back.

Way dumber

Sam Altman, quoted by TechCrunch, 8 August 2025. OpenAI's release notes, 12 August 2025: "4o is back in the model picker for all paid users by default."

3 Oct 2025March 2026

Then OpenAI said it would "soon begin" sending some sensitive chats, like ones showing "signs of acute distress" (someone in a really bad way), to a reasoning brain, the kind that stops and thinks first. On 3 October 2025 it said: "we've been using our real-time router to direct sensitive parts of conversations" that way. The router is the switcher. By March 2026, if you pay for ChatGPT and hit your limit on its thinking brain, GPT-5.4 Thinking, you get moved down to a smaller one, GPT-5.4 mini. And big companies can set ChatGPT's automatic mode to use that small brain by default.

Meanwhile at Anthropic

Over at Anthropic, the docs say Claude "decides for itself whether to think and how much", one question at a time. People building apps with Claude can turn its effort setting up to "max", or down to save time and money.

5 June 2026The money

Here's the part that actually matters: the money. On 5 June 2026, CNBC reported that letting software pick the brain, which the industry calls "model routing", fixes AI overspending, and is a problem for OpenAI and Anthropic. Scott Wu, who runs Cognition (it makes an AI coder called Devin), said that for a lot of boilerplate work, the dull same-again jobs, companies can get five to 10 times better cost efficiency (more work for the money) from brains that are still good enough for the task. His example: ask a model to name the third US president. Each one, no matter how expensive, will tell you it was Thomas Jefferson. Paying top price for that is like hiring a professor to tell you what day it is.

Yet Glean's boss, Arvind Jain, has estimated that roughly 95% of company AI use still runs on the most expensive brains, even for jobs cheaper ones could easily handle. Jeetu Patel, Cisco's chief product officer, did the sums: about $200 of tokens per worker per week comes to about $10,000 a year each. Tokens are little blocks of data, and the bill counts every one the AI gets through. With 90,000 employees, a company is looking at $900 million a year.

CNBC's own conclusion: "Pricing power is shifting from the companies selling premium AI toward the companies buying it." In plain English, the power to set prices is moving to the buyers.

22 Sept 2026

On 22 September 2026 both big AI companies launched cheaper brains. OpenAI added two GPT-6 brains, Sol and Luna, and cut the prices it charges app builders 50% compared with its special-offer prices for GPT-5.6. Anthropic said its newest Claude, Opus 5.5, "costs 40% less to run than Opus 5".

Today

The price list is now the length of a takeaway menu. OpenAI charges by the token. GPT-6 Luna, the brain OpenAI calls its "most efficient model", costs $0.50 for a million tokens of writing. GPT-6 Astra, its "most capable model", costs $50 for the same. Then you pick a lane: Fast mode costs double, and two cheaper lanes, called Batch and Flex, cost half.

Put it all together

A switcher picks your AI.

Put it all together and our answer to who picks your brain is, more and more, a switcher: software that, in TechCrunch's words, "decides which model to use for a particular prompt". In plain English, it decides which brain answers each question.

Next · our guess

Where it could go

From here on, this is our guess. For now you can still see behind the counter: in October 2025 OpenAI said ChatGPT "will continue to tell users which model is active when asked". The bigger shift would come if nobody tells you anything, and the brain you get depends on what's cheapest that day, for the free user, the student, the person asking for a recipe.

You would never know if you got the smart brain or the bargain-bin brain. You would just notice, some days, that the answer feels a bit dumber, the way people did in August 2025, except this time nobody would tell you why, or say sorry, or bring back the old one.

Next · three futures

Three ways this could go

Three ways this could go. Plain English, silly names, and the first sign you're living in each one.

Our bet

The Vending Machine

Every AI company makes the switching fully automatic and invisible, everywhere, always. You press a button, something comes out, you never see the machinery.

Winners
The AI companies, whose pitch would shift from "our brain is smarter" to "our machine never jams".
Losers
People who wanted to pick the smart brain on purpose and can't any more.
First sign
The menu of brains disappears from the app for everyone, paying customers included.

The Secret Menu

The switching stays automatic for everyone, but a hidden menu opens back up for people who pay extra or know to ask: picking your own brain becomes a paid extra.

Winners
Professionals who need a specific brain for a specific job.
Losers
Everyday users who never knew the secret menu existed.
First sign
A "choose your model" switch buried three menus deep, clearly built for people who go looking for it.

The Group Project

Several brains answer the same question at once, cheap and expensive, and something compares the answers before showing you one. It would be slower and pricier per question, but much harder to get a "way dumber" day.

Winners
Anyone doing something that really matters, like medical questions, legal questions, or code that runs a real business.
Losers
The AI companies' profits, because running three brains costs more than running one.
First sign
Your answer takes visibly longer to arrive than it used to, on purpose.

Our prediction · with a date

Our prediction

By 30 September 2027, at least two of OpenAI, Anthropic and Google will have their software pick the brain automatically, by default, for businesses that build apps on their AI.

In the jargon: automatic model routing becomes the default on their main API.
