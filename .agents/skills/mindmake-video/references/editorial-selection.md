# Editorial selection and transcript treatment

The output standard is a self-contained, high-value idea, not a filled publishing slot. Prefer no candidate and a precise rerecord brief to a coherent-looking but unimportant clip.

## Decide the argument before the edit

Identify the audience problem, the speaker's claim, the mechanism or evidence, the payoff, and the final implication. Score semantic coherence, impact, relevance, insight, specificity, audience value, opening strength, and ending strength independently. A technically clean excerpt still fails when it lacks a meaningful idea or would not reward the target audience.

Complete the semantic checklist before scoring: the clip must stand alone without the source, resolve every important referent, preserve claim boundaries and causality, include any visual dependency it needs, deliver a specific audience payoff, and end a complete thought. Every item must pass for `publishable`; record specific failure notes instead of compensating with a high aggregate score.

Use scores as anchored judgement, not decoration. Around `0.9` means unusually strong and directly evidenced; around `0.75` means clearly defensible but not exceptional; around `0.5` means generic, context-dependent, or merely adequate. Never round a weak dimension upward because the source is convenient or the weekly queue is empty.

The candidate must make sense to a viewer who has not seen the source. Do not rely on an unheard question, an unidentified “it/they/that”, a missing visual, or context supplied only by the editor. Preserve negation, uncertainty, exceptions, timeframes, attribution, causal limits, number qualifiers, and any wording that changes the claim.

## Choose the cut structure

Default to one continuous cut. It may be the strongest 20 seconds inside a much longer recording. Identify and record the strongest continuous baseline before considering a stitched version. Use a stitched cut only when every section is source-grounded, remains in source order unless a separately justified narrative order is truthful, the complete sequence forms one stronger semantic whole, and the rationale explains why it beats that continuous baseline.

A stitched cut must not create false causality, conceal a change of subject or tense, combine incompatible answers, turn a conditional claim into certainty, or make room tone and jump cuts distract from the idea. Avoid microcutting merely to increase pace. Record why the stitched version is better than the strongest continuous alternative. Record whether source order is preserved; if it is changed, explain precisely why the new order remains truthful and coherent.

For a final longer than 30 seconds, explicitly decide whether a source-grounded cold open of roughly five seconds improves the idea. It is optional, must not duplicate footage later in the cut, and must still make sense before context. Record why it was used or rejected.

The ending must land on a consequence, decision, useful insight, memorable proof, or genuine unresolved edge. A trailing clause, generic summary, polite sign-off, or incomplete thought fails even if the opening is strong.

## Treat captions without rewriting speech

The displayed caption script is a deletion-only edit of verified transcript tokens in their original order. Remove verbal false starts, filler, accidental duplicates, and redundant setup where the remaining words still form natural, faithful sentences. Punctuation, capitalisation, and verified identity or transcription corrections may change; no synonym, reordered phrase, grammatical bridge, or invented word may be introduced. Deletion must not erase negation, uncertainty, conditions, contrasts, quantities, or claim qualifiers. If a meaning-critical token disappears because its entire clause is intentionally omitted, record that token and explain why the retained claim is unchanged.

Intentional repetition may remain only when it adds rhythm, emphasis, contrast, or meaning, with a recorded rationale. If deleting filler makes the sentence ungrammatical or changes the speaker's meaning, keep the spoken wording or reject the excerpt. Do not create captions that substantially diverge from audible speech merely because the text reads better.

When identity metadata establishes Krish as the presenter, correct a self-introduction transcribed as Chris to Krish before selection. Never apply that correction blindly to a guest actually named Chris. Any real guest or subject named Chris must be declared in the candidate's identity mentions with source evidence, removing the ambiguity without weakening the Krish identity rule. Proper nouns, product names, numbers, and consequential claims still need human verification.

## Reject and direct a rerecord

Return `rerecord` rather than forcing an edit when the source lacks a complete argument, strong payoff, credible proof, usable ending, clean identity or claim verification, or audience relevance. Give Krish a specific brief containing:

- an exact hook direction;
- the missing proof, mechanism, example, or qualifier;
- the proposed beat structure and target duration;
- delivery advice, including where to pause or accelerate;
- the intended final line or ending function.

Also reject or quarantine excerpts with unsupported claims, confidentiality or rights risk, off-series framing, misleading generated evidence, unusable audio, missing visual proof that the words depend on, or a source question whose absence changes the answer. No volume of caption or pacing treatment makes those publishable.
