# Candidate station

## Responsibility
Choose and author the strongest self-contained editorial unit. Own the exact argument and edit plan, not evidence approval or visual styling.

## Inputs
Use the verified transcript for extract and solo work, or the exact approved script for short-native work, plus active scoped editorial rules.

## Outputs
Produce a candidate with hook, payoff, continuous baseline, optional stitched sequence, ending, assessment, risks and challenge packet.

## Quality gates
Hard-block broken meaning, unresolved references, weak audience value, misleading cuts and incomplete endings. Soft-block low impact, relevance, insight, specificity, novelty or hook strength.

The opening carries its own contract. An extracted or solo opening is hard-blocked when its first sentence names nothing from the approved brief title: the viewer clicked on that promise and the first thing they hear has to confirm it. A short-native opening is only reported, because its packaging is derived from the hook and a drift there means the script and the brief have parted company rather than the packaging lying. Two further reports are for a human, not a machine verdict: an opening that never states the narrator's relation to the claim, and a story past thirty seconds that never tells the viewer where they are.

## Failure and fallback
Reject or ask Krish to rerecord with specific direction. Content for content's sake is not an output.

## Handoff
Claims review receives the exact candidate hash and edit plan. No discovery window can masquerade as an authored candidate.

## Learning boundary
Use confirmed scoped preferences. Keep taste evidence separate from platform performance and from editorial truth gates.

## Change control
Selection criteria changes need counterexamples, stitch and continuous-cut fixtures, and a version bump.
