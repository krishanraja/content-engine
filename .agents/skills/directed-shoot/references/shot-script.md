# The shot script

The shot script is what Krish reads before he films, and what he keeps beside him while he films. It is written like
a film script: what he says, where he stands, what he does with his hands and body, what the camera does, and what
the edit will turn it into. He should be able to film it alone, from this page, with a phone on a tripod or in his
hand.

## Its parts

**Header**
- The point, in one sentence: what the effect proves for the viewer.
- The format (for example `reveal-peel`).
- The runtime target.
- The location, and when to shoot there (time of day, light).
- The props list: each real object, what it will become, and why its shape fits.
- What to wear.
- The camera: phone, 9:16, 4K at 30 or 60 fps, held or on a tripod, at what height, and how far away.

**Beats.** A numbered table. Each row has:

| Column | What it holds |
|---|---|
| # | Beat number |
| Time | Start to end, in seconds |
| Line | His exact words. Short, said aloud and written to make-piece's script rules |
| Blocking | Where he is, where he moves, and what his hands do, in plain verbs: "walk to the bench", "pick up the bag", "hold it to your chest", "crouch beside it" |
| Camera | Locked, slow push, or follow. The framing: full body, waist up or close |
| Becomes | What the edit shows in its place, on which word, and the trigger in the take (`wild.md`, "The craft") |
| Watch for | The one thing that ruins the take: a hand hiding the prop, walking out of frame, a fast turn |

**Takes**
- How many full takes to film: three, at least.
- The one he should not stop for (keep going through a stumble; the cut picks the best take whole).

## Rules for footage a model will transform

- **One continuous take.** There are no cuts in the original, ever. The reveal needs the raw panel to be the whole truth.
- **Props shaped like what they become.** The silhouette, size and pose must fit: a bag for a cat, a low box for a car
  bonnet, a seat with handlebars for a motorbike. Never ask the model to invent a movement his body did not make.
- **Slow, clear movement.** Walk at half speed, hold each pose for a beat before the line that changes it, and keep
  his hands and the prop in frame and in light.
- **Simple, steady ground.** Few people, no moving cars in the background, no strong flicker from lights, no mirrors.
- **His face open to the camera** on every line that matters, so the render keeps it.
- **The audio is the real audio.** He speaks the lines in the take itself. A voiceover added later breaks the sync between the panels.

## A worked example, for makeyourmindup (illustrative, never filmed)

Point: "AI can now make a thirty-second video of your life that never happened, and the only proof left is the raw
footage."

| # | Time | Line | Blocking | Camera | Becomes | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 to 4 | "Everything you're about to see is fake, and I'll prove it." | Walk towards camera along the hallway, slow | Locked, full body, chest height | A glass office corridor | Walking past the frame edge |
| 2 | 4 to 8 | "Here's my team. Actually, it's my houseplants." | Stop, gesture left at the plants on the shelf | Locked | Three colleagues at desks, peeled back to the plants on "houseplants" | His arm hiding the plants |
| 3 | 8 to 13 | "And this is my studio. Actually, it's the spare room." | Turn, sit on the stool | Locked | A lit podcast studio, peeled back on "spare room" | Sitting too fast |
| 4 | 13 to 18 | "Morning, by the way. Actually, it's eleven at night." | Sit, look straight to camera | Locked | Morning sun in the window, peeled back to night on "eleven at night" | A lamp flickering |
| 5 | 18 to 24 | "So when you see a video now, ask one question: where's the raw footage?" | Stay seated, lean in on "one question" | Slow push to waist up | Nothing changes. Both panels match | Looking away from the lens |

A generated colleague is a picture of a person who does not exist, so beat 2 needs his yes before it is rendered, and
it is always undone on screen.
