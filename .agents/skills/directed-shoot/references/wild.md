# Wild: anything goes

Ruling (Krish, 2026-11-18): "I want this experiment skill to literally be capable of going nuts - putting me on space,
having me turn in to sam altman randomly, absolutely nuts stuff that has ZERO limits. make it so"

So in this route the idea has no ceiling. Space, other planets, the bottom of the sea, the inside of a server rack. Him
the size of an ant or of a building. Him turning into a robot, a cartoon, a statue, a crowd of copies, or a famous
person. Gravity off, time backwards, the room folding up. Nothing is too much. The only questions are whether it is
funny or striking, and whether it makes the piece's point land.

The few lines that stay are in SKILL.md, "What stays". They are about spend, the law and his channel's name, never
about how far an idea can go.

## The craft: every transformation needs a trigger in the take

A model does its best work when the take gives it a clear moment to change on and a body that already moves the right
way. So every wild beat in the shot script names three things:
- the **trigger**: the action in the take where the change happens;
- the **body**: what he does so that the transformed version looks right;
- the **ground**: what the model paints around him.

| Transformation | Trigger in the take | His body | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launched into space | Looks up, bends his knees, jumps | Arms out and slow in the air; on landing, floats his arms as if weightless | Shoot against open sky or a plain wall. Low angle, phone tilted up |
| Becomes someone else (a famous person, a robot, a statue) | Turns away from camera and turns back, or wipes a hand down across his face | Holds the new person's pose: a known stance, a walk, a gesture | Real people: see SKILL.md, "What stays". The change happens on camera |
| Shrinks to ant size, or grows to building size | Crouches low or stands on tiptoe, and the camera drops or rises with him | Looks around in wonder at the "huge" or "tiny" world | Keep the props on the floor or table that will become giant or tiny |
| A crowd of clones | Walks across the frame several times in one locked take, in different spots | A different action each pass | Locked tripod, same light. The passes are layered in post |
| Gravity off, room spins | Grips a door frame, then lets go | Leans, sways, pushes off the wall in slow motion | A slow handheld roll of the phone helps the model |
| Time runs backwards | Throws something and catches it | Moves smoothly, then freezes on the line | Reverse in post. He speaks the line forwards, separately, in sync |
| The world swaps around him (office to jungle to Mars) | Claps, or snaps his fingers | Stays still on the snap, then reacts | One continuous take with snaps every few seconds |
| Becomes a cartoon or another medium | Puts on glasses, or steps through a doorway | Slightly exaggerated moves read better once rendered | Doorways are perfect cut points |
| Talks to a copy of himself | Two takes in one locked frame, left and right | Looks at the right spot, and leaves pauses for the other side | His eyeline is the thing to get right |

## Rules for wild footage

- **One continuous take where you can.** The fewer cuts in the raw footage, the more the transformation feels like
  magic and the less like editing.
- **Plain, steady ground** wherever the world will be replaced: open sky, a plain wall, an empty floor. Moving
  people and cars in the background confuse the model.
- **Hold the pose a beat after each trigger.** The model needs a few clean frames to land the change.
- **His voice is real and in sync,** whatever he turns into. A voice change (a robot, or the famous person) is an
  effect in post laid on his own words. Nobody else's voice is ever cloned.
- **Shoot spare takes.** Wild renders fail more often. Three takes of every trigger, at least.

## Formats that use it

- `reveal-peel` (`reveal-peel.md`): the lies are undone one by one beside the raw take.
- **Escalation:** each beat gets more absurd than the one before (desk, then office tower, then Mars, then the sun),
  landing on the point.
- **Interruption:** a straight talking-head piece in which, at random, he becomes something else for one line and then
  carries on as if nothing happened. Krish's own example: turning into Sam Altman at random.
- **Two of me:** he argues both sides of the piece's question with his clone.

New formats are welcome. Each one gets a reference file here, written from a reference he sends, read in full.
