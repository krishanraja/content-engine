# The titles round

Reference for the `make-piece` skill, read at the Titles row of its steps table (`.agents/skills/make-piece/SKILL.md`). Moved out of the core unchanged on 29 October 2026 so a session starts lean (`docs/COST_GUARDS.md`, G14); the house rules it carries are named in the core.

From 9 November the round is the Titles press on the day's tab on Sunday evening (Krish, 2026-10-28: "Sunday evening press"): three options per surface, best first, each through the cold read below and `copy_lint.py --applies-to titles`; a surface he has not picked by 21:30 takes its top-ranked option for the Sunday-night run (`produce-video`), said as that on the Monday board and never recorded as his pick. Only once a take of the piece is on the record (house rule TITLES_AFTER_RECORDING; Krish, 2026-10-17: "only after recording do I really feel like I've got a message. Usually I change or understand a fair bit more after reading a script out loud than I do creating a script."). First line the take up with its script: `python3 scripts/post-pack/narration_diff.py <script> <take words.json> --md ... --json ...` (what he actually said), then `narration_diff.py ledger`. Then start from the story (STORY_FIRST): `title_check.py lessons --surface <surface> --plan docs/plans/<plan>` prints it first (Krish's own statement in the plan's `story.md` when he has given one, else the outline's spine and answer, and the series' question), and every line is judged by whether a reader would come away with that story, not only whether it is legible (Krish, 2026-10-17: "The story is about there being so many recent AI model releases ... It's a "mind the gap" piece remember. Why do I have to remind you about what this story is about?"). Then put every packaging line to him in one round, by surface, each saying what that surface is and where it shows (`python3 scripts/post-pack/title_check.py lessons --surface <surface>` prints the surface's job, his own playbook's formulas, then the hooks research and his past verdicts): the Substack headline and its cover words, the YouTube title and its thumbnail words, the Shorts title, the video's title card. That round is his one sign-off sheet and goes first, before any render, capture or drawing that waits on it: every decision that is his for the piece (the lines, the headings and dividers, the intro stand-off, any script or speed choice for a cut still to record), so that once he answers the session finishes to publish-ready without him, except a send, publish, spend or delete (SIGN_OFF_FIRST; Krish, 2026-10-17: "do you not want to get sign off on headings and titles first so you can punch through to 100% without me?"). Never queue it behind a slow machine step. Every picture heading and chapter divider is written like a real media channel's (CHANNEL_HEADINGS): one device across the episode (numbered chapters or levels, one shape and length), each heading a short claim or promise that names the real thing, with a small kicker giving the source and date, built on the point he makes at that moment in the take and his best phrases; never his words pasted from the transcript (Krish, 2026-10-17: "I do worry that using my own words for headings and chapter names will lose some of what makes this feel like a real media channel") and never a line written to pass the checker (Krish, 2026-10-17: "These headings are so stupid it's insane"; the lesson is in config/title-verdicts.json). `title_check.py "option" --surface <surface> --source <body> --source <script>` is a floor, never the writer. Then the cold read, before anything reaches him: a fresh subagent with no context, told it is a senior leader scrolling past on LinkedIn, YouTube or Substack, is shown each line alone and asked what it is about, who it names and whether it reads as cheap or confusing; any line it cannot explain in one go, or calls cheap, is dropped, never offered (Krish, 2026-10-17, of "YOU'RE ON LOW", "OLD BEAT NEW" and "WHO PICKED LOW?": "This isn't a well-known concept and it's not a well-known media business ... What the hell does that even mean?"). Above every formula: a stranger understands it in one second and it names a real thing (the first lesson in config/title-verdicts.json). Record every pick and every rejection with what it teaches and his own words; a packaging pick is refused while the piece has no take on the record. Krish, 2026-10-14: "Stop fucking generating assets ... when I haven't actually approved your new approach ... figure out why I dislike it, and iterate, and understand what I actually like, and then encode that into the system forever"

Record: `title_check.py verdict "Title" --picked|--rejected --why "..." --krish "his words" --surface <surface> --piece <uuid>`
